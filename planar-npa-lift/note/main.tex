\twocolumn[{\centering
{\Large\bfseries Infinite NPA depth, finite semidefinite lift\par}
\vspace{5pt}
{\normalsize An exact quantum region in the simplest Bell experiment\par}
\vspace{13pt}}]

The quantum region of the CHSH value and the sum of two marginal averages requires infinite Navascués–Pironio–Acín depth, yet admits an exact finite semidefinite lift. We construct this lift by showing that every supporting bound is attained with equal measurement overlaps: one angle determines the entire boundary. Finite NPA certificates must remain valid for two independently varying angles, where the obstruction to finite depth survives. The same region has the smallest semidefinite extension degree possible for a nonpolyhedral set, separating infinite hierarchy depth from minimal semidefinite complexity.

Alice and Bob choose between two measurements with outcomes $\pm1$.
Write $a_x=\langle A_x\rangle$, $b_y=\langle B_y\rangle$ and
$E_{xy}=\langle A_xB_y\rangle$. Retain
\begin{equation}
 M=a_0+b_0,\qquad S=E_{00}+E_{01}+E_{10}-E_{11}.
 \label{eq:coordinates}
\end{equation}
Their quantum region $\W=\{(M,S):p\in\Q_{222}\}$ includes all quantum
behaviors, with no equality imposed on the marginal averages.
For its level-$L$ NPA outer approximation $\W_L$, the preceding
Letter~\cite{prior} and the construction below give
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\begin{gathered}
 \W\subsetneq\W_L\quad\text{for every finite }L,\\
 \sxdeg(\W)=2.
 \end{gathered}}
 \label{eq:result}
\end{equation}
We construct one lift with positive semidefinite (PSD) blocks of orders
five and four. Extension degree is the smallest possible largest block
order among all finite lifts; its value two follows separately from a
planar representation theorem.

\emph{Why one angle suffices.}
To determine $\W$, consider an arbitrary supporting inequality
$hS+\alpha M\leq\lambda$, with real $h,\alpha$.
Jordan's lemma reduces its validity to positivity of two-qubit slacks
$\lambda I-H$, where
\[
\begin{gathered}
 H=h\,\mathrm{CHSH}+\alpha(ZI+IZ),\\
 A_1=cZ+\sqrt{1-c^2}X,\quad B_1=dZ+\sqrt{1-d^2}X.
\end{gathered}
\]
Here $A_0=B_0=Z$ and $c,d\in[-1,1]$ are the cosines of the local
measurement angles. Deterministic strategies attain
$\ell=2|h|+2|\alpha|$, so $\lambda\geq\ell$ is necessary.
Above this bound, the first three elementary symmetric functions of
the eigenvalues of $\lambda I-H$ are nonnegative. Its determinant
therefore decides positivity: if it too is nonnegative, the characteristic
polynomial of $H-\lambda I$ has nonnegative coefficients and hence
no positive root.

Put $x=(1-c)/2$, $y=(1-d)/2$, and write
$D(x,y)=\det(\lambda I-H)$. The determinant takes the form
\begin{equation}
 D(x,y)=F(xy)+64h^2(4h^2-\alpha^2)xy(x+y),
 \label{eq:key}
\end{equation}
where $F$ depends on the product alone.
In the nonclassical range $|\alpha|\leq|h|$, the coefficient of $x+y$
is nonnegative. At fixed product, $x+y\geq2\sqrt{xy}$, with equality
at $x=y=\sqrt{xy}$. Thus equal overlaps minimize the determinant;
positivity there implies positivity for every pair.
For $|\alpha|\geq|h|$, the quantum support is the deterministic value
$\ell$: average the two signed one-sided tilted-CHSH bounds and add
the remaining marginal slack. Equal overlaps attain this value too.
Hence they suffice for \emph{every} support direction.

At equal overlaps $c=d$, the singlet decouples. In the remaining
three-dimensional block, set $c=(1-t^2)/(1+t^2)$ and multiply the slack
by $(1+t^2)^2$. With
\[
 p_0=(1+t^2)^2,\qquad p_1=1-t^4,\qquad p_2=1+4t^2-t^4,
\]
the result is the quartic matrix
\begin{equation}
 K(t)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \lambda p_0-2hp_1&-2\alpha p_0&0\\
 -2\alpha p_0&\lambda p_0-2hp_2&-8ht^3\\
 0&-8ht^3&\lambda p_0+2hp_2
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:K}
\end{equation}
Its value at zero and leading coefficient force $\lambda\geq\ell$,
which also controls the singlet. The missing endpoint follows by
continuity. We have therefore proved
\begin{equation}
 \max_{\W}(hS+\alpha M)\leq\lambda
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad K(t)\succeq0\quad\forall t\in\mathbb R .
 \label{eq:support}
\end{equation}
All three support coefficients still enter \emph{affinely}. This is
what will give one lift for the whole region.

\emph{From one variable to one finite lift.}
A PSD real univariate matrix polynomial is a polynomial matrix sum
of squares, including on its singular boundary~\cite{hs}.
For a quartic, the factors have degree at most two.
For $\xi\in\mathbb R^3$, the parity of Eq.~\eqref{eq:K} splits the nine
quadratic features into
\[
\begin{aligned}
 Z_+&=(\xi_1,\xi_2,t\xi_3,t^2\xi_1,t^2\xi_2)^T,\\
 Z_-&=(\xi_3,t\xi_1,t\xi_2,t^2\xi_3)^T .
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently Eq.~\eqref{eq:support} is equivalent to the polynomial identity
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 \xi^TK(t)\xi=Z_+^TGZ_++Z_-^TJZ_-,\\
 G\succeq0,\quad J\succeq0,
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:gram}
\end{equation}
with $G$ of order five and $J$ of order four. Matching coefficients
gives sixteen affine equations.

Dualizing gives the membership lift
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 \W=\{&(m(z),s(z)):z\in\mathbb R^{16},\\
       &\ell(z)=1,\ U(z),V(z)\succeq0\}.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:membership}
\end{equation}
The matrices $U,V$ are the adjoints of the coefficient map, of orders
five and four; $\ell,m,s$ are its linear coordinate maps. Their rational
entries are given in the complete proofs.

This lift attains the boundary. The positive definite Gram pair
\[
 G_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,2,1,1),\quad
 J_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,2,2,1)
\]
represents $(\lambda,\alpha,h)=(1,0,0)$ and supplies strict feasibility.
The normalization is
$\operatorname{tr}(U(z)G_0)+\operatorname{tr}(V(z)J_0)=1$,
which bounds the PSD matrices and every auxiliary variable.
The normalized dual image is compact and has exactly the support
values in Eq.~\eqref{eq:support}. It therefore equals $\W$, including
its boundary.

\emph{Why the NPA obstruction remains.}
Equal overlaps determine support values; a finite NPA certificate
must represent the bound for independently chosen measurement angles.
The earlier nontermination proof~\cite{prior} uses this freedom:
near the local threshold, an NPA error bounded by a constant times
the quantum violation above the local bound would force a shifted
Motzkin polynomial to be a sum of polynomial squares.
The complete earlier argument is retained in the companion.

\emph{Minimal block order and thirty-two exact projections.}
For $f(\alpha)=\max_\W(S+\alpha M)$, the exact quantum bound has
the expansion~\cite{prior}
\begin{equation}
 f(1-T)=4-2T+\tfrac43T^3+O(T^4),\qquad T\downarrow0 .
 \label{eq:cubic}
\end{equation}
A polygon would have affine support sufficiently close to $\alpha=1$,
contrary to Eq.~\eqref{eq:cubic}. Thus $\W$ is nonpolyhedral.
It is also closed, convex and semialgebraic, so Scheiderer's planar
theorem gives $\sxdeg(\W)\leq2$~\cite{soc}.
Order-one lifts are polyhedral, hence $\sxdeg(\W)=2$.
The blocks $5,4$ are explicit; the optimal-order lift is existential.

Physical input and output relabellings give the same quantum region for
\[
 \left(a_x+\chi_{xy}b_y,\ \sum_{ij}\chi_{ij}E_{ij}\right),
 \qquad \prod_{ij}\chi_{ij}=-1.
\]
The eight CHSH sign patterns and four selected input pairs give
32 distinct planes. Intersecting their fixed lifts with any NPA
relaxation makes every support value on these planes exact, including
all the directions responsible for finite-level nontermination.
