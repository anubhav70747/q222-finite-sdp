\title{Infinite NPA depth at minimal semidefinite complexity}
\author{Anubhav Chaturvedi}
\affiliation{Division of Quantum Optics and Information, Institute of Theoretical Physics and Astrophysics, Faculty of Mathematics, Physics and Informatics, University of Gda\'nsk, 80-308 Gda\'nsk, Poland}
\date{27 October 2026}
\begin{abstract}
The quantum region of the CHSH value and the sum of two marginal averages
is not recovered by any finite level of the Navascu\'es--Pironio--Ac\'in
hierarchy. We give an exact finite semidefinite lift of this same region.
The proof reduces every supporting inequality to equal measurement
overlaps. At fixed product of the overlap parameters, the determinant
is affine in their sum. In the nonclassical range the coefficient is
nonnegative, so equal overlaps minimize it; the remaining bounds are
local. The resulting univariate quartic
matrix depends affinely on the supporting inequality and gives an
explicit lift with blocks of orders five and four. The region has
semidefinite extension degree two, the smallest possible for a
nonpolyhedral set. Thus the family that requires unbounded NPA depth
has a finite representation of minimal semidefinite block order.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle

Alice and Bob each choose between two measurements with outcomes $\pm1$.
Their marginal averages are $a_x=\langle A_x\rangle$ and
$b_y=\langle B_y\rangle$, and their correlators are
$E_{xy}=\langle A_xB_y\rangle$. Retain two observed numbers:
\begin{equation}
 M=a_0+b_0,\qquad S=E_{00}+E_{01}+E_{10}-E_{11}.
 \label{eq:observed}
\end{equation}
The second is the CHSH value~\cite{CHSH}. Let $\W$ be the set of
pairs $(M,S)$ obtainable in quantum theory. All quantum correlations
are allowed, including those with unequal marginal averages.

In our preceding Letter~\cite{prior}, we showed that no fixed level
of the Navascu\'es--Pironio--Ac\'in (NPA) hierarchy~\cite{NPA2007,NPA2008}
recovers this region. For every finite level, symmetric doubly tilted
inequalities $S+\alpha M\leq\omega_{\rm Q}(\alpha)$ are overestimated
sufficiently close to the local threshold $\alpha=1$.
We now construct one exact finite semidefinite representation of
the entire region $\W$.

\begin{theorem}
The compact convex quantum region $\W$ has an affine semidefinite
lift with blocks of orders five and four and sixteen auxiliary variables.
If $\W_L$ is its level-$L$ NPA outer approximation, then
\begin{equation}
 \W\subsetneq\W_L\quad\text{for every finite }L,
 \qquad \sxdeg(\W)=2 .
 \label{eq:theorem}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
A semidefinite lift describes a set by affine constraints and positive
semidefinite (PSD) matrices in its observed and auxiliary coordinates.
The extension degree $\sxdeg$ is the smallest possible largest
block order among such finite lifts. We construct the explicit
$5,4$ lift below; the optimal order two follows from a planar
representation theorem.

\emph{Two measurement angles, one determinant.}---A compact convex
region is the intersection of its supporting half-planes.
It therefore suffices to decide, for every real $h,\alpha,\lambda$,
whether
\begin{equation}
 hS+\alpha M\leq\lambda\qquad\text{throughout }\W .
 \label{eq:supportquestion}
\end{equation}
For fixed measurements this is equivalent to positivity of
$\lambda I-H$, where $H=h\,\mathrm{CHSH}+\alpha(A_0+B_0)$:
its expectation in any state is the slack in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supportquestion}. Jordan's lemma reduces the
measurements to two-qubit blocks. Choosing local axes gives
$A_0=B_0=Z$ and
\begin{equation}
 A_1=cZ+\sqrt{1-c^2}X,\quad
 B_1=dZ+\sqrt{1-d^2}X .
 \label{eq:angles}
\end{equation}
Here $c,d\in[-1,1]$ are the cosines of the two local measurement
angles. Deterministic strategies attain
$\ell=2|h|+2|\alpha|$, so $\lambda\geq\ell$ is necessary.

Above this local bound, the first three elementary symmetric
functions of the eigenvalues of $\lambda I-H$ are nonnegative.
Thus its determinant decides positivity~\cite{SM}. Indeed, if the
determinant is also nonnegative, $\det(rI+\lambda I-H)$ has
nonnegative coefficients and no positive root; a negative eigenvalue
would give such a root.

Put $x=(1-c)/2$ and $y=(1-d)/2$. The determinant has the form
\begin{equation}
 D(x,y)=F(xy)+64h^2(4h^2-\alpha^2)xy(x+y),
 \label{eq:det}
\end{equation}
where $F$ is a polynomial in the product alone.
For $|\alpha|\leq|h|$, the coefficient of $x+y$ is nonnegative.
At fixed product, $x+y\geq2\sqrt{xy}$, with equality at
$x=y=\sqrt{xy}$. Hence
\[
 D(x,y)\geq D(\sqrt{xy},\sqrt{xy}).
\]
Positivity at equal overlaps therefore implies positivity at
every pair of overlaps. This elementary minimization removes one
measurement angle.

The other directions, $|\alpha|\geq|h|$, have quantum support
$\ell$. The one-sided bounds
$\pm\mathrm{CHSH}+2\tau A_0\leq4I$ and
$\pm\mathrm{CHSH}+2\tau B_0\leq4I$, $\tau=\pm1$, follow from the
critical tilted-CHSH sum of squares~\cite{SM}.
Average the two with the required signs, multiply by $|h|$ and
add the marginal slack of weight $|\alpha|-|h|$.
This proves $H\leq\ell I$, attained by equal-overlap deterministic
strategies. Equal overlaps thus decide \emph{all} supporting lines.

\emph{One angle, one finite lift.}---At $c=d$, the singlet decouples
with eigenvalue $-2hc$. On the remaining three Bell states, set
$c=(1-t^2)/(1+t^2)$ and multiply the slack by $(1+t^2)^2$.
With
\[
 p_0=(1+t^2)^2,\qquad p_1=1-t^4,\qquad p_2=1+4t^2-t^4,
\]
the resulting quartic matrix is
\begin{equation}
 K(t)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \lambda p_0-2hp_1&-2\alpha p_0&0\\
 -2\alpha p_0&\lambda p_0-2hp_2&-8ht^3\\
 0&-8ht^3&\lambda p_0+2hp_2
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:K}
\end{equation}
Its value at zero and its leading coefficient force
$\lambda\geq\ell$, which also controls the singlet.
The endpoint $c=-1$ follows by continuity, and the parity
$K(-t)=RK(t)R$, $R=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1)$,
allows all real $t$. Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \max_{\W}(hS+\alpha M)\leq\lambda
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 K(t)\succeq0\quad\forall t\in\mathbb R .
 \label{eq:equiv}
\end{equation}

A PSD real univariate matrix polynomial is a polynomial matrix
sum of squares, including when singular~\cite{HanselkaSinn}.
For a quartic, every factor has degree at most two, since leading
real squares cannot cancel. For $\xi\in\mathbb R^3$, the parity of $K$
splits the quadratic features into
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 Z_+&=(\xi_1,\xi_2,t\xi_3,t^2\xi_1,t^2\xi_2)^T,\\
 Z_-&=(\xi_3,t\xi_1,t\xi_2,t^2\xi_3)^T .
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:features}
\end{equation}
Thus Eq.~\eqref{eq:equiv} is equivalent to the polynomial identity
\begin{equation}
 \xi^TK(t)\xi=Z_+^TGZ_++Z_-^TJZ_-,
 \qquad G\succeq0,\quad J\succeq0 .
 \label{eq:sos}
\end{equation}
The blocks have orders five and four. Matching coefficients
eliminates $t$ and gives sixteen affine equations.

Since $K$ is affine in \emph{all} support coefficients, these equations
describe the whole cone of valid inequalities. Semidefinite duality
therefore gives a single membership lift:
\begin{equation}
 \W=\left\{(M,S):
 \begin{array}{l}
 \exists z\in\mathbb R^{16},\ U(z),V(z)\succeq0,\\
 \ell(z)=1,\ m(z)=M,\ s(z)=S
 \end{array}\right\}.
 \label{eq:membership}
\end{equation}
Here $U,V$ are the adjoints of the coefficient map, and $\ell,m,s$
are the corresponding linear coordinate maps; all their rational
entries are given in the Supplemental Material~\cite{SM}.

The lift also attains the boundary. The Gram pair
\[
 G_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,2,1,1),\qquad
 J_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,2,2,1)
\]
is positive definite and represents $(\lambda,\alpha,h)=(1,0,0)$.
It supplies strict feasibility. Moreover, the normalization is
$\operatorname{tr}(U(z)G_0)+\operatorname{tr}(V(z)J_0)=1$,
which bounds both PSD matrices and every auxiliary variable.
The normalized dual image is compact, and its supporting half-planes
are exactly Eq.~\eqref{eq:equiv}. This proves equality in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:membership}, including boundary points.

\emph{What the NPA obstruction measures.}---Equal overlaps determine
the support values, whereas a finite NPA certificate must represent
the bound for independently chosen measurement angles.
The preceding proof of nontermination uses precisely that
two-variable freedom: near the local threshold, an NPA error bounded
by a constant times the quantum violation above the local bound
would force a shifted Motzkin polynomial to be a sum of
polynomial squares~\cite{prior}. The univariate squares in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sos} arise \emph{after} the support minimization and
need not be identities in a fixed list of observable words.
The full earlier argument is included in the companion proofs.

The optimal block order follows from the same boundary.
For $f(\alpha)=\max_\W(S+\alpha M)$, the exact quantum
bound~\cite{GigenaEtAl2025} has the expansion~\cite{prior}
\begin{equation}
 f(1-T)=4-2T+\tfrac43T^3+O(T^4),\qquad T\downarrow0 .
 \label{eq:cubic}
\end{equation}
A polygon would have affine support for $\alpha$ sufficiently close
to one, contrary to Eq.~\eqref{eq:cubic}.
Thus $\W$ is nonpolyhedral. It is also closed, convex and semialgebraic
by Eq.~\eqref{eq:membership}; Scheiderer's planar
theorem~\cite{Scheiderer} gives $\sxdeg(\W)\leq2$.
Order-one lifts are polyhedral, so $\sxdeg(\W)=2$.
The optimal-order representation is existential; the blocks $5,4$
above are explicit.

Finally, physical relabellings give the same quantum image $\W$ for
\begin{equation}
 \pi_{\chi,xy}(p)=
 \left(a_x+\chi_{xy}b_y,\ \sum_{ij}\chi_{ij}E_{ij}\right).
 \label{eq:32}
\end{equation}
Here $\prod_{ij}\chi_{ij}=-1$. The eight CHSH sign patterns and four selected input pairs give
32 distinct planes. Intersecting any NPA relaxation with
$\pi_{\chi,xy}^{-1}(\W)$ for all of them retains every quantum
correlation and gives exact support on all 32 planes.
In particular, it resolves the entire family responsible for
finite-level nontermination.
