\begin{abstract}
This record gives an evaluated finite positive-semidefinite certificate for one
regular-C2 contact graph and an independent testing box. All coefficients are
rational, all square-root radicands have explicit sign certificates, and the
delivered coefficient maps contain every term of the original seven-source
parent. The extraction has 25 linear frames, 785 blocks in $\Splus^2$, 1,570
listed features including repetitions, and 237 ambient testing monomials.
There are 27,747 nonzero product records and 295 nonzero records in the seven
source target columns. These are local data. A finite global cover and a pencil
projecting onto the full eight-coordinate quantum set are not supplied here;
the global extraction items E1--E3 remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Complete parent and the precise domain}

The seven source coordinates, in their fixed order, are
\[
  (d,n,m,C,A,D,\alpha).
\]
Write $D_u=1+u^2$, $D_v=1+v^2$. The complete homogeneous quarter-parent is
\begin{align}
\widetilde P={}&nD_uD_vX^2
 +m(vW-X+uvY-uZ)^2\notag\\
 &+CD_v(uW-Y)^2+AD_uD_vY^2\notag\\
 &+(D+d/2)(uW+uvX-Y-vZ)^2\notag\\
 &+(\alpha+d/2)D_u(vY-Z)^2
 +(d/2)D_v(X+uZ)^2-(d/2)D_uD_vZ^2.
 \label{eq:parent}
\end{align}
Every term in \eqref{eq:parent} is retained. For the local certificate set
$d=Z=1$, let $c=(n,m,C,A,D,\alpha)$ and $z=(u,v,W,X,Y)$, and denote the resulting
polynomial by $P_c(z)$. The testing-variable order is fixed throughout.

The source/contact anchor is
\begin{equation}
 c_*=(7/8,9/8,1/18,11/8,9/8,1/18),\qquad
 \theta_*=(1,1,1,-1/10,1/10).
 \label{eq:anchor}
\end{equation}
For $\theta=(p,q,a,b,r)$ define the six contact equations
\begin{equation}
  F(c,\theta)=\bigl(P_c(\theta),\partial_uP_c(\theta),
  \partial_vP_c(\theta),\partial_WP_c(\theta),
  \partial_XP_c(\theta),\partial_YP_c(\theta)\bigr)=0.
  \label{eq:contact}
\end{equation}
These equations impose the complete value and gradient conditions. In the
original homogeneous state variables, the value equation and the three
displayed state derivatives also imply the missing $Z$ derivative by Euler's
identity for a homogeneous quadratic. No state-kernel condition is discarded.

Let the eleven centered source/contact variables and five independently
centered testing variables be
\begin{equation}
 s=(c-c_*,h),\qquad h=\theta-\theta_*,\qquad
 t=z-\theta_*.
 \label{eq:center}
\end{equation}
Their order is the six coordinates of $c$, followed by the five coordinates
of $h$; the order of $t$ is that of $z$. Set
\begin{equation}
 \rho=\frac{510101005}{350806159660744}.
 \label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
The source domain used in this record is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal S_\rho=\{(c,\theta):F(c,\theta)=0, |s_i|\leq\rho
 \ (1\leq i\leq11)\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal T_\rho=\{t:|t_i|\leq\rho\ (1\leq i\leq5)\}.
 \label{eq:domain}
\end{equation}
The source/contact point and the testing point vary independently. Stationarity
alone is not asserted to imply global physical validity of a source.

\begin{theorem}[Evaluated local certificate]\label{thm:main}
For each $(c,\theta)\in\mathcal S_\rho$ there are 785 positive semidefinite
matrices $Q_b(c,\theta)\in\Splus^2$. There are 785 fixed two-component testing
feature vectors $\psi_b(t)$, independent of $(c,\theta)$, such that
\begin{equation}
 P_c(\theta_*+t)=\sum_{b=0}^{784}\psi_b(t)^TQ_b(c,\theta)\psi_b(t)
 \qquad(t\in\mathcal T_\rho).
 \label{eq:main}
\end{equation}
All radicands, multipliers, and source Gram factors are explicitly evaluated in
the accompanying JSON files. The symmetric products of these features are
polynomials in the 237 listed monomials, of degree at most six. The complete
seven-source parent has the coefficient matching map stated in
Section~\ref{sec:compiler}.
\end{theorem}

\section{The contact graph is regular}

Substitution in \eqref{eq:contact} gives $F(c_*,\theta_*)=0$. The derivative
with respect to the six coordinates of $c$, at the anchor, is
\begin{equation}
 J_*=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1/25&1/25&81/50&1/25&1/25&81/50\\
 1/25&-9/25&18/5&1/25&-9/25&81/50\\
 1/25&11/25&81/50&1/25&11/25&-9/25\\
 0&2/5&18/5&0&-2/5&0\\
 -4/5&-2/5&0&0&-2/5&0\\
 0&2/5&-18/5&4/5&2/5&-18/5
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \det J_*=\frac{114048}{78125}\ne0.
 \label{eq:jacobian}
\end{equation}
Thus the contact equations define a five-dimensional graph near the anchor.
There is also a direct uniform regularity check on the declared contact box.
Since $F$ is affine in $c$, its source Jacobian $J(\theta)$ depends only on
$\theta$. Put $E(h)=J_*^{-1}J(\theta_*+h)-I_6$. Its entries are rational
polynomials with zero constant term. Direct exact coefficient collection gives
\begin{equation}
 \max_i\sum_j\sum_{\beta\ne0}|[h^\beta]E_{ij}(h)|
   =\frac{114199}{160}.
 \label{eq:jacnorm}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for $|h_i|\leq\rho\leq1$,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{E(h)}_\infty\leq
 \frac{11650604933999}{11225797109143808}<1.
 \label{eq:jacbound}
\end{equation}
If $(I+E)x=0$ and $x\ne0$, choosing a component of $x$ of maximal absolute
value contradicts \eqref{eq:jacbound}. Hence $J(\theta)$ is invertible on the
whole contact box. Equivalently, the source coordinates are the unique
rational solution of the six affine contact equations for each fixed contact
point in that box; the definition of $\mathcal S_\rho$ further retains the
source-coordinate bounds. No global property of that rational graph follows
from this local statement.

\section{Exact polynomial Taylor matrix}

Expand $P_c(\theta+\Delta)$ in $\Delta=(\Delta_1,\ldots,\Delta_5)$, retaining
polynomial coefficients in $(c,\theta)$. Remove the terms of $\Delta$ degree
zero and one. For each remaining monomial apply the following deterministic
allocation rule. If an exponent is at least two, use the first such coordinate
and divide out its square, adding the result to the corresponding diagonal
entry. Otherwise use the first two occupied coordinates and add half of the
divided monomial to each of the two symmetric off-diagonal entries. This
defines a symmetric polynomial matrix $H(c,\theta,\Delta)$ and proves the
exact identity
\begin{equation}
 P_c(\theta+\Delta)=P_c(\theta)+\nabla P_c(\theta)^T\Delta
                 +\Delta^TH(c,\theta,\Delta)\Delta.
 \label{eq:taylor}
\end{equation}
There is no asymptotic remainder in \eqref{eq:taylor}. All fifteen upper
entries, both before and after substituting $\Delta=t-h$, are supplied as
complete sparse rational polynomials.

At the anchor the matrix is half the Hessian:
\begin{equation}
 H_0=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 10201/3600&-1089/400&121/360&14/5&-253/90\\
 -1089/400&10201/3600&-9/40&-14/5&253/90\\
 121/360&-9/40&103/36&1/2&-11/18\\
 14/5&-14/5&1/2&29/4&-11/4\\
 -253/90&253/90&-11/18&-11/4&341/36
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:hessian}
\end{equation}
Its exact factorization $H_0=LD_0L^T$ is
\begin{equation}
 L=\begin{pmatrix}
 1&0&0&0&0\\
 -9801/10201&1&0&0&0\\
 1210/10201&8991/20002&1&0&0\\
 10080/10201&-5040/10001&79/1010&1&0\\
 -10120/10201&5060/10001&-119/1010&2/81&1
 \end{pmatrix},
 \label{eq:L}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 D_0=\operatorname{diag}\left(
 \frac{10201}{3600},\frac{20002}{91809},\frac{27775}{10001},
 \frac{891}{202},\frac{1067}{162}\right)>0.
 \label{eq:pivots}
\end{equation}
In particular $H_0$ is positive definite. Define the rational linear functions
$f=L^Tz$, and put $\widehat H=L^{-1}HL^{-T}$. This orientation is essential:
$\Delta f=L^T\Delta$ and
\begin{equation}
 \Delta^TH\Delta=(\Delta f)^T\widehat H(\Delta f).
 \label{eq:orientation}
\end{equation}

\section{Twenty-five fixed linear frames}

Set
\begin{equation}
 \eps=\frac1{20}\min_i(D_0)_{ii}=\frac{10001}{918090}.
 \label{eq:eps}
\end{equation}
Use the five frame functions $f_i$ and, for every pair $i<j$, the two frame
functions $f_i+f_j$ and $f_i-f_j$. Their respective coefficient polynomials
are
\begin{equation}
 \widehat H_{ii}-8\eps,\qquad
 \eps+\tfrac12\widehat H_{ij},\qquad
 \eps-\tfrac12\widehat H_{ij}.
 \label{eq:coefficients}
\end{equation}
Order the five individual functions first; then order the pairs
lexicographically, with the plus function preceding the minus function.
The exact frame identity is
\begin{align}
 \Delta^TH\Delta={}&
 \sum_i(\widehat H_{ii}-8\eps)(\Delta f_i)^2\notag\\
 &+\sum_{i<j}\left(\eps+\tfrac12\widehat H_{ij}\right)
                   (\Delta f_i+\Delta f_j)^2\notag\\
 &+\sum_{i<j}\left(\eps-\tfrac12\widehat H_{ij}\right)
                   (\Delta f_i-\Delta f_j)^2.
 \label{eq:frame}
\end{align}
Indeed each pair adds $2\eps$ to each of its two diagonal entries, and its
cross coefficient is $2\widehat H_{ij}$. Every coordinate participates in
four pairs, giving the subtraction $8\eps$.

After inserting $c=c_*+s_{1:6}$, $\theta=\theta_*+h$, and $\Delta=t-h$, denote
the 25 coefficient polynomials by $\gamma_k(s,t)$ and the 25 linear testing
functions by $\ell_k(t)$. Their constant terms satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_k(0,0)\geq\eps.
 \label{eq:constantpositive}
\end{equation}
For the ten plus and ten minus functions the constant is $\eps$; for the
five individual functions it is $(D_0)_{ii}-8\eps\geq12\eps$.

For a rational polynomial let its nonconstant coefficient norm mean the sum
of the absolute values of all its nonconstant coefficients, with every
source and testing monomial distinct. Exact coefficient collection gives
\begin{equation}
 B_*:=\max_k\sum_{(a,b)\ne(0,0)}|[s^at^b]\gamma_k|
   =\frac{43803942777143}{46827180450}.
 \label{eq:B}
\end{equation}
The radius in \eqref{eq:rho} is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \rho=\min\left(\frac12,\frac{\eps}{8(1+B_*)}\right).
 \label{eq:radiusformula}
\end{equation}
In particular $\rho B_*<\eps/8$. The radius is also smaller than half the
absolute value of each nonzero anchor coordinate in \eqref{eq:anchor}.

\section{Positive separation of the two variable groups}

Fix one frame and omit its index. Partition its complete coefficient support
uniquely as
\begin{equation}
 \gamma(s,t)=\gamma_0+G(s)+T(t)
              +\sum_{\beta\ne0}A_\beta(s)t^\beta,
 \label{eq:grouping}
\end{equation}
where $G,T$, and every $A_\beta$ have zero constant term. The sum ranges only
over the nonzero mixed groups. Define
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_\beta=\sum_a|[s^a]A_\beta|,\quad
 U_\beta(s)=A_\beta(s)+\sigma_\beta\rho,\quad
 V_\beta(t)=t^\beta+\rho^{|\beta|},\quad
 B=\sum_\beta\sigma_\beta\rho^{|\beta|+1}.
 \label{eq:shifts}
\end{equation}
The two pure remainders are the following explicit polynomials:
\begin{align}
 p(s)&=\gamma_0/2-B/2+G(s)
                -\sum_\beta\rho^{|\beta|}A_\beta(s),\notag\\
 q(t)&=\gamma_0/2-B/2+T(t)
                -\sum_\beta\sigma_\beta\rho\,t^\beta.
 \label{eq:remainders}
\end{align}
Expanding every product proves, coefficient by coefficient,
\begin{equation}
 \gamma(s,t)=p(s)+q(t)+\sum_\beta U_\beta(s)V_\beta(t).
 \label{eq:separation}
\end{equation}
The constant $B$ is subtracted exactly once through the two halves $B/2$.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform sign certificates]\label{lem:sign}
On the independent boxes in \eqref{eq:domain}, all $U_\beta$ and $V_\beta$
are nonnegative, and for every frame
\begin{equation}
 p(s),q(t)\geq\frac{5\eps}{16}
              =\frac{10001}{2937888}>0.
 \label{eq:remainderbound}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every source monomial of $A_\beta$ has positive degree. Since $0<\rho\leq1$,
$|A_\beta(s)|\leq\sigma_\beta\rho$, proving $U_\beta\geq0$. Also
$|t^\beta|\leq\rho^{|\beta|}$, proving $V_\beta\geq0$.

Let $g$ and $r$ be the coefficient norms of $G$ and $T$, and write
$M=\sum_\beta\sigma_\beta$. The nonconstant norm of this frame is exactly
$g+r+M\leq B_*$. The losses in $p$ obey
\[
 p(s)\geq\gamma_0/2-\rho g-\tfrac32 B.
\]
The corresponding bound for $q$ is
$q(t)\geq\gamma_0/2-\rho r-\tfrac32B$. Because $|\beta|\geq1$ and
$\rho\leq1$, we have $B\leq\rho M$. Thus either lower bound is at least
\[
 \gamma_0/2-\tfrac32\rho(g+r+M)
 >\eps/2-3\eps/16=5\eps/16.
\]
The stated weak inequality follows. This proof uses finite polynomial
inequalities, with no numerical approximation or compactness step.
\end{proof}

\section{Explicit blocks and tensor certificates}

For a given frame $\ell_k$, regard $\ell_k(h)$ and $\ell_k(t)$ as the same
rational linear form evaluated in its respective variable group. There are
two pure separated pairs and one pair for each mixed group:
\begin{equation}
 (U,V)=(p_k,1),\quad(1,q_k),\quad
 (U_{k,\beta},V_{k,\beta}).
 \label{eq:pairs}
\end{equation}
For each such pair define
\begin{equation}
 \psi(t)=\sqrt{V(t)}\binom{1}{\ell_k(t)},\qquad
 Q(s)=U(s)
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \ell_k(h)^2&-\ell_k(h)\\
 -\ell_k(h)&1
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:gram}
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:sign} makes $\psi$ well defined and $Q$ positive semidefinite.
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 \psi(t)^TQ(s)\psi(t)
   =U(s)V(t)\bigl(\ell_k(t)-\ell_k(h)\bigr)^2.
 \label{eq:gramidentity}
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:separation}, \eqref{eq:frame}, and \eqref{eq:taylor}, and
then imposing all six contact equations, proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
No truncated Taylor term or contact remainder survives this assembly.

The evaluated grouping gives 735 mixed pairs. The two pure pairs for each
of 25 frames add 50 more pairs. Therefore
\begin{equation}
 735+50=785\text{ blocks},\qquad 2\cdot785=1570
 \text{ listed features}.
 \label{eq:counts}
\end{equation}
Repeated features are retained with their block labels; no unsupported claim
of feature linear independence is made.

The same construction works over independently labelled real closed field
extensions $K_c,K_t\supset\R$. Put $(c,\theta)$ in the declared source domain
over $K_c$, and $t$ in the testing box over $K_t$. Every separated term is the
square in $K_c\otimes_\R K_t$ of
\begin{equation}
 (\sqrt{U(s)}\otimes\sqrt{V(t)})
 \bigl(1\otimes\ell_k(t)-\ell_k(h)\otimes1\bigr).
 \label{eq:tensorsquare}
\end{equation}
Square roots are taken separately in their own real closed fields. This is a
local tensor sum-of-squares statement on the displayed domains. It does not
extend these domains or provide the missing global finite cover.

\section{Complete product compiler and seven source columns}
\label{sec:compiler}

For every block in \eqref{eq:gram}, its three symmetric feature products are
exactly
\begin{equation}
 \psi_0^2=V,\qquad \psi_0\psi_1=V\ell_k,\qquad
 \psi_1^2=V\ell_k^2.
 \label{eq:products}
\end{equation}
Their supports have 237 distinct monomials. Fix the lexicographic ordering
of their five-component exponent vectors and write these monomials as
$m_0(t),\ldots,m_{236}(t)$. The maximum degree is six. Define the complete
coefficient array $p_{b,ij,k}$ by
\begin{equation}
 \psi_{b,i}(t)\psi_{b,j}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{236}p_{b,ij,k}m_k(t),
 \qquad 0\leq i\leq j\leq1.
 \label{eq:productmap}
\end{equation}
Every nonzero coefficient is supplied; there are 27,747 records. The number
237 is an ambient monomial count, not a separately asserted rank of the
product span.

For the target retain all seven source variables in \eqref{eq:parent}; set
only $Z=1$ and $z=\theta_*+t$. Because the parent is linear in these seven
variables, there is a complete rational array $T_{k\nu}$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde P_{(d,n,m,C,A,D,\alpha)}(\theta_*+t,1)
 =\sum_{k=0}^{236}\sum_{\nu=0}^6 T_{k\nu}\lambda_\nu m_k(t),
 \quad\lambda=(d,n,m,C,A,D,\alpha).
 \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
All seven columns lie in the listed ambient monomial space. Their 295
nonzero coefficients are delivered, including the full $d$ column.

The resulting conic coefficient matching system is
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{b=0}^{784}\sum_{0\leq i\leq j\leq1}
       (2-\delta_{ij})p_{b,ij,k}Q_{b,ij}
  =\sum_{\nu=0}^6T_{k\nu}\lambda_\nu,\quad 0\leq k\leq236,
 \qquad Q_b\succeq0.
 \label{eq:matching}
\end{equation}
There are 2,355 scalar upper-triangular Gram variables and 237 coefficient
equations. The factor two on the off-diagonal entries is part of the trace
pairing and must be retained. For a coefficient functional $\eta$ on the
ambient monomials, the corresponding dual block has entries
\begin{equation}
 M_b(\eta)_{ij}=\sum_{k=0}^{236}p_{b,ij,k}\eta_k.
 \label{eq:dualblock}
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:matching} and \eqref{eq:dualblock} are evaluated local
coefficient data. They are not an evaluated projection onto the eight original
quantum coordinates. Nor is it asserted that every polynomial nonnegative
on the box belongs to this particular finite certificate cone.
