Alternative existence route: two measurement circles
22 October 2026. Bounded analytical investigation; no solver calls.

Conclusion

The two-circle representation gives a global finite polynomial SOS certificate
for EACH valid full eight-coordinate support. It does not yet give ONE common
finite feature space for all supports. Moreover, an ambient uniform-degree
theorem based only on matrix order four and trigonometric bidegree (1,1) is
false. An explicit counterfamily and proof appear below. The counterfamily is
outside the nine-dimensional Bell-operator subspace, so it does not disprove a
finite SDP lift of Q222, or even a common polynomial degree on that subspace.

1. Exact full-set operator reduction

Write an affine functional on all eight coordinates as

 f(q)=c+sum_x a_x <A_x>+sum_y b_y <B_y>
          +sum_xy e_xy <A_x B_y>.

For z,w on the complex unit circle put

 R(z)=((z+z^-1)/2) Z+((z-z^-1)/(2i)) X,
 A_0=B_0=Z, A_1=R(z), B_1=R(w),

and define the Hermitian 4 by 4 matrix

 B_f(z,w)=c I4+sum_x a_x A_x tensor I2
                 +sum_y b_y I2 tensor B_y
                 +sum_xy e_xy A_x tensor B_y.

This is a Laurent matrix polynomial of bidegree at most (1,1), and its
coefficients depend linearly on the nine coefficients of f. The real-qubit
atom reduction in compiler/effective_algebraic_extraction.tex proves exactly

 f>=0 on Q222  iff  B_f(z,w)>=0 for every (z,w) in T^2.                 (1)

No restriction to correlators is made. Positivity against real state vectors
suffices because these physical matrices are real symmetric at each pair of
angles; it is equivalent to positivity against complex state vectors. Thus the
state sphere has been eliminated by matrix positivity, with all measurement
circle endpoints and singular matrices retained.

2. The verified imported theorem, and what it actually gives

Primary source: Michael A. Dritschel, Factoring non-negative operator valued
trigonometric polynomials in two variables, Mathematische Annalen 391 (2025),
515-537, Theorem 4.1:
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00208-024-02895-9
Public full PDF: https://d-nb.info/1348962968/34

The theorem applies to finite matrix size and nonnegative, rather than merely
strictly positive, Laurent matrix polynomials on T^2. At bidegree (1,1), it
supplies at most two analytic matrix-square summands, with degree (N,1) for
some finite N depending on the input matrix polynomial. The paragraphs before
Theorem 4.1 explicitly distinguish this conclusion from a degree bound uniform
over inputs. The September 2024 correction concerns the missing word finite in
the abstract; it does not strengthen the degree quantifier.

Combining this theorem with (1) proves, for every valid f, an identity

 B_f=F_1^* F_1+F_2^* F_2,

where the F_j are analytic 4 by 4 matrix polynomials of some degree (N(f),1).
If one measurement variable is absent, ordinary matrix Fejer-Riesz gives the
corresponding one-variable assertion. Hence the conclusion covers every valid
support, including degenerate supports, without an atlas of contact strata.

3. Precise finite SDP consequence, conditional only on a common degree

Let A_N={0,...,N} times {0,1}, and form the block column

 V_N(z,w)=(z^i w^j I4 : (i,j) in A_N)^T.

Define K_N to consist of those affine supports f for which

 B_f(z,w)=V_N(z,w)^* H V_N(z,w),   H>=0.                            (2)

Here H has complex Hermitian order 8(N+1). Matching all Fourier coefficients
is a finite system of REAL LINEAR equations in f and the real and imaginary
parts of H. For a frequency (k,l), the matched block is

 sum_{beta-alpha=(k,l)} H_{alpha,beta}.

It equals the corresponding coefficient of B_f, including zero whenever
|k|>1. Complex positivity can be replaced by the usual real symmetric
realification, of order 16(N+1). Thus K_N is an evaluated finite Gram SDP cone.

Let K be the cone of all affine supports nonnegative on the full Q222. Then

 K_1 subset K_2 subset ... subset K,      K=union_{N>=1} K_N.        (3)

Every K_N is closed. Indeed, the constant Fourier coefficient in (2) gives
tr(H)=tr(B_f,00). For a convergent sequence of f's, feasible H's therefore
have bounded trace; positive semidefinite H's with bounded trace form a compact
set. A convergent subsequence supplies a feasible Gram matrix at the limit.

A proof that K=K_N for SOME fixed N would establish a finite SDP lift of
Q222 by finite Gram duality, equivalently by applying the fixed-feature
assembly argument to the real and imaginary parts of V_N(z,w) psi. This is
an alternative sufficient target that avoids the unresolved singular atlas.
Equation (3) alone is not that proof. Existence of some arbitrary finite SDP
lift is also not known to imply stabilization of this polynomial hierarchy.

4. Ambient obstruction: matrix order four and bidegree (1,1) do not bound N

We first give an elementary scalar family. For 0<t<1 let

 a(theta)=1-cos(theta),  b(phi)=1-cos(phi),
 p_t(theta,phi)=a^2 b+a b^2+t^6-3t^2 ab.                            (4)

This has trigonometric bidegree (2,2) and is nonnegative. In fact a,b>=0,
and AM-GM on a^2 b, a b^2, t^6 gives their sum at least 3t^2 ab.

Claim: the p_t do not have trigonometric SOS representations with one common
finite frequency bound, even if the number of squares is unrestricted.

Proof. Suppose a common frequency bound existed. The corresponding Gram
space has fixed finite dimension, so each representation can be chosen with
a fixed maximum number of real trigonometric square summands g_{j,t}^2.
Complex square summands can first be split into real and imaginary parts.
Integration of p_t=sum_j g_{j,t}^2 over T^2 bounds each summand's L2 norm.
In this fixed frequency space, all Fourier coefficients and derivatives up
to order four are consequently bounded uniformly in t.

Set theta=sqrt(2)t X, phi=sqrt(2)t Y. Then, uniformly on each bounded
rectangle in (X,Y),

 t^-6 p_t(sqrt(2)t X,sqrt(2)t Y)
     -> M(X,Y)=1+X^4 Y^2+X^2 Y^4-3X^2 Y^2.                        (5)

Taylor-expand each g_{j,t} at (0,0) through total degree three. After this
substitution and division by t^3, the degree-four remainder is O(t), because
of the uniform fourth-derivative bound. Since the squared sum of the scaled
g's is bounded on a fixed rectangle by (5), the resulting cubic Taylor
polynomials are bounded there. Norm equivalence in the finite-dimensional
space of cubics bounds their coefficients. Along a common subsequence they
converge to real cubic polynomials h_j, and (5) yields

 M=sum_j h_j^2.                                                     (6)

This is impossible. To see it directly, the zero coefficients of X^6,Y^6
in M first remove X^3,Y^3 from every h_j. The zero coefficients of X^4,Y^4
then remove X^2,Y^2, and those of X^2,Y^2 remove X,Y. Each h_j can therefore
contain only 1, XY, X^2Y, XY^2. The coefficient of X^2Y^2 in their squared
sum is a sum of squares of XY coefficients, hence nonnegative, whereas it
is -3 in M. This proves the claim. Adding t^7 to (4) gives a strictly positive
family with the same obstruction and the same limiting argument.

Now reduce the scalar bidegree while increasing matrix order only to four.
For z on T set

 U(z)=[[0,z],[1,0]],     C(z)=(U(z)+U(z)^*)/2.

U(z) is unitary, U(z)^2=z I2, and

 C(z)^2=((2+z+z^-1)/4) I2.

Put C=C(z), D=C(w), and define

 Q_t(z,w)=(I2-C)^2 tensor (I2-D)
             +(I2-C) tensor (I2-D)^2+t^6 I4
             -3t^2 (I2-C) tensor (I2-D).                            (7)

Every entry of Q_t has Laurent bidegree at most (1,1). Its eigenvalues
are the four values of (4) obtained by choosing square roots of z and w:
U(z) has eigenvalues zeta and -zeta, with zeta^2=z. Thus Q_t>=0 on T^2.

If every such Q_t had SOS factors of a common bidegree bound, then p_t would
also have a common bound. Explicitly, h(zeta)=(zeta,1)^T satisfies

 U(zeta^2) h(zeta)=zeta h(zeta),    ||h(zeta)||^2=2.

For h_total=h(zeta) tensor h(eta), one has

 p_t(zeta,eta)=h_total^* Q_t(zeta^2,eta^2) h_total /4.

Inserting a matrix SOS identity for Q_t therefore gives a scalar SOS for
p_t, with degrees increased only by the fixed substitution and multiplication
by h_total. For factors of degree (N,1), the scalar degree is at most
(2N+1,3). This contradicts the proved scalar claim. Hence no uniform finite
polynomial Gram degree exists for all PSD 4 by 4 Laurent matrix polynomials
of bidegree (1,1).

5. The physical scope is narrower, and remains essential

The Q_t in (7) are NOT Bell operators of the form in Section 1. For a Bell
operator B_f the trace is the angle-independent constant 4c, since each
nonidentity Pauli observable and each product term is traceless. In contrast,
write alpha(z)=(2+z+z^-1)/4 and beta(w)=(2+w+w^-1)/4. Directly from (7),

 tr Q_t=4(2+alpha(z)+beta(w)+t^6-3t^2),

which depends on the angles. No reduction of this counterfamily into the
physical Bell subspace has been established here. Nor does a failure of
uniform polynomial degrees by itself rule out an SDP lift using other fixed
semialgebraic features.

Consequently the usable global result is (3), and the exact failed shortcut
is replacing its per-support degree by a bound inferred from matrix order,
bidegree, or compactness alone. Any completion through this route must exploit
the nine Bell coefficients to prove a common finite feature space, or must
provide a separate finite factorization theorem tailored to that subspace.
The determinant/Newton structure may provide such additional information;
it was left to the lead investigation to avoid duplicating that route.
