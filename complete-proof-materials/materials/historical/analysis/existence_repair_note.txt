Bounded repair note: unrestricted tensor SOS while retaining transverse terms
22 October 2026

These are sufficient local lemmas, not a global Q222 closure claim. They do
not require evaluated global features or order-two blocks.

1. FULL NONDEGENERATE MINIMUM: AN EXACT ALL-RATES REPAIR

Let Kc,Kt be separately labelled real closed extensions of R. Let
F_c(Y) be a polynomial in finitely many testing variables, with coefficients
in Kc. Suppose an exact source-side point a in Kc^m satisfies
grad F_c(a)=0 and m_c=F_c(a)>=0. Let the source coefficients, a, and an
independent testing tuple y be bounded and have common real center a0, and
suppose the Hessian of F_c at a has strictly positive definite real residue.

Polynomial Taylor division is exact:
  F_c(Y)=m_c+(Y-a)^T H_c(Y)(Y-a),
where H is a symmetric polynomial matrix over Kc and H_c(a) is half the
Hessian. No inverse is required in this division (2 is a real unit).
Evaluate Y in the testing factor. The resulting finite tensor matrix H has
positive definite real residue. Choose a constant real invertible congruence
making that residue the identity. Apply the fixed frame identity in
source/diagonal/diagonal_proofs.tex:150--172. Its finitely many coefficients
have positive residue, hence each is a finite sum of products of positive
one-factor elements. Their one-factor square roots exist in Kc and Kt.
Thus the exact loss is a finite tensor SOS, plus (sqrt(m_c) tensor 1)^2.
Every coordinate difference is 1 tensor y_i - a_i tensor 1; all rates are
independent. Positive infinitesimal values and shifts are retained exactly.

This proves the regular all-rates case. To apply it one must establish the
actual full critical center and the complete positive Hessian residue.
A residue contact or a scalar Schur Hessian alone is insufficient. At a
degenerate contact it gives no automatic replacement for the child argument.

2. UNIVARIATE NONNEGATIVE RESIDUAL: NO NONDEGENERACY NEEDED

Suppose an EXACT full-parent normal completion has reduced the remaining
term to a univariate polynomial p(T) in Kc[T], nonnegative for every T in Kc.
Then p=A(T)^2+B(T)^2 for A,B in Kc[T]. Proof: over a real closed field,
every real root has even multiplicity; every irreducible quadratic is
(T-b)^2+c^2 with c>0; the leading scalar is nonnegative. Multiply these
two-square expressions using
  (a^2+b^2)(c^2+d^2)=(ac-bd)^2+(ad+bc)^2.
The zero polynomial is immediate. Evaluation at any independent t in Kt
gives an exact tensor SOS. Each root is a finite sum
sum_j (coefficient in Kc) tensor t^j. No rank-two estimate is needed.

For nonnegativity only on T>=0, apply the preceding argument to p(S^2).
Average the SOS under S -> -S, split each polynomial into even and odd
parts, and obtain a finite representation
  p(T)=sum_i A_i(T)^2 + T sum_i B_i(T)^2.
At a nonnegative testing t, use sqrt(t) in Kt. Laurent denominators are
handled on their open domains by pure-testing powers; literal zero must be
proved separately. Again this keeps all infinitesimal source coefficients.

This can repair an actual one-dimensional scalar child, even if its
critical roots merge. Example with epsilon>0 infinitesimal:
  F(T)=(T^2-epsilon)^2.
Its residue is T^4; its exact minima are +/-sqrt(epsilon). Restricting to
epsilon=0 loses the transverse term, but the displayed binomial already is
a finite tensor root under independent evaluation. Nondegenerate centering
at the real residue zero is unnecessary here.

LIMIT OF THIS REPAIR

The parent must first be reduced by an exact tensor identity. Completing
normals using a mixed inverse is not licensed, and the nonnegative scalar
restriction to an exact kernel is not the full parent. A general bivariate
residual has no analogous elementary real-closed factorization. Consequently
these lemmas do not establish the exceptional/global cover in the August
proof or the absent LC1--LC10 body cited by the newer R21 opening.
