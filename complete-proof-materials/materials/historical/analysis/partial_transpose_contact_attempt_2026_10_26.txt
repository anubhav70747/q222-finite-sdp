1. Elementary real separability; no PPT theorem is imported.

Use row-major vec[[W,X],[Y,Z]]=(W,X,Y,Z). Set
 epsilon=[[0,-1],[1,0]], J=epsilon tensor epsilon.
For a real vector z, z^T J z=2 det(mat(z)). Hence z is a real product
vector iff z^T J z=0. Real symmetric 4x4 matrices split as
 Sym_2 tensor Sym_2 plus span{J}.
Partial transpose fixes the first summand and negates J. The canonical
Pauli parent lies in Sym_2 tensor Sym_2, so Tr(PJ)=0 exactly, at all
source/test values, and P is invariant under partial transpose.

Suppose P>=0 and rank(P)=r. Factor P=RR^T with R of size 4xr and full
column rank. H=R^TJR is real symmetric and Tr H=Tr PJ=0. There is an
orthogonal Q with diag(Q^THQ)=0: if a diagonal is nonzero, the zero
trace gives diagonals of opposite signs; rotate their two coordinates
to make one diagonal zero. The remaining compression still has trace
zero. Induction proves the assertion. Each rotation needs only the
real root of a quadratic (or the elementary intermediate-value argument).
Then P=sum_j z_j z_j^T, z_j=RQ e_j, and every z_j has zero determinant.
Thus z_j=x_j tensor y_j. There are at most r<=4 terms. This also covers
singular and zero matrices. The same argument works over a real closed
field, but a factorization over a joint field is not a factorization in
the source/testing tensor ring.
