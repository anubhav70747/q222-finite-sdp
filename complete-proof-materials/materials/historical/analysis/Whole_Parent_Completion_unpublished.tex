A positive quadratic form can be completed square by square at each
measurement. A finite semidefinite certificate requires more: the same
finite family of testing functions must work for every Bell inequality.
The following elementary obstruction explains why eliminating the state
variables first can lose this property.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:poles}
Let $I$ be a nonempty open interval in $(0,\infty)$. If a family of
functions on $I$ contains nonzero multiples of
\[
                    \frac{s}{s+r}
\]
for infinitely many distinct $r>0$, it has no representation
$g_c(s)=f(s)^TQ_c f(s)$ with one finite vector $f$ independent of $c$.
The matrices $Q_c$ may depend arbitrarily on $c$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For distinct $r_1,\ldots,r_k$, a linear relation between
$s/(s+r_i)$ on $I$ is a rational identity. Multiplication by
$\prod_i(s+r_i)$ and evaluation at $s=-r_i$ makes each coefficient
zero. These functions are therefore linearly independent. A Gram
representation with $m$ fixed functions would place all of them in the
span of the $m(m+1)/2$ products $f_i f_j$, which is impossible.
\end{proof}

\section{The cancellation in the Bell slack}

Consider the stationary C2 source family used in the uniform contact
certificate, with $d=1$ and $\lambda=\beta=0$. At the testing angle
$v=0$, write the state as
\[
 W=w+uM,\quad Y=uw-M,\qquad
 X=N-u\zeta,\quad Z=\zeta+uN.
\]
The complete homogeneous slack numerator is
\begin{align}
 T={}&(C+D+\tfrac12)(1+u^2)M^2+A(-M+uw)^2 \nonumber\\
 &+(m+\tfrac12)(1+u^2)N^2
       +n(N-u\zeta)^2+\alpha(\zeta+uN)^2 .       \label{eq:whole}
\end{align}
All six source coefficients are nonnegative. Thus this restriction
already has a finite polynomial sum of squares, including $n=0$.
The two normals $M,N$ need not be eliminated.

To see what elimination changes, collect the $N$ terms. Set
\[
 A_N=n+m+\tfrac12,\qquad B_N=m+\alpha+\tfrac12,
 \qquad h_N=A_N+B_Nu^2.
\]
Their exact completion is
\begin{align}
 &h_NN^2+2(\alpha-n)uN\zeta+(nu^2+\alpha)\zeta^2 \nonumber\\
 &\quad=h_N\left(N+\frac{(\alpha-n)u\zeta}{h_N}\right)^2
 +\left(nu^2+\alpha-
       \frac{(n-\alpha)^2u^2}{h_N}\right)\zeta^2.       \label{eq:split}
\end{align}
Both terms on the right are nonnegative, since the original
two-variable form is positive semidefinite. Their rational parts cancel
in the sum. Keeping the first term separately introduces a moving
denominator that was absent from~\eqref{eq:whole}.

Indeed, restrict the state to $W=Y=0$, $X=-u$, $Z=1$. This choice is
independent of the source and gives $N=M=w=0$, $\zeta=1$. The first
term of~\eqref{eq:split} becomes
\begin{equation}
 g_c(s)=\frac{(n-\alpha)^2}{B_N}\frac{s}{s+r_c},
 \qquad s=u^2,\quad r_c=\frac{A_N}{B_N}.             \label{eq:moving}
\end{equation}
Normalizing the state multiplies every $g_c$ by the same positive
function $1/(1+s)$ and does not change the argument.

\section{The obstruction occurs at actual stationary sources}

Use the native source parameters
\begin{equation}
 j=1,\qquad H=\tfrac12,\qquad B=T=\varepsilon>0,
 \qquad \tfrac14\leq p\leq\tfrac12 .              \label{eq:path}
\end{equation}
The rational source dictionary has the following uniform limits as
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$:
\[
 n\longrightarrow\frac{p}{4(1-p)},\qquad
 m\longrightarrow\frac{p}{4(1-p)},\qquad
 \alpha\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad r_c\longrightarrow\frac{2}{2-p}.
\]
The denominators remain nonzero on this compact $p$ interval. Hence
$n>0$ for all sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$, uniformly in
$p$; the other event weights and native gaps are positive by the
source formulas. In particular these are admissible stationary
sources, not independently chosen event coefficients.

At the endpoints, the limiting ratios are $8/7$ and $4/3$. For every
sufficiently small fixed positive $\varepsilon$, the rational function
$p\mapsto r_c$ is therefore nonconstant and $n-\alpha$ is nonzero.
It takes infinitely many positive values. Proposition~\ref{prop:poles}
applies to~\eqref{eq:moving} on any open interval of $s>0$, including
the remaining region $s>(9/256)^2$.

Thus the separated positive Schur term has no finite fixed testing
family, even though its sum with the second term has the polynomial
certificate~\eqref{eq:whole}. A finite certificate for the full slack
must preserve this cancellation before choosing its positive pieces.
This result excludes the separated Schur construction; it places no
obstruction on a different Gram representation of the full slack.

\section{What remains for the global construction}

The uniform contact certificate and the ten-row Bob completion admit
the quantitative R1 sources on
\[
 |u|\leq9/256\quad\hbox{or}\quad |v|\geq1/16.
\]
The strip $|u|>9/256$, $|v|<1/16$ still needs a certificate for the
complete slack. The calculation above identifies a cancellation that
such a construction should retain.

Source coverage is a separate requirement. For example, the same
native dictionary has the admissible path
\begin{equation}
 B=s^2,\quad T=1/s,\quad j=1,\quad p=\tfrac12,
 \quad H=s/2,\qquad 0<s\leq2^{-10}.                \label{eq:r2}
\end{equation}
Its contact angles satisfy $u_c=s$ and $v_c=\sqrt{s}$, both inside
the testing cap. Nevertheless $T\geq1024$, outside the R1 source
domain. Its physicality numerator is
\[
 8N=s^6-s^5+5s^4-7s^3+6s^2-8s+4>3.
\]
The positive source denominators and gaps follow directly from
$E=1$, $L=s+1/2+3/(2s)$, $HT=1/2$ and
$1-s/2+s^2/2>0$. In native coordinates this is the bounded-ratio R2
branch, $a_s=1$, $t_s=1/s$, $B_s=s^2$,
$r=h=1/(2+s^2)$.

A global evaluated pencil requires admission of these R2 sources as
well as the remaining R1 strip. The long proof treats R2 separately;
testing coverage of its contact does not establish its source
admission. A finite source-cone reduction could replace a separate
R2 construction, but such a reduction is not supplied by the current
quantitative certificates.
