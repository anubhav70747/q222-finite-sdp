THE CONTACT SCALAR HAS A FIXED FOUR-FUNCTION SOS; COUPLING REMAINS
26 October 2026. One bounded construction attempt; no publication.

Completed result. In the source-only contact-Hankel coordinates, gamma
has an exact source/testing-separated SOS on the FIXED testing span
{1,u,v,uv}, with at most three squares, and no multiplier. This applies
to every globally valid finite native C2 source, including n=0 (and to

1. Elementary scalar lemma.

Let g(u,v) be a real polynomial of separate degree at most two in each
variable, nonnegative for ALL real u,v, and zero at (u0,v0). Put
s=u-u0,t=v-v0. Nonnegativity makes its two first derivatives zero, so
uniquely
 g=A s^2+B t^2+2C st+2D s^2t+2E st^2+F s^2t^2
  =(s,t,st) G (s,t,st)^T,
 G=[[A,C,D],[C,B,E],[D,E,F]].                         (1)
Then G>=0. Indeed, for any (x,y,z) with xyz!=0, choose
lambda=z/(xy), s=z/y, t=z/x. Thus (s,t,st)=lambda(x,y,z), so
(x,y,z)G(x,y,z)^T=lambda^(-2)g(s+u0,t+v0)>=0.
Such vectors are dense; continuity gives G>=0 on all real vectors.
The converse follows directly from (1). This proves the lemma without
Hilbert's ternary-quartic theorem or a pointwise testing factorization.
The argument also holds over a real closed field.

The coefficients are explicit source-only derivatives at the zero:
 A=g_uu/2, B=g_vv/2, C=g_uv/2,
 D=g_uuv/4, E=g_uvv/4, F=g_uuvv/4.                   (2)
There is no missing coefficient or affine Gram freedom in this
three-root representation. Semidefinite/singular G is allowed.

2. Application to the actual native parent.

Let psi_c=vec(Psi_c) be the source contact, delta=det(Psi_c)!=0, and
use the source-only local congruence of the preceding contact note.
The fourth coefficient in its symmetric/antisymmetric state split is
 gamma_c(u,v)=psi_c^T P_c(u,v)psi_c/(4 delta^2).
The raw canonical P_c has separate degree at most two in u,v. At the
contact P_c psi_c=0, so gamma vanishes there. Global validity makes
gamma nonnegative on the ENTIRE real testing plane, not merely its
positive quadrant. Explicitly, the parent satisfies
 P(-u,v)=Su P(u,v)Su, Su=diag(1,1,-1,-1),
 P(u,-v)=Sv P(u,v)Sv, Sv=diag(1,-1,1,-1).
Thus positivity for positive testing angles and every state extends
to negative signs by changing the state, and to axes by continuity.
Gamma itself need not be even: psi_c stays fixed, while its transformed
state under these identities is also an allowed test state.

Take g=gamma in (1)--(2), with u0=u_c,v0=v_c. If phi=(1,u,v,uv)^T,
 define D_c=[[-u_c,1,0,0],[-v_c,0,1,0],
             [u_c v_c,-v_c,-u_c,1]].
Then the EVALUATED coefficient matrix
 Q_gamma(c)=D_c^T G_gamma(c) D_c>=0
gives gamma=phi^T Q_gamma(c) phi for all independent testing angles.
It has order four and rank at most three. Factoring G_gamma over the
source field yields at most three separated squares. The source-only
shifts introduce no new testing functions or mixed square roots.

For native C2, delta=(b+zr h)/a>0, z=c^2, h=b-a^2t. For native C1,
delta=S(1+k^2r(1-theta))>0. Neither (1), (2) nor D_c divides by n,D
or beta. Thus the permitted literal zero-event faces remain included.
This is a genuine fixed-library result for gamma, with T=1. It is
stronger than merely knowing a rank bound for pointwise factorizations.

3. One explicit coupling completion and its exact unresolved condition.

After the same source-only contact congruence, write the full state
matrix in a constant symmetric/antisymmetric basis as
 K=[[H,zeta],[zeta^T,gamma]], H_02=H_11-gamma.
Each component zeta_i has separate testing degree <=2, with zero
constant after shifting to (s,t)=(0,0). Let
 zeta_i=sum_{j,k=0}^2 a_(jk)^(i) s^j t^k, a_(00)^(i)=0.
An exact finite polynomial division gives zeta=V(s,t) q, q=(s,t,st)^T:
 V_i1=a10+a20 s,
 V_i2=a01+a02 t,
 V_i3=a11+a21 s+a12 t+a22 st.                        (3)
All entries lie in the same fixed bilinear testing span. Other V can
give the same zeta and may permit a better allocation.

For a chosen V, suppose V annihilates ker G_gamma and set the
SOURCE-only generalized inverse G_gamma^dagger. Then exactly
 K=diag(H-V G_gamma^dagger V^T,0)
   +[V; q^T G_gamma] G_gamma^dagger [V^T,G_gamma q].   (4)
The second term is a separated SOS with a fixed finite polynomial
feature list. Equation (4) avoids the mixed inverse gamma^(-1).
But it leaves the explicit condition
 H-V G_gamma^dagger V^T >=0                         (5)
as a polynomial-matrix assertion at every independent test, together
with a separated certificate for that remainder. Neither the range
condition nor (5) follows automatically from native stationarity.

The distinction from the ordinary Schur condition is exact. If G>0
and gamma=q^TGq>0, put a=G^(1/2)q. Then
 V G^(-1)V^T-zeta zeta^T/gamma
 =V G^(-1/2)[I-aa^T/(a^Ta)]G^(-1/2)V^T >=0.
Thus (5) asks for MORE budget than the pointwise Schur complement,
not a consequence of it. The projector version gives the same fact

completion is asserted.

4. Residual implication and multiplier scope.

The new lemma certifies precisely the scalar gamma and supplies a
finite algebraic representation of all three couplings. To finish this
route one must choose a compatible coupling Gram/allocation and prove
a fixed-feature separated SOS for the remainder, uniformly over all
native rates, zero faces and independent tests. Enlarging the features
to fractional powers may change the available allocation; no such
uniform completion is established here.

No fixed pure-testing multiplier for the FULL F is proved. The earlier
optimal-current calculation only rules out a finite testing dictionary
for that particular reconstruction; it does not exclude other currents
or a native-C2-specific multiplier. Likewise det(H3)F being SOS does not
provide (5) or permit cancellation of that mixed multiplier.
