NO FIXED SEPARABLE POLYNOMIAL MULTIPLIER FOR ALL NATIVE C1+C2 CONTACTS
26 October 2026. Private transfer argument; no numerical work.

Conclusion. The theorem in multiplier-lean/fixed_polynomial_multiplier_
obstruction.tex DOES exclude a single pair of fixed nonzero polynomials
p(u),q(v), nonnegative on all of R, for which p(u)q(v)F_c is an ordinary
real polynomial SOS for EVERY strict native C1 and C2 contact source.
The same assertion includes, a fortiori, all their permitted finite
zero-event faces. Roots and their coefficients may depend on the source;
no a priori degree bound or fixed feature library is assumed.

The transfer does not require a polynomial certificate for the two
noncoercive closed faces. A tight-face decomposition removes them.

A. Tighten the explicit obstruction family to a nonlocal contact.

Retain eta=1-T and Bell operator B_T=S+eta(A1+B0). Its local maximum is
ell_T=2+2eta=4-2T: this is its maximum on the sixteen deterministic
assignments, attained by the all-positive assignment. Put
 w_T=4-2T+4T^3,
 b_T=max quantum expectation of B_T.
The existing strict-validity proof gives b_T<w_T<4, for 0<T<=1/100.
The maximum is attained on the compact real two-qubit parameter/state
space by the two-observable reduction.

The same exact polynomial state test in the multiplier theorem, with
x=y=1 and T=t^2, has
 F_T(-t,t;psi_t)=(8/3)t^6+O(t^8),
 (1+t^2)^2 ||psi_t||^2=1+O(t^2).
Consequently its Bell expectation is
 ell_T+(4/3)T^3+O(T^4)>ell_T
for all sufficiently small T. Thus ell_T<b_T<w_T. A maximizing test
cannot have either measurement pair commuting: a commuting pair gives
a local behavior and cannot exceed ell_T. A maximizing real test can
therefore be expressed with finite nonzero half-angles; the exact state
sign congruences make both angles positive.

The tight slack f_T^*=b_T I-B_T has canonical coefficients
 d*=4-b_T>0,
 lambda*=m*=A*=(b_T-ell_T)/4>0,
 n*=D*=alpha*=(b_T-2T)/4>0,
 C*=beta*=(b_T-2)/4>0.
It is quantum-valid and zero at that finite noncommuting contact.
No explicit formula or rationality for b_T is needed.

B. Decompose INSIDE this contact, so no closed-face atom occurs.

Fix its angles and normalized real contact state. The equations
 P(c)psi=0, psi^T P_u(c)psi=0, psi^T P_v(c)psi=0
are six homogeneous linear equations in the nine canonical source
coefficients. Intersect their kernel with c>=0 and sum c_i=1. This is
a compact polytope. Its vertices have at most seven positive source
coordinates, since the augmented linear system has rank at most seven.
The normalized source c_T^* is a finite convex combination of these
vertices.

Each vertex with d>0 is globally valid by the stationary-zero criterion
SG7 in latex/chapters/04_geometry.tex. Every physical d>0 source with
this finite noncommuting contact has coercive exponent support, by
SG17 and its preceding explicit support argument. Hence it has at
least five positive event weights (SG9), and its at-most-seven active
source coordinates give at most six. The finite CHSH-preserving event
symmetries put it in C1 or C2, including a permitted third omission
(SG10 and the exact native entry parametrizations). A d=0 vertex is
an event atom: its nonnegative event sum vanishes on the contact only
if every retained event amplitude does, so the contact slice of d=0
is the simplex on those event atoms.

Equivalently the existing global extreme-source decomposition suffices:
every summand must be tight at this contact, and SG17 excludes every
positive-d noncoercive closed-face summand. The direct contact-polytope
argument avoids importing that global decomposition.

C. One multiplier for the native charts gives one for all finite orbits.

Physical relabelings of two binary observables, recanonicalized with
A0=B0=Z, act on each real half-angle by u -> +/-u or +/-1/u (likewise v),
possibly exchanging the parties. Their state congruences have polynomial
numerators divided only by sqrt(1+u^2) and/or sqrt(1+v^2). This follows
by choosing the new first observable as the Z axis: if inputs are
swapped the rotation numerator is [[1,u],[-u,1]]; outcome reversals
use fixed Pauli matrices and/or reciprocal half-angles. Thus this
finite physical group preserves the following multiplier property.
For an explicit verification use the three involutions printed after
SG17, whose event-cycle permutations are i->5-i,3-i,2-i modulo eight.
Let N(w)=[[1,w],[-w,1]], X=[[0,1],[1,0]], and let Pi interchange the
two physical state factors. Their exact RAW-parent identities are
 D_u F_(gA c)(u,v;psi)=v^2 F_c(-u,-1/v;(N(u) tensor I)psi),
 D_v F_(gB c)(u,v;psi)=u^2 F_c(1/u,-v;(X tensor N(v))psi),
 F_(gT c)(u,v;psi)=u^2v^2 F_c(-1/v,-1/u;Pi psi).
To check: gA sends observables to (A1,A0,B0,-B1), then conjugating by
N(u)/sqrt(D_u) sends the first Alice observable to Z and the second
to A1(-u). gB sends them to (-A0,A1,B1,B0), followed by X on Alice
and N(v)/sqrt(D_v) on Bob. gT sends them to (B0,-B1,A0,-A1).
These maps preserve CHSH and permute the eight event definitions
exactly as above. Identities initially written for uv!=0 are rational
identities; after clearing they hold polynomially, including axes.
Their cycle permutations generate all i->+/-i+j, j=0,...,7, giving an
explicit finite list of sixteen source relabelings. Composing the
identities proves the required rational/SOS transport for each orbit.

Precisely let M=pq, and B=(deg M+6)/2. Every polynomial SOS root of
MF has total degree at most B, because F has total degree at most six;
higher leading squares cannot cancel. For a relabeling g, let U,V be
its transformed half-angles, i,j indicate reciprocal substitutions,
and e,f indicate the sqrt(D_u),sqrt(D_v) state denominators. With an
appropriate polynomial state numerator N, the raw forms satisfy
 D_u^e D_v^f F_(g c)(u,v;psi)
 =u^(2i) v^(2j) F_c(U,V;N psi).
After substituting the hypothesized SOS and multiplying by
u^(2B)v^(2B), every square becomes polynomial. In particular
 M_g=u^(2B)v^(2B) D_u^e D_v^f p(U)q(V)
is a fixed nonzero separable polynomial, nonnegative on the entire
real testing plane, and M_g F_(g c) is SOS. Party interchange merely
exchanges the two univariate factors. Extra harmless even powers may
be used if a particular convention uses a larger clearing exponent.

Take the product M_* of the finitely many M_g, including the identity
chart. Every M_g and every quotient M_*/M_g is a product of globally
nonnegative univariate polynomials, hence is polynomial SOS. Therefore
M_* certifies every native orbit with ONE fixed separable multiplier.
It also certifies every event atom: the exact SG2 event forms are
squares times D_u and/or D_v, and D_u,D_v are SOS.

If the assumption covers only strict six-event sources, the native
third-omission opening argument in EXACT_LIFT_ROUTE.md, section
'Only the strict contact families need new admission proofs', gives
sequences of strict contacts approaching every permitted finite face.
The degree of M_*F is fixed. Its polynomial SOS cone is closed: the
integral of the monomial Gram on a compact box bounds the trace of PSD
Grams for any coefficient-convergent sequence. Hence the same M_*
certifies those limits. No uniform positive Gram margin is required.

D. Contradiction with the original fixed-multiplier theorem.

By B--C, M_*F_T^* is SOS. The actual strictly valid obstruction parent is
 F_T=F_T^*+(w_T-b_T) D_uD_v (W^2+X^2+Y^2+Z^2).
The second summand times M_* is polynomial SOS, since w_T-b_T>0 and
all its testing factors are products of nonnegative univariate
polynomials. Therefore M_*F_T would be SOS for every sufficiently
small T, contradicting the original theorem for this FIXED M_*.
This proves the claimed native-union obstruction. It does not identify
which of C1 or C2, or which individual native source, fails a given
multiplier; it does not exclude source-dependent multipliers,
nonseparable multipliers vanishing at the origin, or fractional
pure-testing features.

E. A separate strengthening on the FULL canonical family.

For ANY fixed polynomial T0(u,v) with T0(0,0)>0, the original scaled

 t^(-6) T0(-tx,ty) F_(t^2)(-tx,ty;psi_t)
 ->(4/3)T0(0,0)(Motzkin(x,y)+2)
coefficientwise in a fixed finite degree. Thus the same closedness

separability assumption. If T0 vanishes at the origin, its leading
homogeneous form remains in the limiting product, and this particular
argument does NOT settle that product (for example T0=u^2+v^2).
The orbit-saturation argument C alone does not transfer a nonseparable
representative-chart multiplier: arbitrary nonnegative bivariate
quotients need not be SOS, and reciprocal clearing can destroy a

does handle a common multiplier for ALL original-frame native orbits.

Closed-face caution. SG12/SG13 themselves describe limiting univariate

F. Nonseparable positive-at-origin obstruction for ALL native orbits.

Stronger precise statement: no fixed real polynomial T0(u,v) with
T0(0,0)>0 can make T0 F_c SOS for every strict native C1/C2 source AND
all their finite physical relabeling orbits in the original testing
frame. No separability or SOS assumption on T0 is needed here. This
is not asserted for a multiplier known only on the two representative
charts, because C's orbit saturation may give zeros at the origin.

Set kappa(T)=(b_T-ell_T)/T^3. Part A gives
 liminf kappa(T)>=4/3, and kappa(T)<4.
Along a sequence T=t^2 ->0 choose kappa(T)->kappa0 in [4/3,4]. The tight
parent has the exact coefficient limit under the same state pullback:
 t^(-6) F_T^*(-tx,ty;psi_t)
 ->(4/3)[M(x,y)+(3kappa0/4-1)].
Indeed subtracting the gap (4-kappa(T))t^6 D_uD_v||psi_t||^2
from the original limit (4/3)(M+2) gives this formula. The new constant
c0=3kappa0/4-1 belongs to [0,2]. For EVERY c0>=0, M+c0 is not polynomial
SOS: each degree-three root is constant on both axes, so the x^2y^2
coefficient of its square sum would be nonnegative, whereas it is -3.

Use the contact decomposition B to write F_T^*=N_T+E_T, where N_T is a
nonnegative source sum of native-orbit parents and E_T is an event SOS.
If only strict contacts are covered, first use the fixed-degree opening
and closure argument in C for their finite omission faces. Pull back
and divide by t^6; write f_t=n_t+e_t. The degrees of these polynomials
in x,y are uniformly at most twelve. Both n_t,e_t are nonnegative for

Furthermore e_t is ordinary polynomial SOS. On a fixed box,
 0 <= integral e_t <= integral f_t,
and the right side stays bounded by the explicit coefficient limit.
The positive-definite integral monomial Gram therefore bounds the
trace of a PSD Gram representing e_t. Pass to a subsequence with

Then n_t=f_t-e_t also converges, to n. The common-multiplier hypothesis
makes T0(-tx,ty)n_t SOS of degree at most 12+deg(T0), independently

T0(-tx,ty)->T0(0,0)>0 coefficientwise, fixed-degree SOS closedness gives
T0(0,0)n SOS, hence n SOS. Thus the displayed limit f=n+e would be SOS,
contradicting M+c0. This argument never asserts that T0 E_T is SOS:
event terms are controlled before multiplication by the bounded
nonnegative decomposition. It therefore closes the event-compatibility
issue for the ORIGINAL-FRAME ORBIT statement without an extra premise.

G. Fixed rational testing spans regular at the angular origin.

A direct corollary of F excludes a fixed finite source-independent
rational testing span phi_i(u,v;psi), linear in the four independent
state amplitudes, if every feature admits a polynomial numerator over
one scalar denominator Q(u,v) with Q(0,0)!=0. Indeed a source-only PSD
Gram for F_c on this span gives, after factoring in the source field,
 Q(u,v)^2 F_c = sum_j R_j(u,v;psi)^2
with ordinary polynomial roots. Thus the SAME fixed multiplier Q^2,
positive at the origin, would certify all native orbits, contradicting
F. A finite collection of such spans is equally excluded: take the
product of their finitely many denominators, still nonzero at zero.
Polynomial identity after clearing holds everywhere, even if the
original rational features have poles away from the origin.

This is a scalar ANGULAR denominator hypothesis; it does not address
source-dependent denominators, rational state-dependent denominators,
fractional/algebraic testing functions, or rational angular features
whose unavoidable denominators vanish at the origin. For only the two
representative native charts, the separable theorem also excludes
fixed rational spans with a common nonzero SEPARABLE denominator,
without requiring its value at the origin to be nonzero.
