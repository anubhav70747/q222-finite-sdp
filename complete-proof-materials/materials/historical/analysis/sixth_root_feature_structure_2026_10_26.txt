WHY THIRDS PLUS HALVES DO NOT AUTOMATICALLY REDUCE THE SIXTH-ROOT GRID
26 October 2026. One bounded structural pass; no native admission claim.

Result. The determinant cubic circuits use only third powers of the
physical half-angle coordinates; multiplying their roots by a common
linear state amplitude does not introduce sixth exponents. However,
the full sixth-root cone is strictly larger than the natural100-feature
span retaining only exponents {0,1/3,1/2,2/3,1} in each angle. An exact
state-linear, bidegree-(2,2) example proves this. It is NOT asserted to
be a native Bell source. Thus no automatic lossless reduction follows
from cubic circuits or their four exact native sample certificates.

1. The natural structure of the196 functions.

Write p=u^(1/3),q=v^(1/3). The196-function grid is exactly the four
parity modules
 p^i q^j psi_l,                   0<=i,j<=3;             64,
 sqrt(p) p^i q^j psi_l,           0<=i<=2,0<=j<=3;       48,
 sqrt(q) p^i q^j psi_l,           0<=i<=3,0<=j<=2;       48,
 sqrt(pq) p^i q^j psi_l,          0<=i,j<=2;             36.
Here l ranges over the four state coordinates. This is the ordinary
positive-quadrant weighted-SOS module in the CUBIC coordinates p,q,
with the degree caps forced by the bidegree of F(p^3,q^3).
In particular u^(1/6),u^(1/2),u^(5/6) are respectively sqrt(p),
p sqrt(p),p^2 sqrt(p); the half-power is the MIDDLE monomial of an
entire weighted quadratic row, not a replacement for its endpoints.
The fields generated by thirds and halves do contain sixth roots:
u^(1/6)=sqrt(u)/u^(1/3). But a fixed source-linear testing SPAN is not
closed under multiplying or dividing testing functions. Distinct
fractional monomials are linearly independent on u,v>0.

2. What the determinant circuits actually require.

For the three-term AM--GM packet, put A=X^(1/3),B=Y^(1/3),C=Z^(1/3).
 X+Y+Z-3(XYZ)^(1/3)
 =1/2 sum_(h in {A,B,C}) sum_(i<j) [sqrt(h)(i-j)]^2.       (1)
The determinant outer terms are monomials in U=u^2,V=v^2. Hence the
angle exponents in each root are (e_h+2e_i)/3, componentwise, not
multiples with denominator six. Integer clearing shifts and polynomial
state/cofactor rows preserve denominator three. This is exactly the
third-root mechanism in checks/c2_multiplied_certificate.py; it proves
an identity for det(H3)F, not cancellation of that mixed multiplier.
No1/6 or5/6 is forced by these determinant circuits alone.

Applying AM--GM across different squared state amplitudes would instead
create fractional STATE powers. That does not automatically embed in
any grid linear in psi. Multiplication by one common linear state
amplitude is safe and changes no angle exponents; general state-matrix
reconstruction remains an additional step.

3. Exact obstruction to the naive100-feature replacement.

Let S={0,1/3,1/2,2/3,1} and define
 P(u,v)=1+u^2v+uv^2-3uv>=0,             u,v>=0.
It is a single cubic AM--GM circuit. The state-linear form P(u,v)Z^2
has a Gram in the full196 grid. To see this, put r=u^(1/6),s=v^(1/6),
A=r^4s^2,B=r^2s^4,C=1. Formula (1) gives nine squares; their root
monomials are among
 (r^6s^3,r^4s^5,r^2s,r^5s^4,r^3s^6,rs^2,r^4s^2,r^2s^4,1).
All exponents are0,...,6. The displayed construction uses both1/6
and5/6 features. The following proves that at least one missing sixth
feature is necessary: no alternative SOS on S tensor S exists.

Suppose it did. After u=r^6,v=s^6, this would be an ordinary SOS of
 P6=1+r^12s^6+r^6s^12-3r^6s^6,
using exponents i,j in J={0,2,3,4,6}. The half-Newton-polygon rule for
SOS restricts every root to
 E={(0,0),(2,2),(2,3),(2,4),(3,2),(3,3),(3,4),
                         (3,6),(4,2),(4,3),(4,4),(6,3)}.
The rule follows by exposing any root exponent outside half the target
Newton polygon: its highest squared face cannot cancel in a sum of
squares. Average the Gram under r and s sign changes. Only pairs with
the same exponent parity remain. Inspect the ZERO coefficients in the
following order:
 coefficient   forces root coefficient
 (4,8)         (2,4)
 (4,6)         (2,3)
 (8,4)         (4,2)
 (6,4)         (3,2)
 (6,8)         (3,4)
 (8,6)         (4,3)
 (8,8)         (4,4)
 (4,4)         (2,2).
At each stage the indicated target coefficient is a sum of squares of
the indicated root coefficients; all possible off-diagonal pairs have
already disappeared. Each forced zero annihilates its whole PSD Gram
row. Only(0,0),(3,3),(3,6),(6,3) remain. The coefficient of r^6s^6 is
then a sum of squared(3,3) coefficients, nonnegative, contradicting -3.
Evaluating a putative state-linear S-grid SOS at Z=1,W=X=Y=0 gives this
same contradiction. This is not the scalar Motzkin limiting argument:
P has bidegree(2,2) in the actual angles, whereas M(u,v) has larger
angle degree and already admits a scalar half-power identity.

4. Precise native implication still missing.

Let J6={0,...,6} and J={0,2,3,4,6}. For a native source, reducing to
the100-feature span is equivalent to finding a full sixth-root PSD
Gram with every row having i or j in {1,5} zero, while retaining the
complete coefficient identity. Existing full-rank reduced rational
Grams cannot simply be projected onto that face; projection changes
the polynomial. The example above shows there is no universal
algebraic reallocation for all state-linear biquadratic forms. It
does NOT show failure of the smaller span at any native Bell source,
nor prove that every native source needs both1/6 and5/6.

The first unproved implication is therefore special to the native
nine-coefficient matrix identities: do they force admission on that
smaller Gram face, or require some weighted cubic-coordinate channels?
Determinant balance, scalar Hurwitz identities and the known individual
rational certificates do not settle it. No native grid reduction or
universal sixth-root admission is claimed here. Stopping at this result.
