Independent bounded review: fixed separable polynomial multipliers
23 October 2026

VERDICT
The stated theorem is supported. For every fixed pair of nonzero real
polynomials p(u),q(v), each nonnegative on the entire real line, the full
canonical parent p(u)q(v)F_c(T) is not polynomial SOS for every sufficiently
small positive T in the printed, strictly physically valid family. The
two-LMI polynomial-completion impossibility follows as stated. No decisive
defect was found. The threshold is existential, depends on p,q, and is not
evaluated by this proof.

1. Full-state validity, source lines 93-159
The canonical coefficient map is exact, including all nine coefficients.
For 0<T<=1/100, d=2T-4T^3>0 and all eight event coefficients are positive.
Swapping Alice's settings gives the symmetric doubly tilted CHSH expression.
I independently reconstructed the printed H_T from
  ZZ+Z B1+A1 Z-A1 B1+(1-T)(Z I+I Z),
with A1=cZ+rX and B1=eZ+sX. Thus its determinant is the relevant full-state
determinant, not a determinant of a restricted state ansatz.

The determinant lower bound has the correct inequality directions. Put
a=p+q0. If R_T<0, pq0<=p+q0 gives a<18T<2; then
2pq0<=a^2/2<=a and R_T>=2a-18T. Since R_T is negative,
  8pq0 R_T >= 2a^2 R_T >= 4a^3-36Ta^2 >= -432T^3.
Together with sigma=4T^3 and P_T>=144-240T this yields
  det(wI-H_T)>=(144-960T)T^3>0.
If R_T>=0 the positive sigma P_T term already suffices. Every sine sign
is retained in the circle reduction. The initial matrix at c=e=1 has the
four printed strictly positive eigenvalues. The angle torus is connected,
and a continuous symmetric matrix with nowhere-zero determinant cannot
change inertia. Hence wI-H_T is positive definite on the whole torus.
The usual two-qubit/reflection reduction, explicitly a project input,
extends this to all quantum models. Real symmetric positivity also holds
on complex state vectors. Literal commuting angles and all zero-sine
endpoints are included, without division by a coefficient.

2. Completion-to-polynomial-SOS interface, lines 61-89
I independently expanded both generic identities, with nine independent
source coefficients and four independent selector entries:
  q Phi = [a;Ub]^T G [a;Ub] + U[a;b]^T H[a;b],
  Phi(u^2,v;L(u)psi) = u^2(1+u^2)F_c(u,v;psi).
Both are exact. Global positivity of the univariate real symmetric
polynomial matrices G,H permits the standard univariate matrix-SOS
factorization, including singular matrices. Substitution U=u^2 and then
L(u)psi is polynomial, so it gives the claimed necessary polynomial SOS.
The principal-minor estimate |Z_ij|^2<=q^2(P+K)_ii(N+K)_jj bounds
deg Z_ij<=deg q+2; vanishing diagonal factors force Z_ij=0 and cause no
exception. No tensor inverse or mixed source/testing cancellation is used.
The univariate matrix factorization is the standard external algebraic
input for this completion consequence; the stronger non-SOS statement
for p(u)q(v)F itself does not need it.

3. Critical full-state pullback and uniform degree, lines 162-220
All four polynomial state entries are present and agree with the printed
Alice rotation numerator. The exact leading term is
  F_c(t^2)(-tx,ty;psi_t) = (4/3)t^6(M+2)+O(t^8).
The O(t^8) is an actual finite polynomial remainder. The independent
normal-square calculation in the supplied checker keeps both state
normal directions. My separate calculation verified the leading term
and the total (x,y) degree: it is 12 for the pulled-back F and 16 after
the factor u^2(1+u^2). Thus q of degree 2r gives target degree <=2r+16
and SOS root degree <=r+8. The latter also follows directly because the
highest homogeneous parts of real squares cannot cancel. Coefficients
of any hypothetical selectors may diverge with t; this does not defeat
the fixed target-degree argument.

4. Closedness, cancellation and the contradiction, lines 222-259
For a full monomial vector z through a fixed root degree, the cube moment
matrix B=integral zz^T is strictly positive definite: a nonzero polynomial
has positive squared integral. Coefficient convergence bounds the
integrals of the SOS polynomials, hence Tr(BQ_j), hence Tr Q_j and every
entry of any PSD Gram Q_j. A convergent subsequence supplies a Gram for
the coefficient limit. This is the correct bounded-degree SOS closedness
argument; it does not assume bounded selector coefficients.

The lowest nonzero Taylor terms of p and q are positive even monomials.
After u=-tx,v=ty, the normalized limit is a positive constant times
  x^(2h)y^(2k)(M(x,y)+2).
Square monomial cancellation is valid by evaluating on a coordinate
hyperplane and then dividing every SOS root by that coordinate, repeated
as necessary. Finally M+2 cannot be SOS: all roots would have degree<=3;
its constant restrictions to both axes force every root to have the form
a0+a1 xy+a2 x^2y+a3 xy^2. The x^2y^2 coefficient of their squared sum is
sum a1^2>=0, contradicting the actual coefficient -3.
If successful certificates existed arbitrarily close to T=0, their square
roots t=sqrt(T) would give the prohibited sequence. Thus the conclusion is
failure for all sufficiently small T, not merely along one subsequence.

5. Scope
This excludes fixed globally nonnegative SEPARABLE polynomial multipliers
and, in particular, fixed polynomial Bob multipliers in the printed
two-LMI ansatz. It does not exclude source-dependent multipliers, general
nonseparable multipliers, or fixed algebraic testing functions. The exact
ramification identity M(x,s^2)=three polynomial squares is correct; it
explains why no half-line/algebraic-cover obstruction follows. The final
simultaneous half-line selector implication remains a separate open claim.
No contradiction to qualitative finite SDP-lift existence or to a finite
algebraic testing family follows, and no global affine pencil is obtained.
