Why the available lemmas do not supply cancellation
--------------------------------------------------
TF3 cancels only a proven nonzero s tensor t with s,t positive in their
own fields. det(H)=sum_{i<j<k} kappa_i kappa_j kappa_k det(r_i,r_j,r_k)^2
is generally a SUM of such source/testing products, not one pure product.
The weighted certificate places F in an SOS cone after adjoining the
mixed inverse of det(H); that localized conclusion is not TF12.

TF1 says that a bounded element with positive real residue is SOS.
It neither makes it a unit nor supplies its inverse in T. TF2 requires
an actual unmultiplied Gram with strict residue; it cannot be applied
to a quadratic form divided by det(H). Positive residue of a normalized
determinant consequently does not fill this step.

The small-cost helper requires an exact identity A(z,H)-H²=SOS with
infinitesimal quadratic costs on an already strict root frame. The
weighted identity does not supply such an identity for unweighted state
normals. The complementary-source lemma needs the explicit conductor
sqrt(b) ell Gamma=sum c_i z_i with zero-residue coefficients and a+b a
positive source unit. No such conductor follows from adjugate completion.

CC3 is a restricted polynomial IDEAL MEMBERSHIP result, not SOS
saturation by an arbitrary multiplier. To use it here one must first
put the unmultiplied residual F-Pi in its specified chart, prove each
first state-normal coefficient is in M intersection J, and prove the
scalar coefficient is in (M²+(D0²)) intersection J². One must also retain
the support/positive-weight bounds making its resulting cofactors
bounded of zero residue, and a strict hard Gram. The multiplied identity
does not establish those unweighted membership/boundedness hypotheses.
These are precisely the current full-state case calculations.

Smallest additional hypothesis for a shorter route
--------------------------------------------------
Direct multiplication would suffice if (detH)^-1 existed and were SOS
in T, or if detH were a nonzero positive pure product. Neither hypothesis
is proved or generally available for the independent-field determinant.
The weaker property actually needed is Bell-specific SOS saturation:
for each canonical physical F and independent testing point, the
particular detH-weighted certificate descends to F in Sigma(T)^2,
including its zero faces. No current lemma states that property.

For a conductor-based proof the first concrete missing datum is a
full-state positive-event subtraction Pi and the UNWEIGHTED contact
module memberships/cofactor bounds just listed. It is not sufficient
to establish those statements after multiplying them by detH.

Concrete source/testing zero type
---------------------------------
Take any physical C1 source with d>0,lambda_ev=n=0 and alpha>0, and
the literal original commuting test u=v=0. In the W,X,Y transverse block,
 H=diag(0,4(m+beta)+2d,4(C+A+D)+2d),
so detH=0. Yet on the state psi=(0,0,0,1), F=4alpha>0.
The weighted identity is identically0 at this test and does not contain
that unweighted state direction. Its root ideal alone therefore cannot
contain F: every weighted root vanishes at this real point, while F
does not. Positive-event restoration before a conductor argument is
essential. A fully numerical trivial example is d=0,alpha=1,all other
weights0: detH is identically0 and F=4Du(vY-Z)^2 is a nonzero event square.
These examples do not challenge validity of F; they show why a
weighted-root conductor cannot be invoked without its extra hypotheses.

Near the C1 commuting corner, detH can instead be nonzero with zero
residue and competing source/testing scales. Pure-factor cancellation
cannot discard its zero opening, and positive-residue inversion cannot
be inferred after normalization. These are the source/test types that
the existing C1/C2 state-normal conductors distinguish.
