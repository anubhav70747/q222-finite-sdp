BALANCED DETERMINANTS AND THE FULL PARENT: THE ADJUGATE DENOMINATOR
24 October 2026

Conclusion. SG3 and the finite SG5 circuit list give an exact fixed-family
SOS for a SOURCE-DEPENDENT-WEIGHTED parent. The direct adjugate/Schur
construction does not remove that weight. Its precise remaining factor
is sqrt(det A_c(u,v)), where A_c is the positive three-dimensional state
block. It is neither a pure source factor nor one fixed pure testing
factor. A source-dependent Gram cancellation or a different normal
allocation is still required; this is not a no-go theorem for those routes.

Locators: latex/chapters/04_geometry.tex, SG2--SG6 and 'The twelve elementary
angular identities'; c2_full_contact_congruence.txt, first state map and
the displayed full normal polynomial. No public files were edited.

1. Exact identity and what the determinant circuits do provide

In the notation of SG2 write the full state matrix and polynomial as
 P_c(t)=[[A_c(t),b_c(t)],[b_c(t)^T,e_c(t)]],
 F_c(t;xi,Z)=xi^T A_c xi+2Z b_c^T xi+e_c Z^2,
 t=(u,v), xi=(W,X,Y), D_c(t)=det A_c(t).
For d>0 and uv!=0, SG3 proves A_c positive definite, hence D_c>0.
A direct polynomial identity, with no inverse, is
 D_c F_c=(A_c xi+b_c Z)^T adj(A_c)(A_c xi+b_c Z)
                            +det(P_c) Z^2.                       (1)
Indeed A adj(A)=D I and det(P)=D e-b^T adj(A)b.

The three-dimensional block already has a finite separated Gram.
The SG3 core is the sum of three squares; all other restricted terms
are the nonnegative event squares. Its rows have source coefficients
times fixed polynomial/testing-root rows (only fixed factors such as
sqrt(1+u^2), sqrt(1+v^2)). For a Gram A=sum_i r_i r_i^T in dimension
three, Cauchy--Binet gives the exact matrix identity
 adj(A)=sum_{i<j}(r_i cross r_j)(r_i cross r_j)^T.
Thus the first summand in (1) has a finite source-independent testing
span: products of the original fixed rows with the polynomial entries
of A and b. Source products/radicals remain in the source Gram witness.

For a stationary-contact source, SG5 and its twelve balanced circuits
likewise give det(P) a finite source-independent testing span after
expanding each circuit square. Source contact scales enter only as
source coefficients; testing roots are fixed monomial rational powers.
The factor 16(1+u^2)^2(1+v^2)^2 u^2v^2 is pure testing. Therefore (1)
really supplies a finite fixed testing family for D_c F_c.

This is not an affine certificate for F_c: D_c depends on c and is
cubic in c. The target D_c F_c is generally quartic in c. Treating
these source products as free variables does not impose their nonlinear
identities in an SDP. Dividing the SOS in (1) by sqrt(D_c) introduces
a source-and-testing-dependent factor into every root. Pure testing
projective normalization removes none of this source dependence.

2. The full contact-normal congruence retains the same factor

For the full C2 state change with T0=0, the three normal columns are
 W=N1+N2, X=u^2 v N3, Y=u N2, Z=0.
The 3-by-3 change has determinant -u^3 v. If A_N is the normal block
of the transformed F/4, then exactly
 det A_N=u^6 v^2 det(A_c/4)=x^3 w D_c/64,
 x=u^2, w=v^2.
Thus the contact-adapted congruence changes the problematic denominator
only by a fixed pure testing monomial. The facts that its normal/T0
couplings vanish at (x,w)=(z,y), and hence belong to the contact ideal,
do not imply divisibility by D_c. SG3--SG5 contain no identity canceling
this determinant from (1).

3. A valid-source family exhibits the genuinely moving denominator

Fix any globally valid d=1 C2 source with lambda=beta=0 and m>0,
for example one of the explicitly certified positive GE1 families
in c2_kernel_scalar_repair_audit.txt, Section 5. Increase C to C+tau,
tau>=0, leaving all other source coordinates fixed. This preserves
validity because the added term is the nonnegative event square.
It preserves the two zero event weights, but need NOT preserve the
native stationary-boundary parametrization. The following calculation
therefore concerns the literal full-source Schur construction; it is
not a proof against first retaining event excesses separately.

Let a(t)=(u,0,-1)^T. The three-dimensional block is
 A_tau=A_0+4tau(1+v^2) a a^T,
 D_tau=D_0+tau D_1,
 D_1=4(1+v^2) a^T adj(A_0) a.
This exact denominator does not factor as a source scalar times one
fixed testing function. To see it inside the C2 zero-event pattern,
set v=1 and compute the logarithmic slope in C:
 at u=0, D_1/D_0=1/[C+A+(D+alpha+1)/2];
 as u tends to infinity,
 D_1/D_0 ->1/[C+n(D+1/2)/(2n+D+1/2)].               (2)
For the first formula the u=0 normal block is the direct sum of
 m(W-X)^2+(2n+1)X^2 and
 [2(C+A)+D+alpha+1]Y^2, in F/4 units.
For the second, its leading u^2 block is
 [[2C+D+1/2,D+1/2,0],
  [D+1/2,2n+D+1/2,0],
  [0,0,m+2A+alpha+1/2]].
These two computations prove (2) directly.

The limits differ strictly:
 n(D+1/2)/(2n+D+1/2)<D/2+1/4,
whereas A+(D+alpha+1)/2>=D/2+1/2.
Hence h(u)=D_1(u,1)/D_0(u,1) is nonconstant, even though every
source along this family is valid. The positive mixed denominator
is present on the ordinary finite-angle chart; this is not an
endpoint-zero or a vanishing-source-gap artifact.

The literal normalization functions D_tau(u,1)^(-1/2) do not lie in
one finite-dimensional pure-testing span as tau varies. Divide by the
fixed function D_0(u,1)^(-1/2), and use a local interval on which the
nonconstant rational function h(u) is a coordinate. The remaining
functions are (1+tau h)^(-1/2). For any distinct tau_i>=0, taking the
first N derivatives in h at an interior point gives a Vandermonde
matrix with distinct nodes tau_i/(1+tau_i h_0); all derivative
prefactors are nonzero. Thus any N such functions are linearly
independent. Source scalars cannot absorb this varying normalization.

4. Exact remaining implication

The direct polynomial construction stops at (1), with source-dependent
multiplier D_c. To obtain the unweighted parent and an affine pencil in
the original nine source coordinates, one still needs an exact
source-only Gram allocation canceling this mixed denominator, or a
different completion avoiding it. Merely knowing D_c>0 permits
pointwise division in one real closed field, not finite separated
division in a fixed tensor/testing library.

This does not exclude a different choice of retained events/core,
a common fixed testing multiplier with an additional proof, or a
polynomial identity exploiting all state-normal/contact equations.
The valid-source variation above in particular can be handled
differently by retaining its added event square before applying any
Schur argument. No universal impossibility of a fixed family follows.
The balanced determinant identities and positive three-dimensional
core, by themselves, supply the weighted identity and not its required
unweighted, source-affine cancellation.
