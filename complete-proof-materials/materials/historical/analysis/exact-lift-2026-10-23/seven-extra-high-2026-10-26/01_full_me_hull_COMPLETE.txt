Q222 1/7: Exact unequal-angle convexification on fixed fibers
A new exact spectrahedral subcone is available, but I cannot establish a finite SDP lift of the full three-parameter hull. The following reduction identifies exactly where the remaining convexification problem begins.
1. An exact change of variables
Set
$x=\frac{a+r}{1-r},\qquad
y=\frac{a+s}{1-s},\qquad k=xy.$
The inverse is
$r=\frac{x-a}{x+1},\qquad
s=\frac{y-a}{y+1}.$
The parameter conditions $a>0$, $0<r,s<1$, $g>0$, $B_0\le0$ become
$\boxed{
a>0,\quad k>\max\{1,a^2\},\quad
x,y>a,\quad xy=k,\quad
x+y\ge w_-(a,k)
}$
where
$w_-=\frac{k(k+a^2-2a)}{a(k-a)}.$
In particular, writing $w=x+y$,
$\boxed{w_-\le w\le w_+:=a+\frac{k}{a}.}$
To verify this, define
$J=(k-a)w+2(1-a)k,\qquad
\Phi=k(k-a^2)-aJ.$
Direct substitution gives
$g=\frac{(a+1)(k-1)}{(x+1)(y+1)},\qquad
B_0=\frac{(a+1)^3\Phi}{(x+1)^2(y+1)^2}.$
Thus $B_0\le0$ is precisely $w\ge w_-$. The upper bound $w\le w_+$ is equivalent to $x,y\ge a$ when $xy=k$.
2. Exact affine PSD cone at every fixed $(a,k)$
Fix any two real constants
$a>0,\qquad k>\max\{1,a^2\}.$
Use active coordinates $c=(d,n,m,C,A,D,\alpha)$. Define the following linear expressions in $c$:
$\begin{aligned}
B&=A+n,&M&=m+D,\\
X&=\frac{k}{k-a}\big[(a-1)A+a(k-1)D\big],\\
Y&=\frac{k}{k-a}\big[(a-1)A+a(k-1)m\big].
\end{aligned}$
Then the closed conic hull of all specified C2 sources having these fixed values of $a$ and $k=xy$ is exactly the following spectrahedral cone:
$\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
a(d+2n)=2A,\\
C+\frac d2=(k-1)D,\qquad
\alpha+\frac d2=(k-1)m,\\
(k-a^2)(A+n)=a^2(k-1)(m+D),\\[2mm]
\begin{pmatrix}
X&kA\\
kA&kY
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0,\\[2mm]
n\ge0,\qquad aA-kn\ge0.
\end{gathered}}\tag{F}$
There are no auxiliary variables. The only nontrivial PSD block has order two; the other two inequalities are scalar blocks. The four displayed equalities are linear in source coordinates because $a,k$ are fixed constants.
Proof of both inclusions. On an original generator,
$X=Ax,\qquad Y=Ay,\qquad xy=k.$
The matrix in (F) is therefore a positive rank-one matrix. The four linear identities follow from the angle law and rectangular minors; the scalar conditions follow from the parameter domain.
Conversely, suppose (F) holds. For $A>0$, let
$w=\frac{X+Y}{A}.$
The scalar conditions are exactly $w_-\le w\le w_+$, while the PSD block implies
$XY\ge kA^2,\qquad w\ge2\sqrt{k}.$
Put
$x_\pm=\frac{w\pm\sqrt{w^2-4k}}2.$
Then $x_+x_-=k$, and $x_\pm\ge a$ because $w\le w_+$. The two endpoint pairs
$(x_+,x_-),\qquad(x_-,x_+)$
are admissible source contacts, including boundary limits. They have the same $w$, and their convex combinations fill precisely the segment allowed by $XY\ge kA^2$ at that $w$. Since the four linear identities uniquely recover the remaining coefficients from $A,X,Y$, these two contact rays reconstruct $c$.
If $A=0$, the scalar inequalities force $n=0$, and the equalities and PSD block force every remaining coordinate to vanish. Thus zero and all finite singular endpoints are included. This proves (F) exactly.
3. The quartic now has a proved converse on individual rays
The rectangular minors imply, with $k=1+U/D$,
$U=(k-1)D,\qquad V=(k-1)m,$
and
$(k-a^2)B=a^2(k-1)M.$
Consequently $k>\max\{1,a^2\}$. Substituting these identities and the angle law into the quartic reduces it to
$R:=(k-a^2)A^2+a(1-a)kAM-a^2k(k-1)mD=0.$
More precisely, the original quartic residual equals
$\frac{(a+1)^2(k-1)^2M^2}{a^2(k-a^2)}R.$
The decisive relation is
$\boxed{
XY-kA^2=-\frac{k(k-1)}{(k-a)^2}R.
}$
Thus, on a fixed fiber, the quartic equality is precisely the rank-one boundary of the PSD block in (F).
There is also a single-ray converse: for positive source coefficients satisfying the angle law and minors, the quartic together with
$n\ge0,\qquad aA-kn>0$
recovers one original admissible source ray, uniquely up to scaling. Indeed, the determinant vanishes, while the two scalar bounds make $X,Y$ positive and force $x=X/A,y=Y/A$ into the admissible parameter domain. Equality in the second bound gives a zero-opening endpoint.
The additional bound is essential. For example,
$c=\left(1,38,\frac{77}{4},\frac{33}{4},
77,\frac78,192\right)$
satisfies coefficient positivity, the angle identity, all three minors and the quartic, with $a=2,k=11$. But
$aA-kn=-264<0.$
It belongs to the wrong algebraic branch, corresponding to $x=3/2<a$. This disproves the bare algebraic conditions as a characterization of individual positive contacts, without asserting that this vector is outside their convex hull.
4. A precise obstruction to imposing the quartic on mixtures
Take the supplied rational valid source at $a=2,r=1/2,s=1/16$, and average it with the valid source obtained by exchanging $r,s$. Their midpoint is
$\bar c=
\left(
1,\frac{43}{299},\frac{749}{5980},
\frac{225}{299},\frac{385}{299},
\frac{749}{5980},\frac{225}{299}
\right).$
It remains in the same $(a,k)=(2,11)$ fiber and satisfies all three rectangular minors. Nevertheless, its quartic residual is
$\boxed{
-\frac{1543042787517}{319701552040}<0.
}$
This follows directly because swapping $r,s$ exchanges $U,V$, and the midpoint changes the quartic by
$-\frac{(a+1)^2}{4}M(B+S)(U-V)^2.$
So even minors plus the quartic equality exclude legitimate convex mixtures. The PSD inequality in (F) is the exact repair on each fixed fiber.
5. The first unproved implication for the full task
Let $F_{a,k}$ denote the explicit spectrahedral cone (F). We now have the exact characterization
$\boxed{
K_{\mathrm{full}}
=
\overline{\operatorname{cone}
\left(
\bigcup_{\substack{a>0\\ k>\max\{1,a^2\}}}
F_{a,k}
\right)}.
}$
This equality includes all singular limits by closure and follows from the two-inclusion fiber theorem.
What remains unproved is that this two-parameter continuum of spectrahedral cones has one fixed finite affine PSD representation. The coefficients $a,k$ in (F) are constants within each fiber, but become variable and multiply source coordinates across the full family. Treating them as ordinary SDP auxiliaries would destroy affineness. No fixed-degree moment truncation or alternative globally affine decomposition has been established.
The strongest completed result for Task 1 is therefore the exact $2\times2$ spectrahedral convexification of every fixed $(a,k)$ fiber, together with the sharp converse for individual generators. It is not a full three-parameter lift, and no testing-region-only inequality has been promoted to a global certificate.
