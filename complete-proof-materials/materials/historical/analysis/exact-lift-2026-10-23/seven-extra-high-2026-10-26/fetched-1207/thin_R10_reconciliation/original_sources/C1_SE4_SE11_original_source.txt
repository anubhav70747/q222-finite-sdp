6117: \paragraph{A conductor that never divides by $\varepsilon$.}
6118: Define the separated monomials and differences
6119: \[
6120:  A=\varepsilon wv,\quad B=v,\quad C=Euw,\quad
6121:  D_0=\varepsilon^2j^3E,\quad k=\varepsilon j,
6122: \]
6123: \[
6124:  x=B-C,\quad y=A-kC,\quad z=uB-kC,\quad h_0=kC-D_0.
6125: \]
6126: The two standard radical pairings are obtained by dividing the original
6127: four squared circuit monomials by $\delta$: they are
6128: $A,uB,kC,D_0$, and their product is $(k^2BC)^2$.
6129: Their strict circuit is
6130: $T_4=y^2+z^2+h_0^2-2yz+2q_1^2$.
6131: The exact contact is $(u,w,v)=(k,j,\varepsilon j^2E)$.
6132: Besides the three basic conductor identities,
6133: \[
6134:  C(u-k)=z-ux,\quad
6135:  \varepsilon C(w-j)=y-\varepsilon wx,\quad
6136:  C(v-\varepsilon j^2E)=E(wz+jy-kwx),                \tag{SE5}
6137: \]
6138: use the additional, division-free identity
6139: \[
6140:  C v(w-j)=Ew(w-j)z+Ejw y-Ekw^2x.                    \tag{SE6}
6141: \]
6142: For a monic contact division
6143: $S=(u-k)T_u+(w-j)T_w+(v-\varepsilon j^2E)T_v$,
6144: whenever $T_w=\varepsilon U+vV$, the bounded conductor is
6145: \[
6146:  \begin{aligned}
6147:  CS={}&x[-uT_u-\varepsilon wU-Ekw^2V-EkwT_v]\\
6148:  &+y[U+EjwV+EjT_v]
6149:  +z[T_u+Ew(w-j)V+EwT_v].
6150:  \end{aligned}                                      \tag{SE7}
6151: \]
6152: No division by $\varepsilon,j,k,r,a,t$, or a contact difference occurs.
