95: \paragraph{Explicit monic division.}
96: Every $S\in R$ has a polynomial decomposition
97: \[
98:  S=S_c+(u-k)T_u+(w-dk)T_w+(v-dk^2b^2)T_v.                 \tag{CC1}
99: \]
100: One obtains it by substituting $u=k$, then $w=dk$, then $v=dk^2b^2$,
101: and using $X^n-Y^n=(X-Y)\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}X^{n-1-j}Y^j$ at each step.
102: There are no parameter denominators. If $S_c=0$, equations
103: \eqref{pfabc29e28ab3:CC-u}--\eqref{pfabc29e28ab3:CC-v} give
104: \[
105:  CS=x(-uT_u-wT_w-b^2kwT_v)
106:      +y(dT_w+dkb^2T_v)+z(T_u+b^2wT_v).                 \tag{CC2}
107: \]

109: \begin{lemma}[First-order and restricted second-order contact lifts]
110: In the above ring, the following assertions hold, with polynomial
111: coefficients in $R$:
112: \[
113:  M\cap J\subset (x,y,z),\qquad
114:  \bigl(M^2+(D_0^2)\bigr)\cap J^2
115:        \subset (x,y,z,h,q)^2.                         \tag{CC3}
116: \]
117: Here $(D_0^2)$ is the principal ideal generated by $D_0^2$; the second
118: assertion does not replace it by all mixed products involving $D_0$.
119: \end{lemma}
120: \begin{proof}
121: For the first assertion, write $F=R_AA+R_BB+R_CC$. Since
122: $A=y+kC$ and $B=x+C$,
123: \[
124:  F=R_Ay+R_Bx+CS,\qquad S=kR_A+R_B+R_C.
125: \]
126: The contact value is $F_c=C_cS_c$, where $C_c=d^2k^2b^2$ is a nonzero
127: polynomial. Since $R_0$ is a domain, $F_c=0$ implies $S_c=0$.
128: Apply (CC2). This cancellation proves a zero identity; it introduces no
129: coefficient $1/C_c$.
130: 
131: For the second assertion choose any displayed representation
132: $F=\sum_{i\le j}f_{ij}m_im_j+\rho D_0^2$, with
133: $(m_1,m_2,m_3)=(A,B,C)$. Substitute
134: \[
135:  A=y+kC,\qquad B=x+C,\qquad D_0=kC-h.
136: \]
137: This gives
138: \[
139:  F=Q_2+C(xH_x+yH_y+zH_z+hH_h)+C^2S,\qquad H_h=-2k\rho,       \tag{CC4}
140: \]
141: where $Q_2\in(x,y,h)^2$ and initially $H_z=0$.
142: The contact value again implies $S_c=0$. Use (CC2) on $CS$ and absorb
143: $C^2S$ into the four coefficients in (CC4). This leaves $H_h=-2k\rho$
144: unchanged and gives the same formula without $C^2S$.
145: 
146: The first derivatives of $F\in J^2$ vanish at contact. The first three
147: root gradients have determinant
148: \[
149:  \det\left(\frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(u,w,v)}\right)_c
150:        =-d^4k^4b^4\ne0.                              \tag{CC5}
151: \]
152: Equation~\eqref{pfabc29e28ab3:CC-star} gives a gradient relation with coefficient vector
153: $(-2k,1,1,-1)$. Its coefficient at $h$ is $-1$.
154: Set $\lambda=2k\rho_c$. Subtract
155: $\lambda(-2k,1,1,-1)$ from $(H_x,H_y,H_z,H_h)$.
156: The last resulting contact value is zero, and the first three satisfy the
157: homogeneous linear system with determinant (CC5). Multiplication by its
158: adjugate and cancellation in the domain $R_0$ show that those three
159: contact values are zero too. No inverse of the determinant is used in a
160: coefficient: each resulting polynomial $J_i$ lies in $J$ and is divided
161: only by the monic procedure (CC1).
162: Consequently
163: \[
164:  F=Q_2+2k\rho_cCr_*+C\sum_{i=1}^4r_iJ_i
165:    =Q_2+2\rho_c(q^2-yz)+\sum_{i=1}^4r_i(CJ_i),          \tag{CC6}
166: \]
167: where $(r_1,r_2,r_3,r_4)=(x,y,z,h)$. Each $CJ_i$ is explicitly a
168: combination of $x,y,z$ by (CC2), which proves the assertion in the
169: unlocalized polynomial ring.
170: \end{proof}
