\section{The exact physical polynomial}

Use the affine functional $H=1-(E_{00}-E_{01}+E_{10}+E_{11})/2$.
Order the eight losing events as
\[
 (\lambda,n,m,C,A,D,\alpha,\beta)
 =\bigl((++|01),(+-|00),(+-|11),(-+|10),
             (-+|00),(-+|11),(--|01),(+-|10)\bigr).
\]
Every event vanishes at the chosen PR point.  Each is one at its own
deterministic CHSH-facet neighbour and zero at the other seven.
Consequently every affine functional has the unique expansion
\[
 f=dH+4\sum_i a_i p_i,\qquad
 d=-f(\mathrm{PR}),\qquad a_i=f(v_i)/4.                 \tag{SG1}
\]
For a quantum-valid functional negative at that PR point, $d>0$ and
$a_i\geq0$.  If a quantum-valid functional is nonnegative at every PR
point, it is nonnegative on the entire nonsignalling polytope and is a
nonnegative combination of its probability inequalities.  The latter
sources have immediate separated square certificates.  Here is a complete short vertex argument. The nonsignalling affine
space has dimension eight. If no local marginal is deterministic, each
two-by-two outcome table has positive row and column sums, hence at
most two zero entries. A vertex requires at least eight active
probability inequalities. Thus every table has exactly two zeros,
necessarily in opposite corners. Write its perfect correlation or
anticorrelation as $a_x=s_{xy}b_y$, with $s_{xy}=\pm1$.
If $\prod_{x,y}s_{xy}=-1$, these equations force all four marginals
to vanish and give a PR vertex. If the product is $+1$, one marginal
remains free and the table is a mixture of two deterministic points,
so an interior marginal value is not a vertex. If instead one
marginal is deterministic, say $A_0$, a local model is obtained by
sampling $A_1$ and then sampling $B_0,B_1$ independently conditional
on $A_1$, using the two given joint tables. Nonsignalling makes their
$A_1$ marginals agree; the tables involving fixed $A_0$ are reproduced
automatically. Such a vertex is therefore deterministic. This proves
that the only vertices are the sixteen deterministic points and the
eight PR points. Linear separation (equivalently finite-dimensional
Farkas duality) now gives the asserted event representation.

Put $D_u=1+u^2$, $D_v=1+v^2$, and write the real state as
$\psi=(W,X,Y,Z)$.  The entire cleared parent is
\[
\begin{split}
 F={}&4\lambda D_u(W+vX)^2+4nD_uD_vX^2
       +4m(vW-X+uvY-uZ)^2\\
 &+4CD_v(uW-Y)^2+4AD_uD_vY^2
 +(4D+2d)(uW+uvX-Y-vZ)^2\\
 &+(4\alpha+2d)D_u(vY-Z)^2
 +(4\beta+2d)D_v(X+uZ)^2-2dD_uD_vZ^2.
\end{split}                                                   \tag{SG2}
\]
It is $D_uD_v\|\psi\|^2f(q)$ for a nonzero unnormalised state.
All these factors are pure testing factors.  Write $P(u,v)$ for its
symmetric state matrix.  If $d>0$ and $uv\ne0$, its $Z=0$ principal
block is positive definite, since the signed part alone is
\[
 2d\{(uW+uvX-Y)^2+D_uv^2Y^2+D_vX^2\}.                 \tag{SG3}
\]
Thus $P$ has at least three positive eigenvalues, its kernel at a
zero is one-dimensional with $Z\ne0$, and
\[
                 P\succeq0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\det P\geq0.
                                                               \tag{SG4}
\]

Here is the entire determinant datum needed for the geometric
reduction.  Set
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
t_1=d+2(\lambda+n+D),&t_2=d+2(\alpha+m+A),\\
t_3=d+2(\lambda+C+D),&t_4=d+2(\alpha+\beta+m),\\
t_5=d+2(\lambda+\beta+n),&t_6=d+2(\alpha+C+A),\\
t_7=d+2(\beta+n+m),&t_8=d+2(C+A+D).
\end{array}
\]
All $t_i>0$.  Directly expanding the four-dimensional determinant of
SG2, or applying Cauchy--Binet to its eight positive and one negative
rank-one packets, gives
\[
 \det P=16D_u^2D_v^2u^2v^2L(u^2,v^2),\qquad
 L(U,V)=h+\sum_{(i,j)\ne(1,1)}q_{ij}U^{i-1}V^{j-1},       \tag{SG5}
\]
where
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
q_{00}=4\lambda\alpha t_7t_8,&
q_{01}=4(\lambda Dt_7t_6+\alpha mt_5t_8),\\
q_{02}=4mDt_5t_6,&
q_{10}=4(\alpha At_7t_3+\lambda nt_8t_4),\\
q_{12}=4(\beta mt_1t_6+CDt_5t_2),&
q_{20}=4nAt_3t_4,\\
q_{21}=4(\beta nt_3t_2+CAt_1t_4),&
q_{22}=4\beta Ct_1t_2.
\end{array}                                                   \tag{SG6}
\]
The central coefficient is fixed without a long expansion by
$h=\det P(1,1)/256-\sum_{(i,j)\ne(1,1)}q_{ij}$.
The eight nonnegative outer coefficients determine the possible
contact geometries.

\section{The twelve elementary angular identities}
\label{sec:angular-circuits}

The determinant of the state matrix has eight nonconstant Laurent
monomials. Their coefficients are nonnegative. A stationary zero
therefore has a particularly simple algebraic decomposition, which
also explains the cubic and quartic identities used below.

Let $E=\{-1,0,1\}^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}$ and write
\[
 L(U,V)=h+\sum_{e\in E}q_eU^{e_1}V^{e_2},\qquad q_e\ge0.
\]
Suppose $U_c,V_c>0$ satisfy $L=L_U=L_V=0$. Put
$w_e=q_eU_c^{e_1}V_c^{e_2}$. The value and derivative equations are
exactly
\[
 h=-\sum_e w_e,\qquad \sum_e w_e e=0.
 \label{eq:angular-balance}
\]
Thus the positive coefficients balance around the origin in the
exponent plane.

\begin{lemma}[Balanced Laurent polynomial]
Let $K_c,K_t$ be labelled real closed extensions of $\mathbb R$.
If the coefficients and contact above belong to $K_c$, then for every
$U,V>0$ in $K_t$ the separated evaluation of $L$ is a finite sum of
squares in $K_c\otimes_{\mathbb R}K_t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First decompose the coefficient vector $w$ using its balance equation.
If $w\ne0$, the origin lies in the convex hull of its active exponents.
A minimal subset containing the origin has two or three elements.
For the eight exponents in $E$, the possibilities, up to rotation
and reflection of the square, are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{exponents}&\text{positive integer weights}&\text{number}\\ \hline
\{e,-e\}&(1,1)&4\\
\{(1,1),(-1,0),(0,-1)\}&(1,1,1)&4\\
\{(1,1),(-1,1),(0,-1)\}&(1,1,2)&4.
\end{array}
\]
Indeed a two-element set must be opposite. A triangle containing the
origin contains either one corner and its two opposite axial points,
or two adjacent corners and their opposite axial point. These are
the last two rows. For its integer weight vector $b$, subtract
$\lambda b$, where $\lambda=\min_{b_e>0}w_e/b_e$.
The remainder is nonnegative and balanced, and has fewer active
entries. Repeating at most eight times gives
$w=\sum_j\lambda_jb^{(j)}$ with $\lambda_j>0$ in $K_c$.

Set $X=U_c^{-1}\otimes U$ and $Y=V_c^{-1}\otimes V$.
Their rational powers mean separate positive roots in the two fields.
For each row of the table the monomials $p_i=X^{e_{i1}}Y^{e_{i2}}$
satisfy $\prod_i p_i^{b_i}=1$. The required elementary identities are
\begin{align*}
 p+p^{-1}-2&=(\sqrt p-p^{-1/2})^2,\\
 a^3+b^3+c^3-3abc
   &=\tfrac12(a+b+c)\bigl[(a-b)^2+(b-c)^2+(c-a)^2\bigr],\\
 a^4+b^4+c^4+d^4-4abcd
   &=(a^2-b^2)^2+(c^2-d^2)^2+2(ab-cd)^2.
\end{align*}
For weights $(1,1,1)$ take $a=p_1^{1/3}$, $b=p_2^{1/3}$,
$c=p_3^{1/3}$; then $abc=1$. Each of $a,b,c$ is an elementary
tensor square, so the middle line is a sum of tensor squares.
For weights $(1,1,2)$ take $a=p_1^{1/4}$, $b=p_2^{1/4}$ and
$c=d=p_3^{1/4}$; then $abcd=1$.
Finally multiply the squares by $\sqrt{\lambda_j}\otimes1$ and sum.
Equation~\eqref{eq:angular-balance} makes the result exactly $L$.
\end{proof}

In a single real closed field this proves that a stationary zero of
$L$ is a global minimum, using only the three displayed identities.
For the Bell state matrix, its positive three-dimensional principal
block then turns determinant nonnegativity into matrix positivity.
The same calculation proves a separated sum-of-squares identity for
the determinant at independent tests. To obtain a certificate for the
whole state polynomial we must also control the state-normal terms;
the following matrix lemmas perform that step.


\section{Stationary zeros and physicality}

\paragraph{Contact lemma.}
Suppose $d>0$, all event weights are nonnegative, and there exist
positive $u_c,v_c$ and a nonzero real state $\psi_c$ such that
\[
 P(u_c,v_c)\psi_c=0,
 \qquad\psi_c^TP_u(u_c,v_c)\psi_c=0,
 \qquad\psi_c^TP_v(u_c,v_c)\psi_c=0.                    \tag{SG7}
\]
Then the source is physical at every angle and every state.  This
statement holds over every real closed field.

\emph{Proof.}
SG3 gives three positive complementary state eigenvalues. The adjugate
at contact is a positive multiple of $\psi_c\psi_c^T$, so SG7 and SG5
give $L=L_U=L_V=0$ there. The balanced Laurent identity just proved
makes $L$ nonnegative at every positive pair in the same field.
Equation SG4 therefore gives physicality for $uv\ne0$.
At a commuting or projective endpoint, simultaneous diagonalization
of the commuting local pair reduces the CHSH operator to $\pm2$
times a remote reflection, so $H\succeq0$. The event terms are
nonnegative there as well. Homogenizing SG2 includes all endpoints.
This proves the claim over every real closed field.\hfill$\square$

This lemma is also the justification for deleting a positive event
whose amplitude vanishes at a contact.  Its square and all first
derivatives vanish; deleting it preserves SG7.  It is not legitimate
to delete an event whose amplitude is nonzero.

\section{The geometry of a stationary zero}

\paragraph{Coercivity at every physical finite contact.}
Every physical source with $d>0$ and a finite noncommuting zero has
coercive Newton support.  Indeed, put
$a_e=q_eU_c^{e_1}V_c^{e_2}>0$ for the active exponent vectors.
The two contact derivatives give
\[
                         \sum_e a_e e=0.                \tag{SG17}
\]
If zero is not in the interior of their convex hull, a separating
linear functional nonnegative on every active vector must vanish
on all of them, by SG17.  Thus the active support is empty or lies
on one of the four lines through the origin in the square grid.
A nonempty one-sided subset cannot satisfy SG17.  The horizontal
opposite pair $q_{01},q_{21}>0$ has four possible product pairings
in SG6.  These respectively force $\lambda n$, $CD$, $\beta m$
or $\alpha A$ positive, activating an off-line coefficient
$q_{10}$ or $q_{12}$.  The vertical pair similarly forces
$\alpha m$, $CA$, $\beta n$ or $\lambda D$ and activates
$q_{01}$ or $q_{21}$.

For the diagonal pair $q_{00},q_{22}>0$, all off-line coefficients
can vanish only on the alternating matching
$\lambda,\alpha,\beta,C>0$, $n=m=A=D=0$.
For the other diagonal they can vanish only on the opposite matching
$n,m,A,D>0$, $\lambda=C=\alpha=\beta=0$.
The latter is not physical: SG2 at $u=v=1$ and
$\psi=(1,-\varepsilon,\varepsilon,1)$ gives
\[
 F=16\varepsilon\{(d+n+m+A+D)\varepsilon-d\}<0
 \quad\text{for }\quad
 \varepsilon=\frac{d}{2(d+n+m+A+D)}.
\]
The first matching is its physical one-step cycle rotation.
If every outer coefficient is zero, SG6 forbids every adjacent or
opposite positive pair in the cycle
$(\lambda,D,C,A,\alpha,m,\beta,n)$.
Any maximal remaining set is a triple with cyclic gaps $(2,3,3)$;
smaller sets extend to such a triple.  The eight triples are rotations
of $\{\lambda,m,A\}$.  At $u=v=1$, $\psi=(1,-1,0,2)$
annihilates precisely the needed three event rows and has signed-core
value $-4$, giving $F=-4d<0$.  This excludes empty support too.

For completeness, the physical cycle symmetries used here are the
CHSH-preserving maps on event coordinates $(x,y,a,b)$
\[
\begin{split}
g_A:(x,y,a,b)&\mapsto(1-x,y,a,(-1)^yb),\\
g_B:(x,y,a,b)&\mapsto(x,1-y,(-1)^{1-x}a,b),\\
g_T:(x,y,a,b)&\mapsto(y,x,(-1)^yb,(-1)^xa).
\end{split}
\]
In the displayed cycle, indexed from zero, they act as
$i\mapsto5-i,3-i,2-i$ modulo eight.  Their compositions give
a one-step rotation and a reflection.  The assertions follow
by inserting the eight event definitions in SG1.

\paragraph{Strict single-field contact Hessian.}
The logarithmic angular Hessian is now
\[
                 \mathcal H_L=\sum_e a_e ee^T\succ0,       \tag{SG18}
\]
since the active vectors span the plane.  Write the state matrix
as $P=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}H_3&b\\b^T&c\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
in the coordinates $(W,X,Y;Z)$ and set $Z=1$.
The positive definite state block SG3 gives, within the single
source field,
\[
 F=(z+H_3^{-1}b)^TH_3(z+H_3^{-1}b)+s(u,v),
 \qquad s=\det P/\det H_3,\quad z=(W,X,Y)^T.             \tag{SG19}
\]
At contact $s=0$ and its gradient vanishes.  Its angular Hessian
is the positive scalar $16D_u^2D_v^2u^2v^2/\det H_3$ times
the congruence of SG18 by $\operatorname{diag}(2/u,2/v)$.
Consequently the full Hessian of $F$ in $(W,X,Y,u,v)$ has
positive state block $2H_3$ and a positive angular Schur complement;
it is positive definite.  All formulas for derivatives and
positive definiteness are algebraic.  The finite real assertion
therefore transfers to every real closed field; logarithms are
only notation for $U\partial_U,V\partial_V$.
The inverse in SG19 belongs to the source field.

\paragraph{Positive contact state orientation.}
Let $F_H$ denote SG2 with $d=1$ and all eight event weights zero.
Its state matrix satisfies
\[
              \det P_H=-16D_u^2D_v^2u^2v^2\ne0.          \tag{SG20}
\]
This follows by a four-dimensional determinant expansion of the
three signed-core squares and the negative $Z^2$ term in SG2.
If all positive-weight event rows vanished at a physical contact,
their state matrices would annihilate the contact state.  The
kernel equation would then give $P_H\psi_c=0$, contradicting
SG20.  Hence at least one event contributes positively.  Since
$F(\psi_c)=0$ and $d>0$, the core value $F_H(\psi_c)$ is strictly
negative.

For positive $u,v$, the $X,Y$ block of $F_H$ is
$2\left(\begin{smallmatrix}h_0&-uv\\-uv&h_0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
where $h_0=1+v^2+u^2v^2$.  Its Schur form is exactly SG15.
It is nonnegative when $WZ\le0$.  Thus the negative core value
forces
\[
                  W_cZ_c>0,\qquad Z_c=1\Longrightarrow W_c>0.
                                                               \tag{SG21}
\]

\paragraph{The forbidden-deletion implication.}
If a positive event row vanished at a contact, deleting that
coefficient would preserve the four kernel equations and both
angular derivatives.  SG7 would make the resulting nonnegative
source physical, with the same finite contact.  SG17--SG18
therefore make its support coercive.  We obtain the exact rule
\[
 \text{a positive contact event cannot vanish if deleting it
 makes the Newton support noncoercive}.                       \tag{SG22}
\]
In particular every deletion introducing an exclusive omitted pair
is forbidden by SG9.  This rule will fix the signs of the contact coordinates in
Chapter~\ref{ch:entry}.

\section{Reduction to two contact families}

The active exponent vectors in SG5 lie on the boundary of
$\{-1,0,1\}^2$.  The exponential sum is coercive exactly when zero
is in the interior of their convex hull.  One direction follows from
uniform positive support in every unit direction; the other follows
from a separating direction along which every active exponential is
nonincreasing.

For this eight-point configuration a separating direction can be
chosen from $(\pm1,0),(0,\pm1),(\pm1,\pm1)$.  For example the axis
condition in SG6 gives
$\lambda\alpha=\lambda D=\alpha m=mD=0$, whose minimal omitted
event pairs are $\{\lambda,m\}$ and $\{\alpha,D\}$.
For a diagonal the additional minimal pair is
$\{\lambda,\alpha\}$; the remaining minimal triples already
contain an axis pair.  Rotating the event cycle
\[
                 (\lambda,D,C,A,\alpha,m,\beta,n)          \tag{SG8}
\]
gives exactly the twelve pairs of exclusive events.  Therefore
\[
 \text{coercive exponent support}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{every two omitted losing events are compatible}.       \tag{SG9}
\]
Compatibility means agreement on each shared local measurement.
Pairwise compatible events extend to a single deterministic
assignment.  Such an assignment loses one or three CHSH inputs;
there are consequently at most three compatible omissions.
In SG8 compatible pairs have cyclic distance one or two, and a
compatible triple is three consecutive vertices.

\paragraph{Extreme-source lemma.}
Normalise the canonical source by $d+\sum a_i=1$ and impose
physicality and nonnegativity.  This is a compact convex subset of
an eight-dimensional simplex.  Every extreme point with $d>0$ and
coercive support has a finite stationary zero and exactly five or
six positive event weights.

\emph{Proof.}
Coercivity gives a finite minimum $\mu\geq0$ of $L$.  If $\mu>0$,
take sufficiently small opposite nonradial perturbations inside the
exact active coefficient face, preserving the normalisation.  Every
positive $q_e$ changes by relative error at most $\varepsilon$,
every zero $q_e$ remains zero, and $h$ changes by absolute error at
most $\varepsilon$.  Thus, uniformly at all positive angles,
\[
 L'\geq(1-\varepsilon)L+\varepsilon h-\varepsilon
       \geq(1-\varepsilon)\mu+\varepsilon h-\varepsilon>0.
\]
The perturbations remain physical by SG4 and the direct endpoint
argument, contradicting extremality.  The perturbation exists:
SG9 permits at most three omissions, hence at least six active
coordinates including $d$.  Thus $\mu=0$ and there is a contact.

Fix its angles and state.  SG7 consists of six homogeneous linear
equations in the source coefficients.  If at least seven events
were positive, there would be at least eight active coordinates;
their solution space has dimension at least two and contains the
source.  Intersecting its directions with coefficient sum zero
leaves a nonzero direction.  Small opposite perturbations preserve
all signs and SG7, and are physical by the contact lemma.  This again
contradicts extremality.  Hence at most six events are positive.
The lower bound five follows from SG9. \hfill$\square$

The CHSH-preserving event relabellings generate the dihedral group
of SG8.  Thus the coercive extreme sources, including every allowed
third omission, reduce to the two patterns
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathsf C_1:&\lambda=n=0,\quad d,m,C,A,\alpha>0,
                  \quad D,\beta\geq0,\quad D+\beta>0,\\
\mathsf C_2:&\lambda=\beta=0,\quad d,m,C,A,D,\alpha>0,
                  \quad n\geq0.
\end{array}                                                     \tag{SG10}
\]
The C1 third omission is $D=0$ or $\beta=0$; the C2 third omission
is $n=0$, already a C1 orbit.  This overlap is harmless.  It is not
legitimate to replace positive infinitesimal coefficients by zeros.

Every point of the normalised source body is a convex combination
of at most nine extreme points.  The fixed nine-term assertion,
the midpoint definition of extremality, SG7, and physicality all
have first-order polynomial formulations.  Their real proof
therefore transfers to a real closed coefficient field, without
using topological compactness or arbitrary infinite decompositions
in that field.  A nonnegative decomposition preserves each literal
zero coefficient term by term.

\section{Noncoercive sources have explicit lifts}

Every noncoercive source omits an exclusive pair.  Up to the same
finite symmetries there are only two possibilities:
$\lambda=m=0$ and $\lambda=\alpha=0$.

We first record the elementary quadratic matrix lemma.  A real
symmetric matrix polynomial $K(t)=K_0+tK_1+t^2K_2$ is PSD for every
real $t$ exactly when there is a skew-symmetric $S$ such that
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}K_0&K_1/2+S\\K_1/2-S&K_2\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
                                                               \tag{SG11}
\]
For completeness, the cone of Gram-representable coefficient
triples is closed because the trace of a representing PSD Gram is
$\operatorname{tr}(K_0+K_2)$.  A nonnegative dual functional has
block Hankel matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}U&V\\V&W\end{smallmatrix}\right)
\succeq0$, with $U,V,W$ symmetric.  Diagonalising
$U^{+/2}VU^{+/2}$ on $\operatorname{ran}U$ writes its moment part as
$\sum_j(z_j,t_jz_j)(z_j,t_jz_j)^T$; the remainder is
$\operatorname{diag}(0,W-VU^+V)\succeq0$.
Its value on $K$ is consequently
$\sum_jz_j^TK(t_j)z_j+\sum_iw_i^TK_2w_i\geq0$.
Separation proves SG11, including singular $U$.  The fixed-size
statement transfers to real closed fields.

For $\lambda=m=0$, use SG11 with
\[
\begin{split}
 K_0&=\operatorname{diag}(4C+4D+2d,4A,4\alpha),\\
 K_2&=\operatorname{diag}(4C,4A+4\alpha+2d,4D),\\
 K_1&=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-(4D+2d)\\
                    0&0&-(4\alpha+2d)\\
                    -(4D+2d)&-(4\alpha+2d)&0\end{pmatrix}.
\end{split}                                                     \tag{SG12}
\]
Necessity follows by setting $W=a/u$ and letting $u\to0$ in the
homogeneous state form.  The surviving form, after $R=a-Y$, is
$2t_5D_vX^2+(R,Y,Z)^TK(v)(R,Y,Z)$.
For sufficiency the determinant identity gives
\[
 \det K(v)=\frac{8D_v}{t_5}(q_{10}+hv^2+q_{12}v^4),
\]
whereas
$L=h+q_{10}/V+q_{12}V+U(q_{20}/V+q_{21}+q_{22}V)$.
PSD of $K$ makes the first three terms nonnegative; all remaining
terms are nonnegative.  SG4 and the endpoint argument finish the
equivalence.  When $d=0$ all forms are event-square sums.
The Gram is strictly feasible at
$C=A=2$, $D=\alpha=d=n=\beta=1$, $S=0$:
after permutation its two Schur margins are $125/56>0$.

For $\lambda=\alpha=0$, put $p=4C+4D+2d$,
$q=4m+4\beta+2d$, $r=4D+2d$.  The exact lift is
\[
 B_1=\begin{pmatrix}4m&-4m&\xi\\-4m&q+4n&q\\\xi&q&q\end{pmatrix}
 \succeq0,\quad
 B_2=\begin{pmatrix}p+4A&4D&-p\\
                 4D&4D&-(4m+r)-\xi\\
                 -p&-(4m+r)-\xi&p\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.          \tag{SG13}
\]
Indeed set $u=\varepsilon s$, $v=\varepsilon$,
$(W,Z)=(w,z)/\varepsilon$, $X=x$, $Y=y$.
The surviving form is
\[
\begin{split}
 G_s={}&4nx^2+4m(w-x-sz)^2+4C(sw-y)^2+4Ay^2\\
       &+r(sw-y-z)^2+(4\beta+2d)(x+sz)^2-4dyz-2dz^2.
\end{split}
\]
Its determinant is $16(q_{02}+hs^2+q_{20}s^4)$.  The same
nonnegative-remainder argument proves equivalence with physicality.
Applying SG11 to its state matrix, the zero diagonal entries on
$sx,sy$ force their Gram rows to vanish.  Coefficient matching leaves
only $\xi$, and the remaining Gram splits into the two blocks SG13.
Equivalently
$G_s=(w,x,sz)B_1(w,x,sz)^T+(y,z,sw)B_2(y,z,sw)^T$;
the $\xi$ terms cancel because $w(sz)=z(sw)$.
At $C=A=n=\beta=2$, $m=D=d=1$, $\xi=-5$, the successive
principal minors are $(4,72,234)$ and $(22,72,234)$.
Both lifts retain all nonnegative coefficient constraints.

\paragraph{Why these source lifts give separated certificates.}
Represent either source cone as $a\geq0$ and
$\exists z\,M(a,z)\succeq0$, absorbing the signs into diagonal
blocks.  The displayed points make this entire homogeneous lift
strictly feasible.  The image of its dual PSD cone is closed:
pairing with a fixed strict feasible matrix bounds the trace of
every PSD preimage of a convergent dual sequence.  Separation
therefore proves attained dual representation for every functional
nonnegative on the source cone.  This is a finite-dimensional
argument, and its fixed matrix-size quantified conclusion transfers.

Given a source in $K_c$, choose its lift $A_c\succeq0$ there.
Given a physical test in $K_t$, its evaluation functional belongs
to the dual source cone in $K_t$; choose its attained dual matrix
$B_t\succeq0$ there.  Their separated trace pairing equals the
slack because all auxiliary-coordinate coefficients cancel.
Factoring $A_c=RR^T$ and $B_t=SS^T$ in their respective fields gives
\[
 \langle A_c,B_t\rangle_{\otimes}
       =\sum_{i,j}\left(\sum_k R_{ki}\otimes S_{kj}\right)^2.
                                                               \tag{SG14}
\]
No mixed inverse or limiting certificate is involved.  Literal
zero coefficients and singular matrices are included.

Let $K_c,K_t$ be separately labelled real closed extensions of

The source partition in Chapter~\ref{ch:cover} reduces the following

\paragraph{Canonical assertion $\mathcal C$.}

\paragraph{Assembly lemma.}
Once $\mathcal C$ is proved, every transferred quantum-valid affine

\emph{Proof.}

Restore $d$ by multiplying each root by $\sqrt d\otimes1$.

\]

\]
The affine constant is preserved because $\sum_iw_i=1$.

# Reducing canonical Bell sources to two closed faces and two contact families

For $d>0$ and nonnegative canonical event weights, a finite noncommuting stationary zero of the complete Bell state matrix determines a global quantum inequality.
This makes the extreme-source classification a finite support argument. Native entry then requires proving every denominator sign.

Write $H=1-(E_{00}-E_{01}+E_{10}+E_{11})/2$. In a chosen PR orientation, the eight losing events give the unique affine expansion
$$
f=dH+4\sum_i a_ip_i,\qquad
d=-f(\mathrm{PR}),\quad a_i=f(v_i)/4,
\qquad (a_i)=(\lambda,n,m,C,A,D,\alpha,\beta).
$$
Here $v_i$ is the deterministic facet neighbour on which $p_i=1$ and the other seven losing events vanish. Quantum validity therefore gives
$a_i\geq0$. A valid slack nonnegative at every PR vertex is a nonnegative sum of probability inequalities, since the nonsignalling vertices are the
deterministic and PR behaviours. Otherwise choose a PR orientation with $d>0$. The event definitions, full parent and determinant coefficients
are given in [SG1–SG6](../latex/chapters/04_geometry.tex).

**Source reduction.** Up to physical relabellings, every canonical valid source is a finite nonnegative sum of event sources, sources on the two
closed faces $\lambda=m=0$ and $\lambda=\alpha=0$, and contact sources in the following domains:
$$
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathrm{C1}:&d,m,C,A,\alpha>0,\quad \lambda=n=0,\quad
D,\beta\geq0,\quad D+\beta>0,\\
\mathrm{C2}:&d,m,C,A,D,\alpha>0,\quad\lambda=\beta=0,\quad n\geq0.
\end{array} \tag{1}
$$
Every extreme source outside the two closed-face orbits has one of these supports and a finite noncommuting contact. Its native coordinates have
strict original gaps, including at the allowed third omissions $D=0$ or $\beta=0$ in C1 and $n=0$ in C2. Native parameters conversely
give valid extreme rays when these gaps, the stationary formulas and all original source-coefficient signs are imposed.

## A stationary contact proves global validity

Let $F_c(u,v,\psi)=\psi^TP_c(u,v)\psi$ be the complete cleared parent SG2, with $\psi=(W,X,Y,Z)$ and $D_u=1+u^2$, $D_v=1+v^2$.
It equals $D_uD_v\|\psi\|^2 f(q)$. For $d>0$ and $uv\ne0$, the
$Z=0$ principal block is positive definite: the signed core already contains
$$
2d\{(uW+uvX-Y)^2+D_uv^2Y^2+D_vX^2\}.
$$
Consequently $P_c\succeq0$ exactly when $\det P_c\geq0$, and any zero
has a one-dimensional state kernel with $Z\ne0$. The determinant is
$$
\det P_c=16D_u^2D_v^2u^2v^2L(u^2,v^2),\qquad
L(U,V)=h+\sum_{e\in\{-1,0,1\}^2\setminus\{0\}}q_eU^{e_1}V^{e_2},
\quad q_e\geq0. \tag{2}
$$
Suppose $\psi_c\ne0$, $P_c\psi_c=0$ and
$\psi_c^TP_{c,u}\psi_c=\psi_c^TP_{c,v}\psi_c=0$ at positive
$u_c,v_c$. The adjugate is a positive multiple of $\psi_c\psi_c^T$,
so Jacobi's formula gives $L=L_U=L_V=0$ there. Set $U_c=u_c^2$, $V_c=v_c^2$,
and $w_e=q_eU_c^{e_1}V_c^{e_2}$. Then
$$
h=-\sum_e w_e,\qquad \sum_e w_e e=0,\qquad
L(U,V)=\sum_e w_e\{(U/U_c)^{e_1}(V/V_c)^{e_2}-1\}. \tag{3}
$$
Weighted AM–GM makes the last expression nonnegative. Algebraically,
the balanced vector decomposes into opposite pairs and triangles with
integer weights $(1,1)$, $(1,1,1)$ or $(1,1,2)$; each subtraction
eliminates an active exponent. The quadratic, cubic and quartic identities
in SG5–SG7 prove AM–GM for these finitely many cases over a real closed field.
Thus the full state matrix is PSD at every finite nonzero test.
At commuting or projective endpoints the CHSH core is nonnegative and
each event is nonnegative; homogeneous clearing includes these endpoints.
This proves global validity from the six stationary equations.

Every physical finite contact has coercive exponent support: zero lies
in the interior of the active exponent polygon. Otherwise balance in (3)
forces all active exponents onto a line through zero. Horizontal or vertical
opposite pairs in SG6 force an off-line positive event product. The diagonal
pairs leave two alternating four-event matchings, related by a physical
cycle rotation. For the matching $n,m,A,D$, the full parent at
$u=v=1$, $\psi=(1,-\epsilon,\epsilon,1)$ is
$16\epsilon\{(d+n+m+A+D)\epsilon-d\}<0$ for sufficiently small
$\epsilon>0$. If all outer coefficients vanish, the maximal remaining
event sets are rotations of $\{\lambda,m,A\}$; the test
$\psi=(1,-1,0,2)$ at $u=v=1$ gives $F_c=-4d$. Neither case is physical.

Coercivity also gives strict contact curvature:
$\sum_e w_e ee^T\succ0$. Completing the three state coordinates in the
single source field shows that the full Hessian in $(W,X,Y,u,v)$,
with $Z=1$, is positive definite. Deleting a positive event whose
amplitude vanishes preserves all six stationary equations and hence
validity. Such a deletion cannot create noncoercive support.

## Six linear equations restrict the extreme supports

In the event cycle $(\lambda,D,C,A,\alpha,m,\beta,n)$, noncoercivity
is equivalent to omitting a pair at cyclic distance three or four.
This follows directly from SG6: a separating direction may be chosen
among the four axes and four diagonals. For example, one axis requires
$\lambda\alpha=\lambda D=\alpha m=mD=0$, whose minimal omissions
are $\{\lambda,m\}$ and $\{\alpha,D\}$; the diagonal adds
$\{\lambda,\alpha\}$. Its other minimal omission sets already contain
an axial pair. Physical rotations and reflections give the twelve pairs.
Compatible omissions have distance one or two; at most three can occur,
and a compatible triple consists of three consecutive cycle vertices.

Normalize $d+\sum_i a_i=1$. Coercivity gives a finite minimum
$\mu\geq0$ of $L$. If $\mu>0$, small opposite nonradial perturbations
inside the active source face preserve validity. Indeed, positive
$q_e$ change by at most relative $\delta$, absent ones stay zero,
and $h$ changes by at most $\delta$, so uniformly
$L'\geq(1-\delta)\mu+\delta h-\delta>0$ for small $\delta$.
An extreme source therefore has $\mu=0$ and a finite stationary contact.
At that fixed contact the four state equations and two angle equations
are homogeneous linear equations in the source coefficients. Seven positive
events would give at least eight active coordinates including $d$, hence
a nonradial normalized direction preserving all six equations.
Small opposite perturbations would remain valid by (3).
Thus an extreme coercive source has five or six positive events.
The compatible omission patterns give exactly (1) and its stated third omissions.

The normalized source body is compact and eight-dimensional, so at most
nine extreme atoms suffice. Its finite quantified decomposition and the
contact implications transfer to real closed fields; this uses no
topological compactness assertion inside a non-Archimedean field.

## The two noncoercive faces include their singular boundaries

The twelve incompatible pairs form two physical orbits, represented by
$\lambda=m=0$ and $\lambda=\alpha=0$. Their exact source pencils are
[SG11–SG13](../latex/chapters/04_geometry.tex). A homogeneous physical limit
gives a quadratic matrix polynomial in one angle. Such a polynomial
$K_0+tK_1+t^2K_2$ is PSD for all real $t$ exactly when
$$
\begin{pmatrix}K_0&K_1/2+S\\K_1/2-S&K_2\end{pmatrix}\succeq0
\quad\text{for some }S^T=-S.
$$
For the first face this is the six-dimensional pencil SG12.
For the second, coefficient matching leaves the single scalar $\xi$
and two $3\times3$ blocks $B_1,B_2$ in SG13.
Their determinant identities recover the required part of $L$;
its remaining Laurent terms are nonnegative. This proves sufficiency,
while the homogeneous limits prove necessity, including singular blocks.
The SG13 transverse-positive-block argument requires $m+A>0$.
Feasibility guarantees it: if $m=A=0$, $B_1\succeq0$ forces $\xi=0$;
then $q=(1,0,1)^T$ has $q^TB_2q=0$ but
$B_2q=(0,-2d,0)^T\ne0$, impossible for a PSD matrix.

## Native entry follows from an analytic contact graph

Over $\mathbb R$, fix $C=2$ in either strict six-event support and vary the other five
positive event weights. Physical values of $d$ form a closed interval of
positive length: at $d=0$ the central determinant coefficient contains
$16mCAD>0$. A fixed CHSH-violating test bounds $d$ above.
At its maximum $d_*$, coercivity and strict logarithmic convexity give
one squared-angle contact and one state with $Z=1$.
Its core value $F_H$ is negative: otherwise every retained event amplitude
would vanish, forcing $P_H\psi=0$, whereas $\det P_H\ne0$.
The Jacobian in $z=(W,X,Y,u,v)$ and $d$ is
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\nabla_z^2F&\partial_d\nabla_zF\\0&F_H\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
which is invertible. The analytic implicit-function theorem gives local
branches; stationarity makes each physical and maximal, and uniqueness
patches them into a connected analytic graph.

On this graph, the exact cofactors, Cramer identities, nonzero seeds and
full-parent reflections in [EN5–EN12](../latex/chapters/05_entry.tex)
fix every contact sign. A Cramer identity is first proved where its
selected minor is nonzero, then extended as a polynomial identity over
the graph: analyticity and a nonzero seed make that set dense.
Event-deletion contradictions exclude the possible equality walls.
This order avoids assuming the native denominator signs in order to prove them.
At a third omission, the same invertible Jacobian opens the missing
event coefficient at the fixed finite contact. The cofactor, deletion and
reflection identities exclude equality in the limiting inequalities;
strictness is not inferred from continuity alone.
The C2 boundary $n=0$ is already a C1 triple-omission orbit.

The resulting strict signs make every denominator in
[EN13–EN16](../latex/chapters/05_entry.tex) nonzero. The native rational
stationary formulas therefore give the unique source at that contact.
Conversely, those formulas with the original coefficient signs give
stationarity and hence global validity by (3). Their rank-six stationary
system makes any nonnegative valid decomposition proportional, proving
canonical extremality also at the allowed third omissions. The resulting finite first-order implications transfer to real closed fields.

This is a geometric source reduction. The next requirement is a certificate
for the **whole native parent** at every independently labelled source/test
pair as a sum of squares in $K_c\otimes_{\mathbb R}K_t$.
Nonnegativity of its Laurent determinant does not supply that certificate
or complete a finite SDP lift.

# A candidate exact lift: the sextic Puiseux–Gram lift

This note proposes an explicit semidefinite lift of the canonical cone `C_can`,
and hence of Q222, and records the numerical evidence for it. The lift is a
single fixed SDP: one Gram matrix in a fixed basis of fractional monomials in
the two measurement angles. It has no charts and no infinitesimals.

Its exactness is a conjecture, developed in this note. It is supported by 302
test sources, including every source that defeats the earlier candidates, by
exact certificates at the hardest sources found, and by the proved results
below.

## The lift

Let `c = (d, λ, n, m, C, A, D, α, β) ≥ 0` be a source and `F_c(u,v,ψ)` its parent
form (see `PEN_AND_PAPER.md`, step 3). For an integer `k ≥ 1`, set

    Z_k = { u^{i/k} · v^{j/k} · ψ_l  :  0 ≤ i, j ≤ k,  l = 1..4 },

and define

    L_k = { c : there is G ⪰ 0 with F_c(u,v,ψ) = Z_kᵀ G Z_k for all u, v > 0 }.

- **It is an SDP.** Distinct fractional monomials are linearly independent on
  `(0,∞)²`, so the identity is a finite set of linear equations in `(c, G)`. So
  `L_k` is the projection of a spectrahedral cone.
- **It is sound.** A Gram certificate gives `F_c ≥ 0` for `u, v > 0`. The exact
  symmetries `(u,Y,Z) → (−u,−Y,−Z)` and `(v,X,Z) → (−v,−X,−Z)` extend this to
  every quadrant, and continuity covers the axes and endpoints. Hence
  `L_k ⊆ C_can`.
- **It is nested.** `L_k ⊆ L_{k′}` whenever `k` divides `k′`.
- **Its size.** A sign symmetry splits the Gram matrix into blocks.

| k | basis | Gram blocks | free auxiliaries per copy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | {1, u}⊗{1, v}⊗ψ | 4, 4, 4, 4 | n/a |
| 2 | {1, √u, u}⊗{1, √v, v}⊗ψ | 16, 8, 8, 4 | 128 |
| 6 | sixth-root grid | 64, 48, 48, 36 | 4608 |

The full sixth-root system has 5098 symmetric Gram variables and 490
coefficient equations. On either open C1 or C2 support face, forced zero
rows reduce it to blocks of orders 52, 36, 36, 30: 3175 Gram variables,
412 equations and 2763 free auxiliaries. The smaller count applies only
after the source face has been fixed.

**Conjecture.** `L₆ = C_can`.

## What is proved

The [verified open-end construction, L12](../complete-proof-materials/CHECKLIST.md)
now gives an exact 16-feature Gram for every sufficiently small positive
epsilon=c^-2 at each strict C2 infinity-face point, uniformly on compact
interior subsets. Exact source inversion gives the corresponding zero-end
neighborhoods. Its all-orders coefficient correction and full last Schur block
are proved. Arbitrary finite native c and coupled approaches to the source
walls remain outside this result.

**Proposition 1 (soundness).** `L_k ⊆ C_can` for every k. See the bullets
above.

**Proposition 2 (closedness).** Each `L_k` is a closed convex cone.

*Proof.* Let μ be the uniform probability measure on `[½, 2]² × S³`. Distinct
fractional monomials `u^{i/k}v^{j/k}` are real-analytic functions with distinct
exponents. They are therefore linearly independent on any open subset of
`(0,∞)²`, so `M = ∫ Z Zᵀ dμ` is positive definite. If `F_c = Zᵀ G Z`, then
`Tr G ≤ Tr(G M)/λ_min(M) = ∫ F_c dμ / λ_min(M)`, and the right-hand side is
linear in `c`. Take a convergent sequence `c_n → c` with certificates `G_n`. The
`G_n` are then bounded. A subsequence converges to some `G ⪰ 0`, and the linear
identity passes to the limit. ∎

**Proposition 3 (reduction to extreme sources).** `L₆ = C_can` if and only if
`L₆` contains every extreme point of the compact base
`B = {c ∈ C_can : d + λ + n + m + C + A + D + α + β = 1}`.

*Proof.* `L₆ ⊆ C_can` is closed and convex. By Krein–Milman, `B` is the closed
convex hull of its extreme points. ∎

**Proposition 4 (events).** Every single-event source lies in `L₁ ⊆ L₆`.

*Proof.* An event term is `c_k·(h_k·ψ)²`, where `c_k` is one of
`1, D_u, D_v, D_uD_v`, and `h_k·ψ` has degree at most 1 in each of `u` and `v`.
Expanding `D_u = 1² + u²` and `D_v = 1² + v²` writes it as a sum of squares of
elements of the span of `Z₁`. ∎

By the classification of extreme sources (paper, Ch. 4), the conjecture is
therefore **equivalent** to one statement:

> **Remaining lemma.** The extreme sources of the two closed faces, and every
> C1 and C2 contact source, have a Gram certificate in `Z₆`.

This lemma is over the reals and involves no infinitesimals. The contact sources
form explicit rational families, parametrized by their contact point
(`c2_short.tex:263–292`, `c1_short.tex:696–748`). A proof could take the form
of a Gram matrix whose entries are rational functions of those parameters,
verified symbolically.

The determinant's circuit types alone do not predict which sources `L₂`
certifies (see "Why sixth roots, physically"). A construction using the
circuits would also have to recover the state matrix and its coefficients.
The numerical comparison does not rule out such a construction.

## Exact certificates at extreme sources

Four genuine boundary sources have exact rational certificates in `L₆`:
two C1 sources (C1A, C1B) and two C2 sources (C2A, C2B). Each is a contact
source from the paper's rational families, with `d = d*` and every non-forced
weight strictly positive. The contact angles are chosen to be sixth powers of
rationals, which makes the contact vector rational.

The checker ([`checks/gram-lift/boundary/`](checks/gram-lift/boundary/), 56/56
exact checks) proves three things for each source:

1. **Valid, on the boundary.** `F_c ≥ 0`, by a weighted AM–GM bound on the
   determinant plus exact positive-definite points in every quadrant, and
   `F_c` vanishes with zero gradient at the contact.
2. **Exact Gram identity.** `F_c` equals a Gram form in `Z₆` exactly, as
   Puiseux polynomials.
3. **Positive definite after reduction.** First remove the 42 monomials forced
   out by the source support face. In each remaining parity block, remove the
   contact vector `z_c`. The resulting four Gram matrices are positive definite.

At all four sources the certificate has the same structure:

- **Kernel.** In the 154-function support-face basis there is exactly one
  contact vector in each parity block. Embedded in the full 196-function
  basis, the Gram matrix also has the 42 forced null directions, so its rank
  is 150 and its kernel has dimension 46.
- **Rank.** After contact reduction, the coefficient map has 412 rows and
  exact rank 406. The six missing dimensions are the four state derivatives
  and two angle derivatives; homogeneity makes the value condition
  redundant. The exact rank calculation and its extension to all nonzero
  contacts are in [`ASSEMBLY_REFINEMENT.md`](ASSEMBLY_REFINEMENT.md).

## A continuation theorem

For a support pattern σ (C1: λ = n = 0; C2: λ = β = 0), let `Θ_σ` be the open
orthant of its six remaining weights, and for `w ∈ Θ_σ` let `c(w) = (d*(w), w)`.
`Θ_σ` is connected, and the contact `x_c(w)` depends analytically on `w`
(paper, Ch. 5). Fix the 154-function support-face basis described above.
Call a certificate for `c(w)` **nondegenerate** if its four Gram matrices
are positive definite after their contact vectors have been removed.
The reduced coefficient map has rank `412 − 6 = 406` throughout the native
charts with nonzero contact coordinates, by monomial scaling and the exact
rank calculation. This definition is relative to the support face. It
cannot use the full 196-function basis: for example `λ=0` forces the row
of the constant feature `W` to vanish before any contact is imposed.

**Lemma (openness).** If `c(w₀)` has a nondegenerate certificate, so does
`c(w)` for every `w` near `w₀`.

*Proof.* Every reduced Gram form vanishes with zero gradient at `x_c(w)`, so the
image of the reduced map lies in the space `C_w` of forms that do. The six
contact conditions are independent at `w₀` (rank `N − 6`), hence near `w₀`, so
`dim C_w = N − 6`. By lower semicontinuity the rank stays at least `N − 6`, so
it equals `N − 6` and the image is all of `C_w`. Now `F_{c(w)} ∈ C_w`, because
`c(w)` has a contact at `x_c(w)`. Choose continuous bases of the contact
complements and write the reduced coefficient map as `A(w)`. Its
constant-rank pseudoinverse corrects the existing positive definite
certificate `G₀` by
`G(w)=G₀+A(w)†(F_{c(w)}−A(w)G₀)`. The correction tends to zero at `w₀`,
so each reduced Gram remains positive definite nearby. ∎

**Theorem (conditional).** Assume:

> **(ND)** For σ = C1 and σ = C2, every `w ∈ Θ_σ` that is a limit of points
> with nondegenerate certificates itself has a nondegenerate certificate.

Then every C1 and C2 contact source lies in `L₆`.

*Proof.* The set `S_σ` of `w ∈ Θ_σ` with a nondegenerate certificate is open by
the lemma, closed in `Θ_σ` by (ND), and nonempty by the seeds C1A and C2A.
Since `Θ_σ` is connected, `S_σ = Θ_σ`. The C1 and C2 sources with `D = 0`,
`β = 0` or `n = 0` are limits of these sources, so they lie in the closed cone
`L₆` too. ∎

With Propositions 1–4, **`L₆ = C_can` follows from (ND) together with the
extreme sources of the two closed faces.** The closed faces are noncoercive,
with no finite contact, so the continuation argument does not cover them.
Numerical tests support membership on 18 of 20 sampled sources; the other
two results are inconclusive at the available precision. Membership of the
closed faces in `L₆` is unnecessary for an exact hybrid lift: their existing
exact pencils can be added directly, as explained in
[`ASSEMBLY_REFINEMENT.md`](ASSEMBLY_REFINEMENT.md).

**The rank half of (ND) holds everywhere [proved].** The reduced coordinates
`p_i = z_i/z_{c,i} − z_{i0}/z_{c,i0}` are invariant under the torus rescaling
`u → u_c·u′`, `v → v_c·v′`, `ψ_l → ψ_{c,l}·ψ′_l`: the basis picks up a diagonal
factor that cancels in each ratio. C1/C2 contacts have all four amplitudes
nonzero (paper, strict sign lemma). So the reduced coefficient map at any
contact equals the map at the standard point `(1, 1; 1, 1, 1, 1)`, up to a
diagonal rescaling of coefficients. Its rank is therefore constant on each
family. In the 154-function support-face basis it is `412 − 6 = 406`, proved
exactly in [`checks/gram-lift/contact_rank.py`](checks/gram-lift/contact_rank.py)
(see [`ASSEMBLY_REFINEMENT.md`](ASSEMBLY_REFINEMENT.md)).

What (ND) still needs is strict positivity of the reduced Gram matrix.

**The forced zeros, physically.** `f(v_i) = 4c_i`, so a zero event weight means
the inequality is tight on a local deterministic strategy. Such strategies sit
at commuting angles, the corners of the Newton square. Newton pruning removes
exactly these, and the seeds show nothing else is forced besides the contact
vector.

**A fixed-cone formulation.** After the rescaling, every contact parent `F̃`
lies in one fixed space `C_std` of dimension `N − 6`. Let `K_std` be the fixed
cone of reduced Gram forms at the standard point.

- Exactness on the C1/C2 families is equivalent to `F̃ ∈ K_std` for every
  contact source.
- By continuation, it is enough that no `F̃` meets `∂K_std`.
- A boundary point needs a nonzero positive pseudo-moment `Λ` with `Λ(F̃) = 0`.
  Point evaluations inside the domain are excluded, because the contact is the
  unique zero there. Corner evaluations are removed by pruning.

So the remaining statement is a **sextic flat-extension property**: every
positive pseudo-moment on the pruned sixth-root basis that annihilates a contact
parent is the point evaluation at its contact.

**Survey of (ND).** 351 C1/C2 contact sources were tested, in bulk, near chart
walls, and along 8-decade gap scans.

- **Rank.** It is `N − 6` at every source, with a singular-value gap above
  10¹³.
- **Strict positivity.** It is certified with margin, at least 10 times the
  solver noise, on 49 of 81 C2 and 24 of 79 C1 sources. No failure was
  confirmed.
- **Walls.** At the walls `g`, `j`, `1 − zh` and `Qc`, the margin scales
  linearly with the gap. The product of the margin and the diverging weight is
  constant to about 3 digits, so this is a normalization effect, not a loss of
  positivity. At the `h` and `Lc` walls the margin stays flat down to gaps of
  10⁻⁸.
- **Treated separately.** Sources with contacts very near commuting
  measurements (`v_c ≲ 10⁻⁴`) and the walls `r → 0`, `r → 1` and `c → ∞` need
  more than double precision; the end analyses below treat them.

**A route to a complete proof (not carried out).** As a contact approaches
commuting measurements, the rescaled parent tends to a limit with one live
angle. For a single variable, a positive semidefinite matrix polynomial on a
half-line is a square-root Gram form, so these limit regimes should be provable
exactly. A complete proof would combine three pieces:

1. a rigorous interval-arithmetic covering of the compact middle region;
2. exact univariate certificates for the limit regimes;
3. uniform margins that join the two.

This is substantial work, and it is where the paper's 31,000 lines also live.

**The near-commuting end is a singular perturbation.** Along the C2 family with
Bob's contact angle `v_c = a·c → 0`, the native formulas give the following
asymptotics:

- the weights `C` and `D` grow like `a⁻²`, and `A` vanishes like `a²`;
- `n`, `m` and `α` tend to finite limits;
- the contact amplitudes `W_c` and `Y_c` grow like `a⁻¹`.

After rescaling to the contact, the leading order `a⁻⁴` of the parent is a
single square, `(C₀ + D₀)(cb)²(u′W′ − Y′)²`, where `C₀ = lim a²C` and
`D₀ = lim a²D`. It vanishes at the contact. So the limit is not a
nondegenerate one-angle problem: it collapses to rank one, and every direction
transverse to it has to be certified at lower orders in `a`.

Handling the ends of the contact families therefore needs a multi-scale
analysis. The first plan was this: near each end, find the exponents with which
the Gram entries must scale, then check that the rescaled limit problem is
strictly feasible. That is the tropical version of the companion's chart
analysis, and there are many ends: `a → 0`, `r → 0`, `r → 1`, `c → ∞` and
their combinations. The `a → 0` end imposes the following exact constraints on
such a construction.

**The `a → 0` end, analysed.** On C2, two blocks of every PSD Gram certificate
in `Z_k`, at every positive integer k, are fixed by single monomials of `F_c`:

- on the pair `(uZ, uvY)`, `B₁ = [[K, −K], [−K, K + 4A]]`, where
  `K = 4m + 4α + 2d`;
- on the pair `(uvX, vZ)`, `B₂ = [[4D + 2d + 4n, −4D], [−4D, 4D]]`.

Indeed, setting `W=X=Y=0` gives `F/Z²=4α+Ku²+4Dv²`. Its Newton polygon
forces every `Z` feature with `i+j>k` to have zero Gram row: a square containing
such a feature would have an uncancellable highest-weight monomial outside
this polygon. The coefficients of `u²vYZ` and `uv²XZ` then each have a unique
surviving feature pair. This proves the displayed blocks are fixed. The
argument uses `β=0`; with a nonzero `u²v²Z²` coefficient, the same pruning
does not follow.

On C2 their relative determinants are `ρ₁ = 4A/(K + 4A)` and
`ρ₂ = (d + 2n)/(2D + d + 2n)`. At this end `A ~ a²` and `D ~ a⁻²`, so both are
of order `a²` along the stated strict native paths. Each relative determinant
is invariant under diagonal congruence, and its block, normalized to unit
diagonal, has least eigenvalue `1−√(1−ρ_i)`. Relating this to the reduced Gram
margin also requires controlling the projection off the moving contact kernel.
A small principal minor alone does not provide that control: the projector
`I−wwᵀ/(wᵀw)`, with `w=(1,1,ε)`, has nonzero eigenvalues `1,1`, although
its first two-coordinate block has determinant `ε²/(2+ε²)`.

A numerical survey at three interior points P1–P3, with `a = 10⁻¹ … 10⁻⁸`,
reports agreement with `t(a) = 1 − √(1 − ρ₁) ≈ c·a²`, with
`c = 0.198, 0.124, 0.063`. At `a = 0` both rescaled blocks have rank exactly
one. The null vector of `B₁` is `c²b·e(u′Z′) + e(u′v′Y′)`, because
`v_cY_c/Z_c → c²b`.

This is a loss of scale, not of positivity:

- In the sheared basis `(uZ − uvY, uvY)`, `B₁` is `diag(K, 4A)` (when β = 0),
  which is positive for every `a > 0`. `B₂` has the same structure, a large
  rank-one part `4D(uvX − vZ)²` plus an O(1) remainder.
- The contact itself does not degenerate. Its Hessian keeps four positive O(1)
  eigenvalues, one eigenvalue of order `a⁻⁴`, and the exact zero.
- What degenerates is the source weights: the weight `A` of event (−+|00)
  vanishes and the weight `D` of event (−+|11) diverges. In the `d=1`
  normalization there is no finite source at `a=0`. A projective normalization
  reaches a boundary with additional zero coefficients.

`B₁` is one minor of a larger forced structure, with a physical reading. At
`u → ∞`, that is `θ = π`, Alice's two observables are opposite and so jointly
measurable. There the leading coefficient of the C2 parent is

`[u²]F = 4C(1+v²)W² + 4n(1+v²)X² + K(vY − Z)² + 4A(1+v²)Y² + (4D+2d)(W + vX)²`.

At `A = 0` it vanishes on the whole curve `W = X = 0, Z = vY`: the Bell
functional is then tight on a continuum of classical strategies, not only at the
contact. Only pairs of basis elements on the `θ = π` face contribute to `[u²]F`.
So on the span of the moment vectors of that curve, every Gram certificate
equals `4A(1+v²)` along the curve, and by Cauchy–Schwarz it is `O(A)`
throughout the span: evaluate at k+1 fixed distinct positive values of v to
obtain a basis, then bound its mixed Gram entries by their diagonal values.
For k = 6 the span is 7-dimensional (`uv^{j/6}Y`, `j = 0, …, 6`, with
`uZ` tied to `uvY`). Along the stated paths these seven directions have Gram
weight `O(a²)` in a fixed unscaled basis, for every certificate, and
`B₁` is the 2×2 minor at `j = 6`.

**Classical states are nearly tight.** On product states `ψ = W(1, v, u, uv)`,
which are `(1, u) ⊗ (1, v)` up to scale, the parent is exactly

`F = 2W²(1+u²)(1+v²)(2Au² + 2λ(1+v²) + v²(d + 2n + 2β(1+u²)))`.

On C2 this is `2W²D_uD_v(2Au² + (d+2n)v²)`, which is `O(a²)` near `v ~ a`. At
k = 4–6 the optimal dual of the margin problem is (to about 10⁻³) a point
evaluation on this product cone.

**Facial reduction, three independent attempts.** Each attempt was then checked
by a separate reproduction at another point. All three end at "unclear"; no
intrinsic obstruction was found.

- **Complete the square** (with `s = a²`, `P = uW′ − Y′` and `Q = v′(uX − Z)` in
  contact-scaled variables). Exactly,
  `F = λ₀s⁻²(P + sκQ)² + s⁻¹(4C₀v′² + c₁)P² + G₀ + O(s)`.
  - `G₀` has closed-form coefficients in `(c, b, t, r)`. On a grid it is ≥ 0,
    and its only interior zero is the contact.
  - **Forced kernel.** Seven faces of its Newton polytope have real zero
    families, which force 4 contact vectors, `B₁`, `B₂` and 5 Z-edge elements
    `e(i, 6−i, Z)`. Interior-point duals suggested 6–8 more. The complete
    kernel is given below.
  - **Transversality is proved only for `B₁` and `B₂`.** That is, the `a²`
    corrections are shown to be positive on those two directions only.
- **Exact shears.** Three exact shears remove every block fixed by single
  monomials that is `O(a²)`; the smallest remaining minor is 0.62–0.89 at
  P1–P3. The margin still decays, about like `a^{1.9}` over `a = 10⁻²…10⁻³`.
  - **Mechanism.** In the limit form with the stiff square removed, the scaled
    smallest eigenvalue falls like `v′⁻²` across a wedge of large `v′`. Only the
    `a`-dependent terms cut this off, at `v′ ~ 1/a`.
- **Boundary layer.** There is no single intermediate scale. Along `v = a^h`
  the dominant balance is `u ~ a^{−(1−h)}`, a continuum of scales. A frozen
  `a^{j/6}` ansatz shows no plateau. One positive lead: shearing every
  `(6, j, X)`–`(0, j, Z)` pair raises the margin at `a = 10⁻²` by a factor of
  about 2 (reproduced at P1–P3).

**A proposed complete kernel for the limit.** The endpoint calculation proposes
that all forced directions come from the product states
`ψ = w(1, v′, uv′)` with `Y = uW`. The reported restriction of the transformed
limit form is `2w²(1+u²)(p u² + q v′²)`, where `p, q > 0`, with Newton polygon
`hull{(0,2), (2,0), (4,0), (2,2)}`.

The argument takes any Gram certificate `G` and works in three steps:

1. Push `G` forward to the image monomials of the family. The result is
   positive semidefinite and represents that restriction.
2. So any image monomial outside half the polygon gets zero weight.
3. Hence the sum of the basis elements that map to that monomial lies in
   `ker G`.

The reported count is 57 forced directions plus the 4 contact vectors, 61 of
the 126. Verifying this count requires the transformed limit form, the explicit
monomial map and its exact rank. With these 61 directions imposed, numerical
solves report margins 0.16, 0.18 and 0.09 at P1–P3, with
solver status "Solved" and residual 10⁻¹³. The earlier figure of about 10⁻⁴,
reached by removing only 17–19 directions, was an artefact. The directions it
missed had diagonals from 10⁻⁹ to 10⁻¹.

**Proposed grading for transversality.** The candidate scaling of a forced
direction is `s^{k/6} = a^{k/3}`, for k = 1, …, 6. The proposed value of k
comes from the lower convex envelope of the order in `s` over a hexagon, with
class counts 12, 11, 10, 9, 8 and 7. A full finite-`a` construction would complete it.

- **The 7 classes of order `s` are exactly positive.** `B₁` carries `4A₁s`.
  `B₂` and `v′X` carry `s(4n₀+2)`. The five Z-edge elements lie on an edge
  whose polynomial is a positive binomial.
- **The other 50 classes enter at fractional orders,** `a^{1/3}` to `a^{5/3}`.
  This is consistent with the `a^{2/3}` rate seen earlier.
- **No sign obstruction on the product family.** There the form is strictly
  positive at finite `a`.

**Next steps at this end.** Two pieces would complete it:

- the six-level graded basis `a^{k/3}`, checked on the full finite-`a`
  problem;
- the Schur correction for the directions outside the product family.

Numerical tests cover `a > 3·10⁻³`; higher-precision solves are next.

The resolved numerical cases support (ND); the remaining cases and the full
native domain are the next targets. Closedness of `L₆` passes established
membership to finite limits; admission along this endpoint is the step the
graded construction is designed to prove. The checker
below verifies the corner identities, the projective-face identity and three
rational path asymptotics. The proposed 61-dimensional kernel and graded Schur
completion require further derivation:
[`checks/gram-lift/ends/check_a0_corners.py`](checks/gram-lift/ends/check_a0_corners.py).

So the conjecture now rests on a single nondegeneracy property of the Gram
certificates, (ND), plus the closed faces; a hybrid lift needs only (ND), since
the closed faces can use their exact pencils. (ND) asks that no accidental
degeneracy appears as one moves through each connected family of contact
sources. The four exact seeds and the 302-source sweep are consistent with it:
there, margins grow in proportion to the distance from the boundary.

If it holds, the repo's full-polar assembly (eight relabelled copies) turns it
into an explicit lift of Q222. The PSD order is 8·196 = 1568, plus 72 scalar
sign blocks. Each canonical pencil has 4608 free Gram coefficients; the
eight-copy dual assembly imposes the corresponding adjoint constraints.
A rational strictly feasible point is now explicit:
`c*=(1/21,1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1)`. Its full positive definite Gram matrix is given
in [`checks/gram-lift/strict_feasibility.json`](checks/gram-lift/strict_feasibility.json).
Strict feasibility settles this assembly hypothesis; once the contact
admission theorem is proved, it completes the lift.

## Admission across the contact families

The open step is universal contact admission, `μσ(c) ≥ 0` for every native
C1/C2 contact source (see [`EXACT_LIFT_ROUTE.md`](EXACT_LIFT_ROUTE.md)). The
ends of the charts, an adversarial search and an exact pointwise test now
give the following picture.

**Exact pointwise certificates.** [`checks/gram-lift/admission/`](checks/gram-lift/admission/)
proves `μσ > 0` at a given rational source in four steps:

1. Solve the equilibrated SDP in double precision.
2. Project the solution exactly, in rationals, onto all 412 coefficient
   equations.
3. Check that no coefficient equation has a nonzero residual.
4. Prove each of the four reduced blocks positive definite by 400-bit ball
   Cholesky.

It certifies the hardest sources found:

- the three worst C1 sources, whose double-precision margins are only
  5·10⁻¹¹ to 10⁻⁹;
- the C2 corner `b ≈ 1 − r`, down to a gap of 0.00125;
- the minimizers of 14 wall scans.

A control source with `n < 0` is rejected, and the dual side then gives an
exact separator.

**No counterexample.** The search ran 69 SDPs and about 1.2·10⁵ surrogate
samples. No source returned `μ < 0`. Every apparent negative fell into one of
two groups:

- a double-precision artefact, with source weights spanning 10¹² to 10³³;
- a dual vector that failed exact repair.

Two spots remain undecided because their weights span more than 10¹¹: C1
sources with `g ≲ 0.012`, and one C2 source near the `g` wall.

**The ends.** Each analysis was reproduced independently where marked.
[`checks/gram-lift/ends/check_c2_ends.py`](checks/gram-lift/ends/check_c2_ends.py)
checks the exact C2 facts.

| End | Exact mechanism | Status |
|---|---|---|
| C2, `r → 0` | `n = n₀/r + O(1)` and the other weights stay finite. Every coefficient containing X is exactly `O(r)`, so one uniform `√r` scale on the X features suffices | closes numerically; margin flat down to `r = 10⁻⁶` (reproduced) |
| C2, `r → 1` | `n(r=1) = −c²(h + a²)/(2(c²(a²+b+1)+1)) < 0`, so the chart ends at an `n = 0` wall first | the `n = 0` wall closes numerically (reproduced); `r → 1` is reachable only through the corner `c² = O(1 − r)`, treated with the corners |
| C2, `c → ∞` and `c → 0` | Along the torus orbit through the contact, `F_c(O(L)) = 4C(u_cv_cW_c)²(L − 1/L)² + [(4n+4m+2)X_c² + (4A+4C+4D+2)Y_c²](1 − 1/L²)²` for every C2 source. | C2, `a → 0` | forced kernel from product states, graded `a^{k/3}` (above) | finite-`a` step next |

**Covering the middle region needs parametric certificates.**

- **Per-box affine certificates** are rigorous but reach only about 1.3%
  half-width, which means 10⁹ to 10¹⁶ boxes.
- **One linear map from the source data to Gram matrices** is valid on boxes
  of about 10%. The rigorous positivity check on such a box, however,
  certifies only about 0.3–0.4%, which means 10¹¹ to 10¹⁸ boxes.

A proof over the whole continuum therefore needs certificates that are
explicit functions of the source parameters. The paper's charts are of this
kind, and so are the explicit cones of `EXACT_LIFT_ROUTE.md`.

**The frontier.** [`COMPACT_NATIVE_CONTACT_COVER.md`](COMPACT_NATIVE_CONTACT_COVER.md)
covers every compact strict native subfamily by one finite cone. Joining
finitely many closed sound cones is then routine (assembly lemma). What
remains is a finite set of fixed cones, each covering a neighbourhood of one
stratum of the singular boundary of the native domains. That boundary is
large. A first inventory of C1 (its scripts are to be added here) found:

- **Six genuine facets, proved.** They are `Lc = 0`, `M = 0`, `J = 0`,
  `Qc = 0`, `D = 0` and `β = 0`. Each one contains an exact rational point
  where every other defining inequality is strict.
- **Exact weight behaviour at the walls.** At the walls `A ~ 1/Lc`,
  `m ~ 1/M`, `D ~ 1/J` and `α ~ 1/Qc`, with the contact fixed. The coordinate
  ends send the contact to an edge or to the corner `(0,0)`.
- **About 225 candidate corner strata, numerically.** By number of meeting
  walls they split 12, 47, 77, 61, 23 and 5, at a resolution of 10⁻⁴.

C2 has a comparable structure. Along `r → 0`, two corners keep a flat margin:
with `h → 0` it stays about 0.2, and with `c → 0` about 0.05–0.08. With
`1 − zh → 0` the margin falls linearly in the gap, while `α` diverges.

**Wall mechanisms.** Three kinds of wall now reduce to a limit problem on the
wall. Each reduction has exact checks; those not listed here are to be added
to this folder.

- **C2, `r → 0`.** Rescale the X features by `ρ = √r`. The coefficient map is
  then a degree-2 polynomial in `s = cρ`, independent of the source, with
  rank 406. The right-hand side is analytic in `ρ`, so the reduced problem
  extends analytically to `ρ = 0`. Its limit is explicit:
  `F_lim = F_WYZ + 4n₀(1+u²)(1+v²)ξ²`.
  - Exact certificates prove strict feasibility of the limit at 10 face
    points, including points with `h = 0.002` and `c = 0.01`.
  - Let `T` be the set of face points where the limit is strictly feasible.
    For every compact `K ⊂ T` there is `r₀` such that every source in
    `K × (0, r₀)` is admitted.
  - The checker also proves `μ > 0` at native sources down to `r = 10⁻¹⁰`.
- **One weight → 0** (C1 `D = 0` and `β = 0`, C2 `n = 0`).
  - The contact stays finite and nondegenerate at all three walls. This is
    proved in general: `Hess_z F ≻ 0`, `F_H < 0`, and the Jacobian is
    invertible.
  - In the full 154-feature basis the wall margin is exactly 0. One Gram
    diagonal entry is fixed to `4w`, where `w` is the vanishing weight.
  - On the wall's own support face (133 features), exact certificates prove
    strict admission at 16 wall points. Near the wall the vanishing feature
    decouples, so each such point gives admission of the nearby sources with
    `w > 0`.
- **One weight blows up** (C1 `M`, `J`; C2 `1 − zh`). The naive split
  `F = w_e q_e + R` fails, because `R` keeps a nonzero gradient at the
  contact. Shift each event form by a monomial so that it vanishes exactly
  at the contact. Then `F = w_e Σ(l_i^e)² + R̃`, and `R̃` stays regular at the
  wall. `R̃₀ + μ q_e` is strictly admitted for a modest `μ` (10–30). Exact
  certificates exist at one point per wall.

- **C2, `c → ∞` and `c → 0`: the limit problem is solved on the whole open
  face.** Rescale as `c⁻²F_c(cU, cV, W, X/c, Y/c, Z)`. The limit form is
  then an exact sum of four squares with explicit positive coefficients:
  `F₀ = c_X(UVX + L_X)² + c_Y(UVY + L_Y)² + p²(VW − a²tUZ)² + q²(UW − tVZ)²`.
  It has a one-parameter `k = 2` Gram,
  `F₀ = Q_κ(UW, UVX, VZ, VW, UZ, UVY) + κ·UV(W − atZ)²`.
  An explicit LDLᵀ shows that this Gram has kernel exactly the contact iff
  `0 < κ < κ_max = 4a·p²q²/(q² + a²p²)`. Every coefficient and pivot is
  positive on the open face, so `κ = κ_max/2` works at every face point.
  The `c → 0` face has the same three-dimensional completion, with
  rescaling `c⁻²F_c(cU,cV,W,cX,cY,Z)`.
  [One short completion lemma](C2_END_GRAM_LEMMA.md) gives the positive
  pivots and exact contact kernels at both ends. The full native identities
  are checked by
  [`checks/gram-lift/ends/check_c2_c_limits.py`](checks/gram-lift/ends/check_c2_c_limits.py).
  - Exact pointwise `k = 2` certificates (hence `L₆`) exist at finite `c`:
    36 of 36 points for `c` up to 10⁸, and 22 of 24 for `c` down to 10⁻⁸.
    The two misses are double-precision failures, not infeasibility.
  - Next: the finite-`c` perturbation step. In charge-graded
    coordinates the limit is only feasible on the boundary, because the
    contact escapes. A multi-level reduction is needed.

Each reduction ends in a limit problem on a wall. At the `c` ends that limit
problem is solved on the whole open face. On the other walls it is certified
exactly at sample points, and the closed-form `c`-end method is the model
for them. The finite-`c` step, the `a → 0` end and the wall corners
are the next targets.

## Why sixth roots, physically

- **Parity.** Γ = Y⊗Y commutes with every correlator and anticommutes with every
  marginal (`checks/physics/check7_parity.py`). Correlations alone split into
  two decoupled 2×2 sectors, which is Tsirelson's elliptope. The marginals are
  exactly the coupling between the sectors.
- **Soft modes.** The CHSH operator's spectrum is 1 ± √(1 ± sin a sin b). At
  compatible measurements each sector has one soft mode, near ∓½ sin a sin b,
  and the other two modes stay gapped at eigenvalue ≥ 1. All the hard behaviour
  is a two-level problem at the scale sin a·sin b ∝ uv.
- **Scale covariance.** Rescaling the angles multiplies each fractional monomial
  by a constant. A Gram certificate in monomials therefore covers every scale
  regime at once. This is why one SDP can replace the companion's chart-by-chart
  analysis. The half-powers in both angles together supply √u·√v ∝ √(uv), the
  soft-mode amplitude. Ramification in one angle at a time misses it, which is
  why the ramified cone plus its mirror fails.
- **Hurwitz, as motivation only.** The determinant's AM–GM circuits with
  weights (1,1,1) are x⁶ + y⁶ + z⁶ − 3x²y²z² in sixth roots. Hurwitz (1891)
  showed this is a sum of squares, and circuits with weights (1,1,2) need only
  square roots. That suggested the ⅙ grid, and the same substitution turns the
  Motzkin polynomial behind the repo's polynomial obstruction into a Hurwitz form.
  **It is not the mechanism.** All 9 sources that defeat `L₂` need (1,1,1)
  circuits at their contact, but so do 252 of the 293 sources that `L₂`
  certifies (`checks/gram-lift/circuit_types.py`). Neither the contact location
  nor the spread of scales separates the two groups. Whatever distinguishes `L₂`
  from `L₆` lives in the 4×4 matrix structure, not in the scalar determinant.

## What exactness would give

**An explicit lift.** If the conjecture holds, `L₆` is an explicit,
finite-size SDP representation of Q222 with rational data.

**A short proof.** The statement "every valid source has a positive semidefinite
Gram in `Z₆`" is first-order over ℝ, so Tarski transfer carries it to every real
closed field. There, `F_c = (Lᵀ Z₆)ᵀ(Lᵀ Z₆)` with a Cholesky factor `L` over
the source field and pure test-side monomials `Z₆`, so the hard lemma of
`PEN_AND_PAPER.md` follows at once. In fact Scheiderer's criterion is no longer
needed, because the lift is explicit.

**One lemma to prove.** The whole construction rests on a single statement
over the reals, with no infinitesimals. For the extreme sources (events, the two closed
faces, and the C1/C2 contact sources), `F_c` has a Gram certificate in `Z₆`.

# Exact certificates for the remaining sources

A shorter proof can retain the bounds already obtained exactly and concentrate
on the Bell inequalities they leave. The additional construction then has a
precise job: supply the remaining supporting inequalities. This

## How the pieces combine

Let `P` be the cone of affine functions nonnegative on Q222. Let `N_k`
be a cone of sound certificates from one fixed finite NPA level, and let
`H_1,...,H_r` be further globally sound certificate cones. Suppose their
duals have finite SDP representations, with attainment established by
strict feasibility in the appropriate face or an explicit facial reduction.

**Assembly lemma.** If

`P = closure(N_k + H_1 + ... + H_r)`,

then

`Q222 = {q : (1,q) ∈ N_k* ∩ H_1* ∩ ... ∩ H_r*}`.

**Proof.** A vector is nonnegative on a sum of cones precisely when it is
nonnegative on every summand. Taking a closure leaves the dual unchanged.
The right side therefore consists of the points satisfying every quantum-valid
affine inequality, and equals Q222 by separation. Join the finite SDP blocks
using the same eight coordinates `q`; their auxiliary variables remain
independent. This gives one finite lift. ∎

The closure in the hypothesis can be omitted when the finitely many
certificate cones are closed in Bell-coefficient space. There is a direct
bound on every valid summand. Write

`f = t + b·χ`, where `χ=(A0,A1,B0,B1,A0B0,A0B1,A1B0,A1B1)`.

Average over the 16 deterministic assignments of four independent signs.
The constant and the eight distinct sign monomials are orthonormal, so

`t = E f(χ)`, and `b_i = E[χ_i f(χ)]`.

Nonnegativity at these assignments gives `|b_i|≤t`. If a sequence of sums
converges, every summand's constant lies between zero and the total constant;
all its other coefficients are therefore bounded too. A common subsequence
converges within the respective closed cones. Their sum is the desired
limit. This argument applies outside a single nonnegative canonical
coordinate orthant. Closedness of a parametrizing PSD cone alone would
not suffice: its image in Bell-coefficient space must be closed.

For a fixed NPA word list, that image is closed by a trace bound. Choose
distinct reduced words modulo the universal involution and cross-party
commutation relations. The identity coefficient of `w_i†w_j` is
`δ_ij`; hence exact coefficient matching in `W†GW=f` gives
`tr G=t`. A convergent sequence of certified slacks has bounded PSD
Grams in this one fixed space. A subsequence preserves positivity and
every coefficient equation, including the unwanted-word zeros. This
proves closedness without a numerical margin or a contact restriction.

It suffices to prove the cone equality on all extreme rays of a compact
normalized base, including their limiting faces. Every additional source
must be certified at **all** independent measurement angles and states.
The order-293 cap/Bob construction still leaves a testing strip, so it cannot

The precise **strict native domain** \(\Omega\) used here is

## 2. Full fixed global library and exact constant coefficient map

Put \(u=cU,v=acV\), \(\psi=\operatorname{diag}(b/a,-cr,c(b/a-at),1)\phi\), so that the entire contact becomes \((U,V;\phi)=(1,1;\mathbf1)\). This is invertible over the *source* field; the testing angles \(U,V>0\) and four real state coordinates \(\phi\) remain independent.

Let \(z_{ij\ell}=U^{i/6}V^{j/6}\phi_\ell\), \(0\le i,j\le6\), \(\ell\in\{W,X,Y,Z\}\). For the **strict C2 support face**, the Newton polygons force (and, for PSD Grams, necessitate)
\[
 i+j\ge6\quad\text{for }W,\qquad i+j\le6\quad\text{for }Z.
\tag{5}
\]
There are 154 retained features, split by angular sign parity into block orders \((52,36,36,30)\). Within each block choose its first lexicographic feature \(z_{q,0}\) and form all differences \(d_{q,k}=z_{q,k}-z_{q,0}\). They vanish identically at the contact for any testing/state rate. Their orders are \((51,35,35,29)\) and they contain \(3021\) independent symmetric Gram entries.

Let \(\mathscr S\) be the 412 ordered monomial keys \((I,J,\ell,m)\), \(0\le I,J\le12,\ell\le m\), arising as products of features *within a parity block*. Define the constant integer map
\[
\mathcal A:\bigoplus_{q=1}^{4}\mathbb S^{n_q-1}\to\mathbb R^{412},\qquad
\mathcal A(H)=\operatorname{coeff}_{\mathscr S}\sum_{q=1}^4 d_q^TH_qd_q.
\tag{6}
\]
For each off-diagonal Gram variable \(H_{ij},i<j\), its coefficient column is twice the polynomial product \(d_id_j\); for a diagonal variable it is \(d_i^2\). This fixes every factor of two. `c2_fixed_map.py` regenerates the complete integer columns, their ordering, and all 412 rows. `C2_154_CONTACT_MAP.json` stores every nonzero sparse coefficient; it is not a numerical approximation.

Let the six rows of \(\mathcal N\in\mathbb Z^{6\times412}\) be the four state derivatives \(\delta_{\ell,k}+\delta_{m,k}\) and two scaled angular derivatives \(I,J\), evaluated at the standard contact. Every column of (6) is killed by \(\mathcal N\). The six rows are independent. Exact modular Gaussian elimination at prime 65521 produces a **406-column, 406-row nonzero minor**, whose original row and column indices and nonzero modular pivot residues are saved in `C2_RANK406_MODULAR_PIVOTS.json` and reproducible by `verify_rank_mod65521.py`.

Therefore
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}\mathcal A=406,
\qquad \operatorname{im}\mathcal A=\ker\mathcal N.}\tag{7}
\]
The first inequality follows from the nonzero 406-minor mod 65521; the second from dimension counting and the six exact annihilators. The contact equations verified in §1 imply that the complete source-normalized coefficient vector \(f(\theta)\) belongs to \(\ker\mathcal N\) **for every strict native source**, and hence admits at least one **source-rational coefficient-matching Gram** \(\mathcal A(H)=f(\theta)\). One deterministic rational construction takes the saved nonsingular minor \(B=\mathcal A_{I,J}\), sets all unselected Gram variables to zero, and solves \(H_J=B^{-1}f_I(\theta)\). It is exact but may be indefinite; this is not a PSD admission theorem.

## 4. Exact compact dual positivity test, and the *first unresolved inequality*

For \(y\in\mathbb R^{412}\), define a linear functional \(L_y\) on the 412 monomials by their coordinates \(y_\kappa\). Its four **contact-truncated moment matrices** are
\[
 M_q(y)_{ij}=L_y(d_{q,i}d_{q,j}),\qquad
 q=1,\ldots,4.\tag{9}
\]
These are explicit integer-linear functions of \(y\), read directly from (6). Define the **source-independent compact spectrahedron**
\[
\mathcal Y=\Big\{y\in\mathbb R^{412}:
 M_q(y)\succeq0\ (q=1,\dots,4),\quad
 \mathcal N y=0,\quad \sum_q\operatorname{tr}M_q(y)=1\Big\}.
\tag{10}
\]
The gauge \(\mathcal Ny=0\) eliminates the six-dimensional kernel of \(\mathcal A^*\). If \(y\in\mathcal Y\), all four PSD matrices have trace sum one and hence bounded entries. Since \(y\mapsto\oplus_q M_q(y)\) is injective on \(\ker\mathcal N\), \(\mathcal Y\) is bounded, hence compact. It is nonempty, for example by taking a noncontact real evaluation functional, removing its \(\operatorname{im}\mathcal N^T\) component, and normalizing its positive trace.

Let
\[
\delta(\theta)=\min_{y\in\mathcal Y} L_y(f(\theta))
=\frac1{\mathscr D(\theta)}\min_{y\in\mathcal Y}
\underbrace{\sum_{\kappa\in\mathscr S}P_\kappa(\theta)y_\kappa}_{\textbf{24 explicit native polynomial terms}}.
\tag{11}
\]
The source-normalized Gram cone \(\mathcal K=\mathcal A(\bigoplus_q\mathbb S_+^{n_q-1})\) is **closed**. Indeed, integrate each difference-root Gram on a fixed product of a positive-angle compact rectangle and a real state sphere. Each difference-column is linearly independent within its parity block; therefore its integral moment matrix is positive definite. A convergent sequence of coefficient vectors bounds the traces of all PSD Gram blocks, and has a convergent Gram subsequence. Thus the limiting coefficient vector remains in \(\mathcal K\).

Finite-dimensional separation and (7) now give the exact equivalences
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
 f(\theta)\in\mathcal K &\iff \delta(\theta)\ge0,\\
 \exists\ H_q\succ0\ \forall q:\ \mathcal A(H)=f(\theta)&\iff \delta(\theta)>0,\\
 f(\theta)\notin\mathcal K&\iff\delta(\theta)<0.
\end{aligned}}\tag{12}
\]
For the strict part: if \(f=\mathcal A(H)\) and \(H_q\succ0\), then \(L_y(f)=\sum_q\operatorname{tr}(H_qM_q(y))\ge\min_q\lambda_{\min}(H_q)>0\). Conversely, let \(e=\mathcal A(I,I,I,I)\). Every \(y\in\mathcal Y\) satisfies \(L_y(e)=1\). If \(\delta(f)>0\), choose \(0<\xi<\delta(f)\); then \(L_y(f-\xi e)>0\) for all \(y\in\mathcal Y\), hence \(f-\xi e\in\mathcal K\), so \(f=\mathcal A(H'+\xi I)\) with every block positive definite. The nonstrict equivalence uses the bipolar theorem and closedness proved above. The dual normalization is therefore exact, with no hidden Slater or image-closedness assumption.

**The exact unresolved global strict-C2 admission statement is**
\[
\boxed{\forall\theta\in\Omega\quad
\forall y\in\mathcal Y:\quad
\sum_{\kappa\in\mathscr S}P_\kappa(\theta)y_\kappa\ge0.}\tag{13}
\]
This is one completely explicit finite polynomial inequality involving only the fixed moment matrices and the 24 printed source polynomials. It is **not proved** here. Proving the stronger **strict** inequality in (13) for every \(y\in\mathcal Y\) would yield a nondegenerate Gram for all strict sources, and then compact-uniform source margins. Merely proving (13) nonstrictly would suffice for their fixed-\(L_6\) admission. An exact \(y\in\mathcal Y\) with negative pairing would instead be an exclusion certificate, directly checkable by `verify_exact_certificates.py`. No such y or positive universal Gram is claimed.

This is the first unproved PSD implication. There is **no remaining affine coefficient, missing test-normal, or kernel-matching condition** in the fixed sixth-root support face.

## 5. Strict physical angular curvature does not prove (13)

For strict C2 sources, the native determinant Laurent polynomial (SG5–SG6) has exactly six positive outer coefficients \(q_{01},q_{02},q_{10},q_{12},q_{20},q_{21}\). Their exponent vectors are
\[
E=\{(-1,0),(-1,1),(0,-1),(0,1),(1,-1),(1,0)\}.
\]
Let \(w_e=q_e U_c^{e_1}V_c^{e_2}>0\) with \(U_c=c^2,V_c=a^2c^2\), and put \(z=(\log(U/U_c),\log(V/V_c))\). The contact value and log-gradient vanish, so
\[
 L(U,V)=\sum_{e\in E}w_e(e^{e\cdot z}-1-e\cdot z).
\tag{14}
\]
Write \(A_*=w_{(-1,0)}+w_{(1,0)}\), \(B_*=w_{(0,-1)}+w_{(0,1)}\) and \(C_*=w_{(-1,1)}+w_{(1,-1)}\), all strictly positive. Then
\[
 \nabla^2 L(0)=\begin{pmatrix}A_*+C_*&-C_*\\-C_*&B_*+C_*\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad\det\nabla^2 L(0)=A_*B_*+C_*(A_*+B_*)>0.
\tag{15}
\]
For \(\|z\|\le R\) the elementary integral remainder inequality yields
\[
 L(U,V)\ge\tfrac12e^{-\sqrt2R}z^T\nabla^2L(0)z.
\tag{16}
\]
These inequalities are source-uniform on compact subsets of \(\Omega\). Together with SG3's strictly positive three-state block, they prove a unique positive-angle physical contact ray and nondegenerate transverse curvature. In particular real evaluation functionals on any compact strictly positive testing-angle window have a positive lower ratio between the parent and the contact-difference norm (extended across the contact by the positive transverse Hessian).

**But (15)–(16) control physical test/state evaluations, not every \(y\in\mathcal Y\).** The latter are positive *truncated pseudo-moments* for the 154 sixth-root features and need not possess a representing measure. Their separation is exactly the extra algebraic issue in (13). It would be incorrect to substitute the scalar determinant Hessian, a physical positivity bound, or rank 406 for that inequality.

## 6. End preservation, symmetry and precise scope

The previously independently verified 16-feature finite-\(c\) theorem proves **PSD** membership for sufficiently large \(c\) near each strict \(c\to\infty\) face point, uniformly on compact interior face subsets, and for the corresponding \(c\to0\) faces by the exact rational source inversion. The original files are copied byte-for-byte under `preserved-open-end/` in this package and SHA256 checksums are recorded.

The verified source inversion is
\[
 c'=1/(ac),\quad a'=a,\quad b'=a^2/b,\quad
 r'=a^2c^2r/b,\quad t'=[1-c^2(b-a^2t)]/b.
\tag{17}
\]
It permutes the exact native weights \((n,m,C,A,D,\alpha)'=(n,D,\alpha,A,m,C)\) and the complete four-state parent by
\(u^2v^2F_{\sigma\theta}(1/v,1/u;Z,X,Y,W)=F_\theta(u,v;W,X,Y,Z)\). Since this permutation maps sixth-root indices \((i,j)\to(6-j,6-i)\), it preserves the **whole fixed** \(Z_6\) library. For interior admission one may therefore restrict to \(ac^2\ge1\), applying inversion to any source with \(ac^2<1\). This reduces the parameter range but does not prove (13).

**Warning about nondegenerate continuation.** The verified 16-feature open-end Grams, embedded by zero rows in the 154-feature support basis, are generally singular after removing the four contact vectors. Thus the open-end theorem proves \(\delta(\theta)\ge0\) there, **not automatically** \(\delta(\theta)>0\). It cannot be turned into an all-interior nondegeneracy continuation argument by openness and closedness alone.

The `L_2` strict rational exclusion in `independent-review/C2_HALF_POWER_OBSTRUCTION.md` additionally rules out a universal 16-feature or half-power certificate. No such claim is made. The entire strict native C2 family, its source walls, and the global hybrid lift remain open for **explicit** finite-degree admission until (13), or another concrete globally sound certificate, is proved. The separately reviewed qualitative spectrahedral-shadow existence assertion is not used as an explicit extraction.
