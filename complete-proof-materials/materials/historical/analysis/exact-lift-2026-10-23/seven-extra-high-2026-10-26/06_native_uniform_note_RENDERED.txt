1. Native source and hypotheses
Use source order (d,n,m,C,A,D,alpha) with lambda=beta=0, and normalize d=1.Let a,b,c,t>0, 0<r<1, z=c^2, and define
$A_c=1+zr,\quad h=b-a^2t,\quad
g=(1+z)b-(1-r+a^2zt),\quad j=1+zr-t.$$\begin{split}
\Delta={}&1+(1+z)b+z(1+z)b^2+2z(1+z)br+z^2r^2+2zr\\
&+a^2z[(1+z)b+1+zr(2-r)],\\
N={}&(1+z)(b+r)(b+a^2(1-t))-a^2(1+a^2z)(1-r)t.
\end{split}$The native conditions are
$h,g,j,1-zh,b-a^2zr>0,\quad n\ge0.$The source entries are
$\begin{aligned}
n&=\frac{(b+r)A_c(A_c+a^2zt)-r\Delta}{2r\Delta},&
m&=\frac{(1+a^2z)(1-r)A_c(A_c+a^2zt)}{2g\Delta},\\
C&=\frac{A_cN}{2a^2t\Delta},&
A&=\frac{a^2A_c(b+r)(A_c+a^2zt)}{2h\Delta},\\
D&=\frac{(1+z)(b+r)(b-a^2zr)(A_c+a^2zt)}{2a^2j\Delta},&
\alpha&=\frac{z^2(b+r)N}{2(1-zh)\Delta}.
\end{aligned}$Delta>0 and N=(1+z)(b+r)h+a^2[g+j+rt(1+a^2z)]>0; hence all remaining coefficients are positive. The exact contact is
$(u_c,v_c;\psi_c)=(c,ac;b/a,-cr,c(b/a-at),1).$Its full first-order stationarity is an exact native identity (not a testing-region assumption).
2. A 12-square scalar determinant certificate
Set $D_u=1+u^2,D_v=1+v^2$, and let $P(u,v)$ be the $4\times4$ symmetric state matrix of the complete C2 parent $F=\psi^TP\psi$:
$\begin{split}
F={}&4nD_uD_v X^2+4m(vW-X+uvY-uZ)^2\\
&+4CD_v(uW-Y)^2+4AD_uD_vY^2\\
&+(4D+2d)(uW+uvX-Y-vZ)^2\\
&+(4\alpha+2d)D_u(vY-Z)^2
+2dD_v(X+uZ)^2-2dD_uD_vZ^2.
\end{split}$The exact determinant identity (SG5–SG6) is
$\det P=16D_u^2D_v^2u^2v^2L(U,V),\quad U=u^2,\ V=v^2,$with $L=h_{\rm det}+\sum_{e\in E}q_eU^{e_1}V^{e_2}$ and six exponents
$\begin{array}{c|c}
e & q_e\\\hline
e_1=(-1,0)&4\alpha m\,t_5t_8\\
e_2=(-1,1)&4mD\,t_5t_6\\
e_3=(0,-1)&4\alpha A\,t_7t_3\\
e_4=(0,1)&4CD\,t_5t_2\\
e_5=(1,-1)&4nA\,t_3t_4\\
e_6=(1,0)&4CA\,t_1t_4
\end{array}$where
$\begin{array}{ll}
t_1=d+2(n+D),&t_2=d+2(\alpha+m+A),\\
t_3=d+2(C+D),&t_4=d+2(\alpha+m),\\
t_5=d+2n,&t_6=d+2(\alpha+C+A),\\
t_7=d+2(n+m),&t_8=d+2(C+A+D).
\end{array}$Set $U_c=c^2,V_c=a^2c^2$, $s_e=q_eU_c^{e_1}V_c^{e_2}$. Native stationarity gives exactly
$h_{\rm det}=-\sum_e s_e,\qquad
s_{e_1}-s_{e_6}=s_{e_5}-s_{e_2}=s_{e_4}-s_{e_3}=: \varepsilon.$Define $\tau_+=\max(\varepsilon,0)$, $\tau_-=\max(-\varepsilon,0)$ and
$\rho_1=s_{e_1}-\tau_+=s_{e_6}-\tau_-,
\quad \rho_2=s_{e_5}-\tau_+=s_{e_2}-\tau_-,
\quad \rho_3=s_{e_4}-\tau_+=s_{e_3}-\tau_-.$All $\rho_i,\tau_\pm\ge0$. Write $x=(U/U_c,V/V_c)$, $x^e=x_1^{e_1}x_2^{e_2}$, and $T_+=(e_1,e_4,e_5)$, $T_-=(e_2,e_3,e_6)$. Then
$\boxed{
L=\sum_{i=1}^3\rho_i(x^{e_i}+x^{-e_i}-2)
+\tau_+\left(\sum_{e\in T_+}x^e-3\right)
+\tau_-\left(\sum_{e\in T_-}x^e-3\right).
}$Only one triangular term can be active. Each pair contributes the square
$u^2v^2(x^e+x^{-e}-2)
=\left[uv(x^{e/2}-x^{-e/2})\right]^2.$For a triangle $T=(f_1,f_2,f_3)$ with $\sum f_i=0$, the cubic Hurwitz identity gives
$\boxed{
u^2v^2\left(\sum_{i=1}^3x^{f_i}-3\right)
=\frac12\sum_{h=1}^3\sum_{1\le i<j\le3}
\left[uv\,x^{f_h/6}(x^{f_i/3}-x^{f_j/3})\right]^2 .
}$The three pair squares plus nine triangle squares prove
$u^2v^2L(u^2,v^2)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{12}\ell_\ell(u,v)^2,$with vanishing square terms omitted, and each $\ell_\ell$ supported on$\{u^{i/3}v^{j/3}:0\le i,j\le6\}$.All circuit weights and contact-angle constants are source-side only. Independent testing $u,v>0$ is unrestricted.
3. Full four-state uniform polynomial identity
Let $\xi=(W,X,Y)^T$, $H=P_{\xi\xi}$, $b=P_{\xi Z}$, and $\delta_H=\det H$. The explicit column is
$\boxed{
b=
\begin{pmatrix}
-uv(4m+4D+2d)\\
u(4m+2d-4Dv^2)\\
v\,[4(D-\alpha)-(4m+4\alpha+2d)u^2]
\end{pmatrix}.
}$Here $D$ in the second entry denotes the source event weight, not $D_v$.
Define the following 19 row vectors $\varrho_i\in\mathbb R[u,v]^3$:
$\begin{array}{l}
\sqrt{2d}(u,uv,-1),\
\sqrt{2d}(0,0,v),\
\sqrt{2d}(0,0,uv),\
\sqrt{2d}(0,1,0),\
\sqrt{2d}(0,v,0),\\
2\sqrt n(0,1,0),\
2\sqrt n(0,u,0),\
2\sqrt n(0,v,0),\
2\sqrt n(0,uv,0),\\
2\sqrt m(v,-1,uv),\\
2\sqrt C(u,0,-1),\
2\sqrt C(uv,0,-v),\\
2\sqrt A(0,0,1),\
2\sqrt A(0,0,u),\
2\sqrt A(0,0,v),\
2\sqrt A(0,0,uv),\\
2\sqrt D(u,uv,-1),\\
2\sqrt\alpha(0,0,v),\
2\sqrt\alpha(0,0,uv).
\end{array}$Direct expansion of SG2 at $Z=0$ gives $H=\sum_i\varrho_i\varrho_i^T$. Consequently the 3-dimensional Cauchy–Binet / cross-product identity gives
$\operatorname{adj}H=
\sum_{1\le i<j\le19}
(\varrho_i\times\varrho_j)(\varrho_i\times\varrho_j)^T.$The $d$-core is strictly positive on $\xi\neq0$ for all $u,v>0$, so
$\delta_H\ \ge\ (2d)^3u^2v^2D_uD_v\ >0.$With $\eta=H\xi+bZ$, the fraction-free Schur identity and the scalar squares yield
$\boxed{
\begin{split}
\delta_H F={}&
\sum_{i<j}\big[(\varrho_i\times\varrho_j)\cdot\eta\big]^2\\
&+\sum_{\ell=1}^{12}
\big[4D_uD_v\,\ell_\ell(u,v) Z\big]^2.
\end{split}}$This is a uniform finite exact SOS for $\delta_HF$, with all four state variables and unrestricted independent testing $u,v>0$. Each square has bidegree at most $(4,4)$ in $u,v$, with exponents in thirds; a fixed basis of $13^2\times4=676$ monomials
$\{u^{i/3}v^{j/3}\psi_l:0\le i,j\le12,\ l=1,\dots,4\}$suffices. There are at most $\binom{19}{2}+12=183$ squares. Coefficients use only native source parameters and source-side real roots. The proof remains literal at $n=0$ because the four $n$-rows simply vanish; no limiting assumption is needed for this multiplied certificate. A general $d>0$ follows by common scaling.
Limitation: $\delta_H$ depends jointly on source and testing variables. Hence equality for $\delta_H F$ is nonlinear in the source coefficients and does not define a sound fixed affine SDP source cone for $F$. It cannot be divided away as an SOS algebraic step.
