# Verified scope and corrections for the 16:30 batch

The original reports and ZIP files are preserved unchanged. The following qualifications govern their use.

## Singular-source density

The independently reviewed theorem is conditional: if all strict real C1 and C2 sources belong to one fixed closed globally sound cone K, the permitted five-event contacts belong to K by coefficient-space density. The complete source cone is the closed finite sum of event rays and the required relabelings of K and the SG12/SG13 face cones. The argument does not put the closed-face cones inside K, and it does not prove either strict-family admission hypothesis.

At a five-event contact the full contact Hessian is positive definite. Opening the missing event and adjusting the signed-core coefficient d gives an invertible six-equation implicit-function problem. The original stationary-source theorem establishes physical validity of the perturbed source; the native inverses place it in the actual strict domain. Closedness is applied to the original Bell coefficients, so no uniform control of contact coordinates or Gram matrices near the boundary is needed. The existing extreme-source classification handles all other limiting coefficients. A finite sum of these sound closed cones is closed because deterministic-assignment averaging bounds every nonconstant Bell coefficient by the constant coefficient.

Two conventions in the recovered proof require precision. First, use J_i = partial F / partial a_i for the event derivative in the perturbation formula d'(0) = -J_i(s0)/F_H(s0). In the displayed full parent J_i is four times the corresponding raw event packet. This factor does not change the sign argument. Second, A*y >= 0 alone describes a dual cone in polynomial coefficient space. For K = {c in C : T c = A(G), G >= 0}, source-dual certificates involve z = T*y + r, with r in C*, and the existing Slater or facial-reduction conditions needed for exact dual representation. The Bell target adjoint/projection must be included when evaluating the final lift. The density and closed-sum proof does not need that shorthand.

A reflected cone can be retained as a separate finite globally sound cone without asserting that it is a monomial subcone of the original Z6.

# A contact experiment exposes its Bell source ray

This is a deduction from the source classification SG1–SG10 and the nonvanishing contact minors EN1–EN16. It does not use the long tensor-SOS atlas.

Let \(C\subset\mathbb R_+^9\) be the canonical quantum-valid source cone in the order
\((d,\lambda,n,m,C,A,D,\alpha,\beta)\).
Let \(c\in C\) be a physical C1 or C2 source with a finite noncommuting contact
\(\tau=(u_c,v_c,\psi_c)\), gauged by \(u_c,v_c>0\), \(Z_c=1\).
The two mandatory omitted coordinates are \(I=\{\lambda,n\}\) in C1 and \(I=\{\lambda,\beta\}\) in C2. Literal third omissions are allowed.

The six equations consisting of the four components of \(P_{c'}(\tau)\psi_c=0\) and the two angular stationary equations are linear in \(c'\). On the seven-dimensional subspace \(c'_i=0\) for \(i\in I\), they have rank six. For C1 use the selected columns \((d,m,A,D,\alpha,\beta)\) and EN8; for C2 use \((n,m,C,A,D,\alpha)\) and EN16. Their factors are nonzero throughout the stated contact domains, including the permitted third omissions. Hence their kernel is precisely \(\mathbb Rc\).

For \(c'\in C\), define
\[
\ell_c(c')=F_{c'}(\tau)+\sum_{i\in I}c'_i\ge0.
\]
If it vanishes, each summand vanishes. Positivity of the full state matrix gives \(P_{c'}(\tau)\psi_c=0\); a zero at an interior angular test gives both angular derivatives. The selected minor therefore forces \(c'\in\mathbb R_+c\). Thus every such contact ray is exposed in the canonical cone.

## A physical exposing correlation for the full Bell-validity cone

Let \(q_\tau\) be the normalized contact behaviour, let \(v_i,v_j\) be the deterministic CHSH-facet neighbours corresponding to the omitted events, and put
\[
w=(1+u_c^2)(1+v_c^2)\|\psi_c\|^2,\qquad
q_{\mathrm{exp}}=\frac{wq_\tau+\frac14v_i+\frac14v_j}{w+\frac12}.
\]
The identities \(F_{c'}(\tau)=wf_{c'}(q_\tau)\) and \(f_{c'}(v_k)=4c'_k\) give
\[
f_{c'}(q_{\mathrm{exp}})=\frac{\ell_c(c')}{w+\frac12}.
\]

All eight canonical losing probabilities of \(q_\tau\) are strictly positive by EN2. Its CHSH value \(S>2\): the positive retained event contribution at contact must balance \(d(1-S/2)\), with \(d>0\).

Every winning probability is also positive. In a nonsignalling extremal decomposition, the canonical PR weight is at least \((S-2)/2\), because every other vertex has this CHSH value at most two. Its winning probabilities are \(1/2\); hence \(p_{\mathrm{win}}\ge(S-2)/4>0\). Thus \(q_\tau\) has full event support.

For every other CHSH orientation \(g\), either \(S_g=-S\), or \(S+S_g\le4\); therefore \(S_g<2\). A nonzero quantum-valid affine functional vanishing at \(q_{\mathrm{exp}}\) vanishes at each of its three positively weighted constituent behaviours. It cannot be a nonnegative event combination, because \(q_\tau\) has full support, or belong to another PR sector, whose CHSH slack is strictly positive at \(q_\tau\). It belongs to the canonical cone. The two deterministic equalities impose its omitted coordinates, and the contact-minor argument makes it a positive multiple of \(f_c\). Consequently
\[
\{f\in Q_{222}^{\vee}:f(q_{\mathrm{exp}})=0\}=\mathbb R_+f_c.
\]
This proves exposure in the full cone of Bell-valid affine functionals.

The primal exposed face contains \(q_\tau,v_i,v_j\), which are affinely independent: the deterministic points have CHSH value two and the contact has a larger value. Its dimension is at least two. A third omitted event gives a third distinct deterministic vertex, hence dimension at least three. These are lower bounds. The mixture has a unique supporting Bell ray; this is not a self-testing assertion.

## Reconstruction and exact examples

With jet rows \(F,F_W,F_X,F_Y,F_u,F_v\), the fourth state-kernel equation follows by Euler homogeneity at \(Z=1\). Fixing \(C=2\) in C1 or \(d=1\) in C2, the invertible selected minor recovers the six remaining source coefficients rationally from the five contact parameters.

\mathrm{C1}&69120&103/37&107/39\\
\mathrm{C2}&184320&46/17&8/3

The selected-minor numbers use this jet convention, rather than the raw-kernel convention in EN8/EN16.

## Consequence for finite source atlases

For finitely many sound source cones \(K_j\subseteq C\), an extreme ray can lie in \(\sum_jK_j\) only if one summand contains it. Each nonzero summand in a decomposition of an extreme ray is on that ray, and cones contain all its positive multiples. Conversely, covering every extreme ray covers the compact normalized base and hence \(C\).

The exposed-ray result makes the C1/C2 contact graphs the exact test objects for a proposed finite atlas, after the closed faces and symmetry images have been included. It does not prove that the order-940 source atlas, its order-7520 outer behaviour pencil, or the sixth-root candidate has this coverage.

# L12 already admits the strict open C2 ends in the half-power library

This is a corollary of the newly published L12 theorem, not a new proof of its coefficient or PSD assertions.

Let
\[
Z_k=\{u^{i/k}v^{j/k}\psi_\ell:0\le i,j\le k,\ \ell=W,X,Y,Z\},\qquad
L_k=\{c:F_c=Z_k^TGZ_k,\ G\succeq0\}.
\]
The L12 proof uses fourteen even rows
\[
UW,\ UVX,\ VZ,\ VW,\ UZ,\ UVY,\ UVW,\ Z,\ UX,\ VX,\ UY,\ VY,\ X,\ Y
\]
and two odd rows \(\sqrt{UV}W,\sqrt{UV}Z\).
Every row has angular exponents in
\(\{0,\tfrac12,1\}^2\). Thus these sixteen functions are already a sublist of \(Z_2\), which itself embeds into \(Z_6\).

The L12 substitutions multiply each angular variable and each state coordinate by a nonzero source-only factor. A monomial row therefore becomes a nonzero source-only multiple of the same original angular/state monomial. Diagonal congruence transports its PSD Gram back into the original \(Z_2\). Its positive source-only scalar multiplier is absorbed into that Gram. No new angular denominator or fractional exponent is introduced.

Consequently L12 proves:
for fixed positive real \(a,t,\eta,\rho\) with
\[
0<\rho<t,\quad 0<t-\rho<1,\quad 0<\eta<1,\quad a^2t+\eta>1,
\]
and native parameters
\(\varepsilon=c^{-2}\), \(b=a^2t+\eta\varepsilon\), \(r=\rho\varepsilon\),
the exact full native C2 source belongs to \(L_2\) for every sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\). The statement is uniform on compact subsets of this strict face domain.

The other end also lies in \(L_2\). Under the exact parent inversion, multiplication of a reciprocal row by \(uv\) maps
\[
u^{i/2}v^{j/2}\ \longmapsto\
u^{(2-j)/2}v^{(2-i)/2},
\]
with the stated state permutation. This preserves the entire \(Z_2\) grid.

The accompanying checker verifies all sixteen exponent pairs and their inverted-grid membership exactly. It does not re-run L12's source matching, all-orders right inverse or graded Schur proof. Those remain the explicit input theorem and its published verification notes.

This does not contradict the known strict-native C2 source outside \(L_2\): it admits only the sufficiently near strict open-end neighborhoods. It gives no parameter-independent numerical cutoff, no uniform passage to source walls or coupled noncompact degenerations, and no arbitrary-finite-\(c\) or global-Q222 admission. Its useful refinement is that L12's local progress requires half-powers only; genuine sixth-root features are not used in these displayed certificates.

# Exact angular cone of total PSD order 13

This represents the nine Laurent coefficients. It does not give an affine pencil in the original Bell-source coefficients. The latter enter through quartic polynomials (SG5–SG6).

Let
\[
L(U,V)=h+\sum_{(i,j)\in\{-1,0,1\}^2\setminus\{0\}}q_{ij}U^iV^j,\qquad q_{ij}\ge0,\quad U,V>0.
\]
Write \(UVL=\sum_{a,b=0}^2g_{ab}U^aV^b\), with \(g_{11}=h\).
Set \(R=\operatorname{diag}(g_{00},g_{01},g_{02})\) and
\(S=\operatorname{diag}(g_{20},g_{21},g_{22})\).
There are thirteen auxiliary variables: \(J\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\),
\(C\in\operatorname{Sym}_2\), and \(\rho\in\mathbb R\). Define
\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}
g_{10}-2J_{00}&-J_{01}-J_{10}-C_{00}/2&\rho\\
*&h-2(J_{02}+J_{11}+J_{20})-2\rho-2C_{01}&-J_{12}-J_{21}-C_{11}/2\\
*&*&g_{12}-2J_{22}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then \(L\ge0\) on the positive quadrant, with the stated eight coefficient signs, if and only if
\[
\operatorname{diag}\left(
\begin{pmatrix}R&J\\J^T&S\end{pmatrix},B,C,[g_{10}],[g_{12}]
\right)\succeq0. \tag{A13}
\]
The orders are \((6,3,2,1,1)\), total 13. This reduces total PSD order relative to the existing order-49 angular SOCP, while using larger PSD blocks; no SOCP-size optimality is claimed.

## Coefficient identity and sufficiency

Put \(t=\sqrt V\), \(z=(1,t,t^2)^T\), \(w=(1,t)^T\), and \(M=\begin{psmallmatrix}R&J\\J^T&S\end{psmallmatrix}\). The five entries displayed for \(B\) are exactly the five coefficient equations in
\[
UVL=(z,Uz)^TM(z,Uz)+Uz^TBz+Ut\,w^TCw. \tag{1}
\]
Each right-hand term is nonnegative for \(U,t>0\). The first PSD block forces six outer signs, and the two scalar blocks force the other two.

## Necessity, including both weighted-triangle orientations

For strictly positive outer coefficients the Laurent sum is coercive in logarithmic coordinates. It has a minimizer \((U_*,V_*)>0\). With \(w_e=q_eU_*^{e_1}V_*^{e_2}\),
\(\sum_e w_e e=0\), and the minimum of \(L\) is nonnegative.
The cone of balanced nonnegative weights is generated by its rays with minimal support. A ray has at most three support points: otherwise the two balance equations have a support-preserving perturbation independent of the ray. Its minimal positive dependencies are four opposite pairs, four triangles with weights \((1,1,1)\), and four with weights \((1,1,2)\). The checker enumerates these twelve supports directly.

After normalization by \((U_*,V_*)\), it therefore suffices to certify positive monomials, a nonnegative constant, and those twelve circuits in (1), with the two diagonal subblocks of \(M\) fixed diagonal.

The opposite pairs use:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
UV(U+U^{-1}-2)=t^2(U-1)^2,&
UV(V+V^{-1}-2)=U(t^2-1)^2,\\
UV(UV+U^{-1}V^{-1}-2)=(Ut^2-1)^2,&
UV(UV^{-1}+U^{-1}V-2)=(U-t^2)^2.
\end{array}
\]
For the unweighted triangle:
\[
U^2V^2+U+V-3UV=(Ut^2-t)^2+U(t-1)^2+2Ut(t-1)^2. \tag{2}
\]
For the two inequivalent orientations of the weighted triangle:
\[
\begin{aligned}
U^2V^2+V^2+2U-4UV&=t^4(U-1)^2+2U(t^2-1)^2,\\
U^2V^2+U^2+2V-4UV&=(U-t)^2+(Ut^2-t)^2+2Ut(t-1)^2.
\end{aligned}\tag{3}
\]
The second line is essential. Directly exchanging \(U,V\) in the first line gives a square with two \(U\)-degree-one monomials, which need not respect diagonal \(S\). The alternative identity in the second line repairs that restriction.

Reflections \(U\mapsto U^{-1}\), \(V\mapsto V^{-1}\), followed by the positive monomial clearing factor, generate the four orientations of (2) and the two orientations of each line of (3). Even powers in each prefactor are absorbed into its root. The resulting roots lie in exactly:
\[
(1,t,t^2,U,Ut,Ut^2),\quad \sqrt U(1,t,t^2),\quad \sqrt{Ut}(1,t).
\]
Every root in the first block connects one \(U\)-degree-zero and one \(U\)-degree-one monomial, so its diagonal subblocks remain diagonal. Positive monomials are diagonal squares in these same blocks. Rescaling at the positive minimizer preserves all three spans. This proves necessity for positive support.

For zero outer coefficients, add \(\epsilon>0\) to all eight coefficients. Integrate (1) over \(1\le U,t\le2\). Each of the three weighted feature moment matrices is positive definite, since its feature functions are linearly independent on the rectangle. All Gram blocks are PSD, so their traces are bounded by the bounded integral of the polynomial divided by a fixed positive eigenvalue. A convergent subsequence preserves the coefficient equations and PSD. The limiting Grams prove (A13), including infima at infinity and missing support.

## Bell-source specializations

When the Bell-source orthant already enforces the eight outer signs, omit the two scalar blocks: total order 11. SG6 gives \(g_{00}=g_{20}=0\) in C1 and \(g_{00}=g_{22}=0\) in C2. PSD annihilates those rows and columns of \(M\), leaving block orders \((4,3,2)\), total nine. At a finite contact all three weighted Gram forms vanish. Each annihilates its nonzero evaluation vector, so the ranks are at most \((3,2,1)\). Thus at most six squares suffice. A third omitted event removes another diagonal row, giving total order at most eight and at most five squares.

These are determinant certificates. Substituting the quartic source map into (A13) is a polynomial matrix inequality, not an affine source lift. No state-normal completion or full-Q222 reverse inclusion follows from this reduction.

\section{The twelve elementary angular identities}
\label{sec:angular-circuits}

The determinant of the state matrix has eight nonconstant Laurent
monomials. Their coefficients are nonnegative. A stationary zero
therefore has a particularly simple algebraic decomposition, which
also explains the cubic and quartic identities used below.

Let $E=\{-1,0,1\}^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}$ and write
\[
 L(U,V)=h+\sum_{e\in E}q_eU^{e_1}V^{e_2},\qquad q_e\ge0.
\]
Suppose $U_c,V_c>0$ satisfy $L=L_U=L_V=0$. Put
$w_e=q_eU_c^{e_1}V_c^{e_2}$. The value and derivative equations are
exactly
\[
 h=-\sum_e w_e,\qquad \sum_e w_e e=0.
 \label{eq:angular-balance}
\]
Thus the positive coefficients balance around the origin in the
exponent plane.

\begin{lemma}[Balanced Laurent polynomial]
Let $K_c,K_t$ be labelled real closed extensions of $\mathbb R$.
If the coefficients and contact above belong to $K_c$, then for every
$U,V>0$ in $K_t$ the separated evaluation of $L$ is a finite sum of
squares in $K_c\otimes_{\mathbb R}K_t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First decompose the coefficient vector $w$ using its balance equation.
If $w\ne0$, the origin lies in the convex hull of its active exponents.
A minimal subset containing the origin has two or three elements.
For the eight exponents in $E$, the possibilities, up to rotation
and reflection of the square, are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{exponents}&\text{positive integer weights}&\text{number}\\ \hline
\{e,-e\}&(1,1)&4\\
\{(1,1),(-1,0),(0,-1)\}&(1,1,1)&4\\
\{(1,1),(-1,1),(0,-1)\}&(1,1,2)&4.
\end{array}
\]
Indeed a two-element set must be opposite. A triangle containing the
origin contains either one corner and its two opposite axial points,
or two adjacent corners and their opposite axial point. These are
the last two rows. For its integer weight vector $b$, subtract
$\lambda b$, where $\lambda=\min_{b_e>0}w_e/b_e$.
The remainder is nonnegative and balanced, and has fewer active
entries. Repeating at most eight times gives
$w=\sum_j\lambda_jb^{(j)}$ with $\lambda_j>0$ in $K_c$.

Set $X=U_c^{-1}\otimes U$ and $Y=V_c^{-1}\otimes V$.
Their rational powers mean separate positive roots in the two fields.
For each row of the table the monomials $p_i=X^{e_{i1}}Y^{e_{i2}}$
satisfy $\prod_i p_i^{b_i}=1$. The required elementary identities are
\begin{align*}
 p+p^{-1}-2&=(\sqrt p-p^{-1/2})^2,\\
 a^3+b^3+c^3-3abc
   &=\tfrac12(a+b+c)\bigl[(a-b)^2+(b-c)^2+(c-a)^2\bigr],\\
 a^4+b^4+c^4+d^4-4abcd
   &=(a^2-b^2)^2+(c^2-d^2)^2+2(ab-cd)^2.
\end{align*}
For weights $(1,1,1)$ take $a=p_1^{1/3}$, $b=p_2^{1/3}$,
$c=p_3^{1/3}$; then $abc=1$. Each of $a,b,c$ is an elementary
tensor square, so the middle line is a sum of tensor squares.
For weights $(1,1,2)$ take $a=p_1^{1/4}$, $b=p_2^{1/4}$ and
$c=d=p_3^{1/4}$; then $abcd=1$.
Finally multiply the squares by $\sqrt{\lambda_j}\otimes1$ and sum.
Equation~\eqref{eq:angular-balance} makes the result exactly $L$.
\end{proof}

In a single real closed field this proves that a stationary zero of
$L$ is a global minimum, using only the three displayed identities.
For the Bell state matrix, its positive three-dimensional principal
block then turns determinant nonnegativity into matrix positivity.
The same calculation proves a separated sum-of-squares identity for
the determinant at independent tests. To obtain a certificate for the
whole state polynomial we must also control the state-normal terms;
the following matrix lemmas perform that step.


\section{Stationary zeros and physicality}

\paragraph{Contact lemma.}
Suppose $d>0$, all event weights are nonnegative, and there exist
positive $u_c,v_c$ and a nonzero real state $\psi_c$ such that
\[
 P(u_c,v_c)\psi_c=0,
 \qquad\psi_c^TP_u(u_c,v_c)\psi_c=0,
 \qquad\psi_c^TP_v(u_c,v_c)\psi_c=0.                    \tag{SG7}
\]
Then the source is physical at every angle and every state.  This
statement holds over every real closed field.

\emph{Proof.}
SG3 gives three positive complementary state eigenvalues. The adjugate
at contact is a positive multiple of $\psi_c\psi_c^T$, so SG7 and SG5
give $L=L_U=L_V=0$ there. The balanced Laurent identity just proved
makes $L$ nonnegative at every positive pair in the same field.
Equation SG4 therefore gives physicality for $uv\ne0$.
At a commuting or projective endpoint, simultaneous diagonalization
of the commuting local pair reduces the CHSH operator to $\pm2$
times a remote reflection, so $H\succeq0$. The event terms are
nonnegative there as well. Homogenizing SG2 includes all endpoints.
This proves the claim over every real closed field.\hfill$\square$

This lemma is also the justification for deleting a positive event
whose amplitude vanishes at a contact.  Its square and all first
derivatives vanish; deleting it preserves SG7.  It is not legitimate
to delete an event whose amplitude is nonzero.

\section{The geometry of a stationary zero}

\paragraph{Coercivity at every physical finite contact.}

\section{Reduction to two contact families}

The active exponent vectors in SG5 lie on the boundary of
$\{-1,0,1\}^2$.  The exponential sum is coercive exactly when zero
is in the interior of their convex hull.  One direction follows from
uniform positive support in every unit direction; the other follows
from a separating direction along which every active exponential is
nonincreasing.

For this eight-point configuration a separating direction can be
chosen from $(\pm1,0),(0,\pm1),(\pm1,\pm1)$.  For example the axis
condition in SG6 gives
$\lambda\alpha=\lambda D=\alpha m=mD=0$, whose minimal omitted
event pairs are $\{\lambda,m\}$ and $\{\alpha,D\}$.
For a diagonal the additional minimal pair is
$\{\lambda,\alpha\}$; the remaining minimal triples already
contain an axis pair.  Rotating the event cycle
\[
                 (\lambda,D,C,A,\alpha,m,\beta,n)          \tag{SG8}
\]
gives exactly the twelve pairs of exclusive events.  Therefore
\[
 \text{coercive exponent support}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{every two omitted losing events are compatible}.       \tag{SG9}
\]
Compatibility means agreement on each shared local measurement.
Pairwise compatible events extend to a single deterministic
assignment.  Such an assignment loses one or three CHSH inputs;
there are consequently at most three compatible omissions.
In SG8 compatible pairs have cyclic distance one or two, and a
compatible triple is three consecutive vertices.

\paragraph{Extreme-source lemma.}
Normalise the canonical source by $d+\sum a_i=1$ and impose
physicality and nonnegativity.  This is a compact convex subset of
an eight-dimensional simplex.  Every extreme point with $d>0$ and
coercive support has a finite stationary zero and exactly five or
six positive event weights.

\emph{Proof.}
Coercivity gives a finite minimum $\mu\geq0$ of $L$.  If $\mu>0$,
take sufficiently small opposite nonradial perturbations inside the
exact active coefficient face, preserving the normalisation.  Every
positive $q_e$ changes by relative error at most $\varepsilon$,
every zero $q_e$ remains zero, and $h$ changes by absolute error at
most $\varepsilon$.  Thus, uniformly at all positive angles,
\[
 L'\geq(1-\varepsilon)L+\varepsilon h-\varepsilon
       \geq(1-\varepsilon)\mu+\varepsilon h-\varepsilon>0.
\]
The perturbations remain physical by SG4 and the direct endpoint
argument, contradicting extremality.  The perturbation exists:
SG9 permits at most three omissions, hence at least six active
coordinates including $d$.  Thus $\mu=0$ and there is a contact.

Fix its angles and state.  SG7 consists of six homogeneous linear
