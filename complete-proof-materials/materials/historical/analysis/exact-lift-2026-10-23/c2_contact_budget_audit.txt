No residue substitution, or lower bound on j,p,j-p,H,1-H, was used.

3. Combined current and the n=0 boundary

Let mu=m(1-p)>=0 and h0=mu+1/2>=1/2. RSR21/RFN14 give exactly
 h0 N0^2+n(N0+j bcur)^2=Nhat^2+Me bcur^2,
 Nhat=sqrt(n+h0)[N0+nj bcur/(n+h0)],
 Me=n h0 j^2/(n+h0).
This completes only the first correlated current; the other whole
square n v^2(N0+p bcur)^2 must still be retained in the full identity.

Let Sigma=Me+f_c/y. If n<=h0, Me>=n j^2/2. By rho^2<=rho and (2)--(3),
 p^2=j^2 rho^2 <=(5/2)j^2(n+B)
      <=5Me+(15/2)f_c/y <=8Sigma.
If n>=h0, Me>=h0 j^2/2>=j^2/4, so p^2<=j^2<=4Me.
At n=0, Me=0, Nhat=sqrt(h0)N0, Sigma=f_c/y>0.
Therefore gamma=p^2/Sigma is source-only and 0<gamma<=8,
including the literal n=0 face. No inverse of Me is required.

Consequently the first purchase reduces identically to
