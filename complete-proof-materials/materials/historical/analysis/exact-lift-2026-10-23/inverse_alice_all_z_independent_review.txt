1. Exact leading identity.
Let Q=1-b,r=bz,H0=1+zQ, q=sqrt(z), J=F-bG, B=H0/(bQ),
 C0=z/[2(1-r)], A0=H0/(2bz), alpha0=1/(2Qz).
For E=qW,F=Y/q,G=wZ/q,R=X+wW,S=pX, direct expansion gives

2z(G-F)^2+2p^2zF^2-4pEG
 +2/(1-r)(E-pzF)^2
 +2H0/b(1+p^2)F^2+2/Q(1+p^2)(F-G)^2
=2/(1-r)(E-pzJ-pG)^2+2B(1+p^2)J^2+2H0G^2.

This identity is exact for all z>0,r<1. I independently expanded both
sides; it is not an asymptotic or determinant-only identity.

2. Normalized contraction and reserve.
Write J*=sqrt(B)J,G*=sqrt(H0)G,a=z/sqrt(B),c=1/sqrt(H0),
L=aJ*+cG*=zJ+G. Exactly
 1-a^2-c^2=zQ(1-r)/H0>=0.
Thus a,c and the contraction deficit are bounded nonnegative pure-source
quantities even when z,B,H0 are unbounded. The leading square coefficient
2/(1-r) is at least2 and its excess is retained whole.

An especially useful reserve retains2p^2 J*^2, rather than clipping it.
Set g=(R,S,E,J*,G*) and
 Qclip=2R^2+2S^2+2(E-pL)^2+2(1+p^2)J*^2+2G*^2.
The exact identity is

Qclip-|g|^2/2
= (3/2)(R^2+S^2)+(3/2)(E-4pL/3)^2
 +(3/2)J*^2+(5/6)G*^2+(2/3)(1-p^2)G*^2
 +(4/3)p^2J*^2
 +(2/3)p^2[(1-a^2-c^2)(J*^2+G*^2)+(cJ*-aG*)^2].

Every term is a square with nonnegative source coefficient or testing
coefficient1-p^2. This gives a coefficient-level reserve, not merely
pointwise positive definiteness. It also avoids introducing Y sqrt(1-p^2):
only wZ sqrt(1-p^2) is needed in the fixed testing span.
