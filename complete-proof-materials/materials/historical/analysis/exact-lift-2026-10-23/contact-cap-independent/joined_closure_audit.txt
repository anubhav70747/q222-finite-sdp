Contact coefficient equations and compression
----------------------------------------------
The public parity-reduced system uses ordinary monomials in the six
variables(u,v,t,r,w,zeta); no nontrivial radical parity remains in its rows.
On the common real open set
 0<u<2^-12, 0<v<2^-25, w>0, zeta>0, t,r arbitrary,
all gates and denominator factors are positive. Within each reduced block
all feature products, multiplied by Du*Dv, are ordinary polynomials:
the common radical character squares to a fixed monomial in u,v,w*zeta,
and the common gate squares to its defining polynomial. Consequently a
coefficient identity is equivalent to the pointwise identity on this open
set: a real polynomial vanishing there is zero. This is the required
reason that compression preserves EVERY encoded equation.

For block b, put V_b=span{Phi_b(x):x in this open set}, and compress G_b
orthogonally to V_b. Its quadratic evaluations are unchanged, and it remains
PSD. Choose finitely many fixed points whose feature vectors span each V_b,
taking the union over the finitely many blocks. The evaluation matrix
M_b=sum_l Phi_b(x_l)Phi_b(x_l)^T is positive definite on V_b.
For compressed PSD matrices, tr(M_b G_b)>=mu_b tr(G_b), with mu_b>0.
Zero-dimensional blocks can simply be deleted.

If c_n converges, summing the pointwise identities at those points gives
 sum_b tr(M_b G_b,n)=sum_l S_c_n(x_l).
The right side is bounded because the target is linear in c. Every term
on the left is nonnegative, so all compressed Gram traces are uniformly
bounded. Cross-block dependencies cannot defeat this estimate. A subsequence
therefore converges to PSD blocks satisfying all coefficient equations.
