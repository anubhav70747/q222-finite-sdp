NATIVE C2: EXACT INTERVAL FIBERS, AND THE REMAINING FOUR-PARAMETER OBSTRUCTION
25 October 2026. Bounded structural derivation; no numerical optimization.

Conclusion
----------
Each fixed native shape (a,b,r,t) has an exact finite SDP description of its
closed source cone using two 3x3 moment matrices. This follows from an exact
one-interval reduction and a degree-five polynomial source curve. It does
not yet produce a single affine pencil for the full C2 cone: the four shape
parameters remain in its coefficient map nonlinearly. Treating them as free
matrix coefficients is exactly the unproved step, not an affine lifting rule.

Source: public latex/certificates/c2_short.tex:263-300, equations
eq:c2-native-source and eq:c2-native-contact. Write k=a^2 and h=b-kt.
Fix k,b,t>0, 0<r<1 and h>0. The variable in this derivation is the SOURCE
contact parameter z=c^2; it is never an independent testing angle.

1. The native source domain is one interval
------------------------------------------
The chart gaps are

 g=b+r-1+hz,   j=1-t+rz,   1-hz,   b-krz.

The denominator has a positive-coefficient form:

 Delta=(b+1)+(b+1)(b+k+2r)z
       +[(b+r)^2+k(b+r(2-r))]z^2 >0,   z>=0.

The only potentially negative source coefficient is n. Its numerator is

 q(z)=(b+r)(1+rz)(1+rz+kzt)-r Delta
     =q0+q1*z-K*z^2,
 q0=b(1-r)>0,
 q1=b(b+2r)(1-r)-(b+r)h-kr(b+1),
 K=r[(b+r)h+kb+kr(2-r)]>0.

Consequently q has exactly one positive root

 z_n=(q1+sqrt(q1^2+4Kq0))/(2K),

and n>=0 iff 0<=z<=z_n. All other source numerators are positive in the
chart, including

 N=(1+z)(b+r)h+k[g+j+rt(1+kz)]>0.

Thus, if nonempty, the entire native domain for this fixed shape is

 L<z<U,   L=max(0,(1-b-r)/h,(t-1)/r),
           U=min(1/h,b/(kr),z_n).

The endpoint z=U is included in the actual chart only when its active upper
restriction is n=0 and the other strict gaps remain positive. Closed source
cones include all finite source-ray limits at either endpoint. No disconnected
contact intervals or extra stationary-source inequalities are hidden here.

2. A uniform degree-five polynomial curve for each fixed shape
------------------------------------------------------------
Use Ac=1+rz, E=1-hz and S(z)=2 Delta*g*j*E>0 on (L,U).
Multiplying the complete source vector c(z), in order
(d,lambda,n,m,C,A,D,alpha,beta), gives the following polynomial vector P(z):

 P_d=2 Delta*g*j*E,
 P_lambda=P_beta=0,
 P_n=q*g*j*E/r,
 P_m=(1+kz)(1-r)Ac(Ac+kzt)*j*E,
 P_C=Ac*N*g*j*E/(kt),
 P_A=k*Ac(b+r)(Ac+kzt)*g*j*E/h,
 P_D=(1+z)(b+r)(b-krz)(Ac+kzt)*g*E/k,
 P_alpha=z^2(b+r)N*g*j.

Each coordinate has degree at most five in z. All divisors in this display
are positive fixed-shape scalars. This is a polynomial identity, not an
approximation or a matrix positivity inference. It retains n=0 and all
state/testing dependence because it only rescales the source coefficients
of the unchanged full parent.

There is one important endpoint cancellation. Divide all nonzero coordinates
of P by their monic common univariate polynomial factor G, and choose its
overall sign so Q=P/G is nonnegative on (L,U). Such a sign exists because
P_d>0 there, so G has no zero and constant sign on the interval. The resulting
polynomial vector Q has degree <=5 and no simultaneous zero on [L,U].
Its endpoint values are nonnegative and are limits of source rays.

This division cannot simply be omitted. For example,
k=1/4,b=19/32,r=1/4,t=9/8 gives h=5/16 and g=j=0 at L=1/2, while
q(1/2)=243/512>0. Every coordinate of P has the common factor z-1/2.
Without its removal, the endpoint vector would be zero and would miss the
limiting combination of the m and D event rays.

3. Exact fixed-shape SDP
-----------------------
Write Q(z)=sum_{i=0}^5 Q_i z^i, padding zero coefficients when necessary.
The closed conic hull of the original native source rays for this fixed
shape is exactly

 c=sum_{i=0}^5 Q_i h_i,
 [h_{i+j+1}-L h_{i+j}]_{i,j=0}^2 >=0,
 [U h_{i+j}-h_{i+j+1}]_{i,j=0}^2 >=0.                    (M)

These are the degree-five Hausdorff moment conditions on [L,U]. For
completeness, their sufficiency is a one-variable fact, not a multivariate
SOS assumption. Every polynomial p of degree <=5 nonnegative on [L,U]
has the form

 p(z)=(z-L) sum f_i(z)^2+(U-z) sum g_i(z)^2,
 degree f_i,degree g_i <=2.

To prove this, substitute z=(L+U s^2)/(1+s^2) and multiply by (1+s^2)^5.
The result is an even nonnegative univariate polynomial of degree <=10.
Real factorization expresses it as a sum of two polynomial squares of
degree <=5. Averaging under s -> -s separates each square into its even
part (degree <=4) and odd part s times a polynomial in s^2 (degree <=4).
Substituting back gives the displayed interval identity. Hence the two
PSD inequalities in (M) make the moment functional nonnegative on every
nonnegative polynomial of degree <=5. Finite-dimensional separation from
the closed conic hull of (1,z,...,z^5), z in [L,U], then gives an actual
nonnegative measure on that interval. Conversely any such measure gives
(M). Applying Q gives precisely the source cone, with no relaxation.

The source image is closed here: Q has nonnegative coordinates and no
simultaneous zero on a compact interval, so sum_i Q_i(z) has a strictly
positive minimum. The sum of source coordinates therefore bounds total
representing mass. Limits of source vectors have subsequential moment
limits and remain in (M). This fixes the possible endpoint-zero issue.

The total PSD order is six, and this is a fixed testing-independent SOURCE
cone for each frozen shape. Matrix coefficients are real algebraic for
algebraic shape parameters, because U may contain the quadratic root z_n.
No rationality of those endpoint coefficients is being asserted.

4. The precise uncompleted step for full C2
-----------------------------------------
The four shape parameters k,b,r,t are independent in the native chart.
Both Q_i and the interval endpoints in (M) vary with them. A proposed full
system c=sum_i Q_i(k,b,r,t)h_i is not affine in its unknowns: it contains
products of shape variables and moment variables, as well as h=b-kt and
the parameter-dependent interval endpoint. Replacing the polynomial
coefficients by independent PSD matrices drops exactly these consistency
relations. It is not justified by the univariate moment theorem.

Equivalently, conic sums over varying shapes require moments of a measure
on the full five-parameter native domain, not just moments of z. Showing
that finitely many matrix coefficients encode those joint moments would
require a further exact finite-order moment theorem or an explicit finite
PSD generating cone for their shape dependence. Neither the stationary
identities nor the degree-five bound supply that theorem. Quantifying over
one shape after another gives a family of pencils, not one finite pencil.

Existing canonical completion also cautions against replacing this missing
step by an arbitrary polynomial matrix selector: the published half-line
and ramified cones have exact all-positive valid-source exclusions. Those
examples are not native C2 lambda=beta=0 counterexamples, so they do NOT
refute (M), nor prove that a new matrix-valued interval method cannot work.
They only rule out inferring global adequacy from a univariate selector
ansatz without proving its source admission.

This is the end of the bounded derivation. The useful new reduction is the
explicit single interval and universal degree-five bound for every fixed
C2 shape. It removes all internal z case splitting in such a fiber. It
does not yet remove the four-parameter shape dependence or yield full C2.
