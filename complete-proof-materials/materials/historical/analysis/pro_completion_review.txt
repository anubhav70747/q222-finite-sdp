2. Polynomial-selector obstruction

The degree bound is valid: G_J's 2x2 principal minors bound each polynomial
Jij squared by a polynomial of degree <=4, so deg Jij<=2. Parity averaging
preserves both LMIs. On C1, the zero diagonals at v=0 eliminate the two
diagonal constants; the bound |J12|^2<=4m(4D+2d)v^4 eliminates its remaining
odd-linear coefficient. Therefore every polynomial selector would yield
the three-parameter form printed in the note; no positivity hypothesis on
individual event weights is needed for this reduction.

At the given contact, U>0 and both PSD terms in the split must vanish.
The resulting kernel equations have rank three and force the stated p,r,s.

which is less than -103/80. The elementary hand bounds are sufficient.

The equivalence with non-SOS of Phi(u^2,v;a,b) is also justified. A real
polynomial SOS for this polynomial has roots linear homogeneous in the
states, degree <=2 in u and <=1 in v: higher leading squares cannot cancel.
The same u-parity averaging and zero-block forcing would then produce a
polynomial J. Conversely the two univariate PSD matrices of a polynomial
selector have polynomial factors and give an SOS by the exact split.
This excludes every polynomial degree, not just a truncated search.

The obstruction does NOT extend automatically to algebraic or piecewise
selectors: the entry v*abs(v), for example, evades the odd-polynomial
vanishing-order argument. Nor does it establish non-SOS of u^2*Du*F in the
original state variables; pulling back a hypothetical SOS introduces extra
factors, whose cancellation requires a separate theorem.
