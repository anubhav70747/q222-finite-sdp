\subsection*{What the statement means}

Let $\mathcal Q_{222}\subset\mathbb R^8$ be the set of correlations
of finite-dimensional bipartite complex tensor-product experiments,
with two binary measurements on each side.  We use the coordinates
\[
 q=(a_0,a_1,b_0,b_1,E_{00},E_{01},E_{10},E_{11}),
\]
where $a_x$ and $b_y$ are the single-party expectations and $E_{xy}$
is the joint expectation of the $\{+1,-1\}$-valued observables.
Write $P$ for the cone of real affine functions nonnegative on this set.

The physical reduction proves the exact formula
\[
 \mathcal Q_{222}
 =\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{9}w_i\Phi(z_i):
     z_i\in S^1\times S^1\times S^3,
     \quad w_i\ge0,\quad\sum_iw_i=1\right\}.        \tag{FA1}
\]
Here $\Phi$ is the polynomial expectation map for
$A_0=B_0=Z$, $A_1=c_AZ+s_AX$, $B_1=c_BZ+s_BX$, and a real unit
two-qubit vector.  The proof includes a simultaneous dilation of both
binary effects of each party, the two-involution block decomposition,
the real/imaginary splitting of a complex state, finite direct-sum
realization, and the nine-point convexification argument.  Consequently
$\mathcal Q_{222}$ is compact, convex, and semialgebraic over $\mathbb R$.

For a real closed extension $K\supset\mathbb R$, the symbol
$(\mathcal Q_{222})_K$ means the set defined by the same fixed finite
formula (FA1), with its variables in $K$.  Likewise
\[
 P_K=\{f\in K[q]_{\le1}:f(q)\ge0\text{ for all }
                              q\in(\mathcal Q_{222})_K\}.       \tag{FA2}
\]
The identification with the transferred real cone is proved by the
first-order description of validity.  No compactness assertion about
the order topology of a non-Archimedean field is used.

\subsection*{Separately labelled fields}

Choose arbitrarily two real closed extensions $K_c,K_t\supset\mathbb R$,
and put $T=K_c\otimes_{\mathbb R}K_t$.  The subscripts distinguish
coefficient variables from testing variables.  The two fields may be
two labelled copies of the same extension.  No algebraic-disjointness
assumption, embedding of $T$ in an ordered field, or ordering of $T$
is made.  For $f=f_0+\sum_{\nu=1}^8 f_\nu q_\nu$, define
\[
 [f,q]=f_0\otimes1+\sum_{\nu=1}^8 f_\nu\otimes q_\nu.           \tag{FA3}
\]
The target is membership in $\Sigma T^2$, the set of finite sums of
squares of elements of this tensor ring.  The finite list of roots may
depend on the chosen fields, functional and testing point.

The local proofs use the convex subring $\mathcal O(K)$ of elements
bounded in absolute value by a real constant, and its ideal
$\mathfrak m(K)$ of infinitesimals.  A positive element is of exactly
one of three types: positive infinitesimal, positive bounded unit
(both it and its inverse are bounded), or positive unbounded.
Literal zero is kept separate.  The residue of a bounded element is
its unique real standard part.  These types organize the proof;
they do not identify an independent source infinitesimal with a
testing infinitesimal.

The common algebraic tool states that a bounded symmetric tensor
matrix with positive definite real residue gives a finite tensor SOS
when evaluated on any finite list of tensor roots.  Its proof uses
finite positive pure-factor decompositions and a fixed real
diagonal-dominance reserve.  It never takes the inverse or square
root of a mixed tensor.  The roots themselves need not be bounded.
The complete strict-Gram proof is included among the foundations.

\subsection*{The full polynomial that every local proof certifies}

In the fixed CHSH orientation put
\[
 H=1-\tfrac12(E_{00}-E_{01}+E_{10}+E_{11}),\qquad
 f=dH+4\sum_{\xi}\xi p_\xi,
 \quad\xi\in\{\lambda,n,m,C,A,D,\alpha,\beta\}.                \tag{FA4}
\]
The coefficients are nonnegative, and $d>0$ on the nontrivial
canonical source.  With $D_u=1+u^2$, $D_v=1+v^2$, and
$\psi=(W,X,Y,Z)$, the complete affine-chart parent is
\begin{align*}
 F={}&4\lambda D_u(W+vX)^2+4nD_uD_vX^2
       +4m(vW-X+uvY-uZ)^2\\
   &+4CD_v(uW-Y)^2+4AD_uD_vY^2
       +(4D+2d)(uW+uvX-Y-vZ)^2\\
   &+(4\alpha+2d)D_u(vY-Z)^2
       +(4\beta+2d)D_v(X+uZ)^2-2dD_uD_vZ^2.          \tag{FA5}
\end{align*}
Products of a coefficient-field number and a testing-field expression
in (FA5) always denote their separated tensor product.  A scalar
restriction to a contact graph does not by itself prove the required
statement: the original state-normal terms and all higher terms must
also be included.  Each local proof gives an exact identity or a
complete root allocation for this parent, and restores every retained
positive packet after proving the bounded remainder.

For homogeneous angle pairs $(a_0,a_1)$ and $(b_0,b_1)$, put
$\Delta_A=a_0^2+a_1^2>0$, $\Delta_B=b_0^2+b_1^2>0$.
The homogeneous parent $\mathcal F$ satisfies
\[
 \mathcal F=(1\otimes\Delta_A\Delta_B\|\psi\|^2)
                  [f,\Phi(z)]                              \tag{FA6}
\]
for every nonzero state, with $z$ its normalized physical atom.
The homogeneous identity is proved directly from all eight event
amplitudes.  It includes the zero and infinite angle coordinates;
those points are not obtained by cancelling a vanished affine
coordinate.  The zero state is immediate.

\subsection*{Source reduction and the precise local proposition}

Every valid functional is either a nonnegative event sum or has a
negative value at a PR vertex.  In the latter case the eight neighboring
deterministic vertices and that PR vertex form the affine evaluation
basis giving (FA4).  Normalize the positive coefficient sum in the
source field.  The real compact convex source base and its fixed
nine-term extreme-point representation transfer as finite first-order
formulas.  The determinant has a convex logarithmic Laurent profile.
Its coercive extreme sources have an actual finite noncommuting
stationary contact and five or six positive events.  The incompatible
omission cases have two explicit closed-face matrix lifts.

The remaining compatible omission patterns reduce, by actual Bell
symmetries, to
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathrm{C1}:&d,m,C,A,\alpha>0,\quad\lambda=n=0,\quad
             D,\beta\ge0,\quad D+\beta>0,\\
\mathrm{C2}:&d,m,C,A,D,\alpha>0,\quad\lambda=\beta=0,\quad n\ge0.
\end{array}                                                     \tag{FA7}
\]
The physicality condition and the actual source contact are retained.
The native-entry proof derives all strict signs and gaps from these
conditions, including the literal third-omission faces.  Source
contacts are never substituted for independent testing points.

\paragraph{Complete local-certificate proposition (LC).}
For every source in (FA7), over every $K_c$, and every independent
projective angle pair and state over every $K_t$, the homogeneous
parent $\mathcal F$ is a finite sum of squares in $T$, possibly after
multiplication by a proved positive pure-source factor.  The statement
includes every allowed zero coefficient, arbitrary positive openings,
all relative source and testing rates, all state ratios, exact sign
gauges, and literal projective endpoints.

The total-scale split uses
\[
 \delta_{\rm tot}=
 \frac{d}{d+\lambda+n+m+C+A+D+\alpha+\beta}.                    \tag{FA8}
\]
It is taken before a later $d=1$ or $C=2$ gauge.  For $d>0$ the
quotient is positive bounded, so its positive-unit and
positive-infinitesimal alternatives are exhaustive.  Setting $d=1$
does not select the first alternative.  Literal $d=0$ is a separate
sum of event squares.  At a literally commuting testing pair the
signed core is nonnegative in the testing field, so it too has a
direct separated certificate.

\subsection*{Finite closure and the existence implication}

\begin{theorem}[Universal tensor statement and finite semidefinite lift]
For every pair of separately labelled
real closed extensions $K_c,K_t\supset\mathbb R$,
\[
       [f,q]\in\Sigma(K_c\otimes_{\mathbb R}K_t)^2
       \quad\text{for every }f\in P_{K_c},\quad
                    q\in(\mathcal Q_{222})_{K_t}.               \tag{FA9}
\]
Consequently $\mathcal Q_{222}$ has a finite semidefinite lift.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
An event evaluation has a testing-field sum-of-squares factorization
from its projector, and a nonnegative coefficient contributes its
source-field square root.  The zero source and nonnegative constants
are included by event normalization.

For a closed-face source use its explicit homogeneous matrix lift.
The displayed strict feasible point, with all free coefficient sign
blocks included, makes the adjoint image of the positive semidefinite
cone closed.  To see the attainment step, a strict feasible matrix
$M(z_*)\succeq\epsilon I$ bounds the trace of any positive
semidefinite dual witness whose adjoint image stays bounded.
Separation then gives an attained matrix witness for every nonnegative
evaluation.  This fixed finite existential statement transfers to the
testing field, independently of source feasibility in the coefficient
field.  Factoring the two positive semidefinite matrices there gives
\[
 \sum_{i,j}X_{ij}\otimes Y_{ij}
   =\sum_{a,b}\left(\sum_i B_{ia}\otimes C_{ib}\right)^2,
       \qquad X=BB^T,\quad Y=CC^T.                           \tag{FA10}
\]
The dual equations cancel all auxiliaries and identify this expression
with the original face slack.  Singular matrices are allowed in these
finite field factorizations.

For a canonical contact source apply LC to each real pure testing atom.
If $(s\otimes1)\mathcal F=\sum_k h_k^2$ with $s>0$ in $K_c$,
then (FA6) gives the exact unmultiplied certificate
\[
 [f,\Phi(z)]=\sum_k\left[
   \bigl(s^{-1/2}\otimes
        (\Delta_A\Delta_B\|\psi\|^2)^{-1/2}\bigr)h_k\right]^2.
                                                               \tag{FA11}
\]
All inverses and radicals in this formula are taken inside one field.
Every additional source or testing gauge is restored in the same
way, after its own nonvanishing has been proved.  Finite real Bell
relabelings only change coordinates and preserve the assertion.

Use the finite source decomposition and the nine-atom testing
decomposition to write
\[
 f=\sum_j a_j f_j,\qquad q=\sum_i w_i\Phi(z_i),\qquad
 a_j,w_i\ge0,\quad\sum_iw_i=1.
\]
If $[f_j,\Phi(z_i)]=\sum_k h_{ijk}^2$, then
\[
 [f,q]=\sum_{i,j,k}
       \left[(\sqrt{a_j}\otimes\sqrt{w_i})h_{ijk}\right]^2.
                                                               \tag{FA12}
\]
Linearity in $f$ and affineness in $q$ prove this identity.  The equation
$\sum_iw_i=1$ preserves the affine constant.  Zero weights produce
zero roots and are never inverted.  The real/imaginary state split and
finite convexification already include complex and mixed states.
This proves (FA9).

Now choose two labelled copies of one arbitrary real closed extension
$K$ of $\mathbb R$.  Formula (FA9) gives the hypothesis of
Corollary~3.19 in Claus Scheiderer's
\emph{Second-order cone representation for convex sets in the plane},
arXiv:2004.04196v2.  That corollary applies to a closed convex
semialgebraic subset of arbitrary finite-dimensional $\mathbb R^n$.
Its point-first tensor convention is equivalent to (FA3) by the factor
flip, which preserves squares.  The physical reduction proves all of
its set hypotheses, and (FA2) identifies its transferred valid-affine
cone.  It therefore gives finite semidefinite-lift existence.
\end{proof}

Explicitly, the existence conclusion has the form
\[
 \mathcal Q_{222}=
 \left\{q\in\mathbb R^8:\ \exists y\in\mathbb R^m,\quad
 A_0+\sum_{\nu=1}^8q_\nu A_\nu+\sum_{j=1}^m y_jB_j\succeq0\right\}
                                                               \tag{FA13}
\]
for finite integers $N,m$ and real symmetric $N\times N$ matrices.
Several semidefinite blocks can be combined diagonally.  Linear
equalities in a lift can be eliminated by parametrization or represented
by opposite scalar blocks.  These operations give (FA13) in the
original eight coordinates.
