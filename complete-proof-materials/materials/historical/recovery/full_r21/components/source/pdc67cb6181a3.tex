\section{C14.4: a contact-centered full-parent identity through the $M$ opening}
\label{sec:recovery-c144-centered-m}

This section is an exact reduction, not an all-tests admission. It supplies
an opening-safe state normal for the remaining C14.4 source. Keep all
original source conditions and write $R_s=1+xr$, $M_s=R_s-\theta/\zeta>0$.
Define only in the coefficient field
\[
 p=\frac{\theta}{\zeta R_s}=1-\frac{M_s}{R_s}\in(0,1),\qquad
 \mu=m(1-p)=\frac{(1+x)(1+\sigma r)eS}{2\zeta P R_s},\qquad
 m_c=\frac m{1+m}.
 \tag{CM1}
\]
Here $0<m_c<1$, $m-m_c\ge0$, and $\mu$ is bounded uniformly through
$M_s\to0$. In particular the potentially unbounded $m$ occurs below
only in an unchanged positive excess square.

On the finite Bob chart let $D_u=1+u^2$, $D_v=1+v^2$ and set
\[
 V=v(W+uY),\quad N=X+uZ-pV,\quad
 J_0=uW-Y+puvV-(1+u^2)vZ.\tag{CM2}
\]
The original event amplitudes are exactly
\[
 vW-X+uvY-uZ=(1-p)V-N,\qquad
 X+uZ=N+pV,\qquad
 uW-Y+uvX-vZ=J_0+uvN.\tag{CM3}
\]
Consequently the entire original quarter-parent is
\[
\begin{split}
 F/4={}&(m-m_c)N^2+C D_v(uW-Y)^2+A D_uD_vY^2\\
 &+m_cN^2-2\mu NV+\mu(1-p)V^2
 +(D+\tfrac12)(J_0+uvN)^2\\
 &+(\alpha+\tfrac12)D_u(vY-Z)^2
 +(\beta+\tfrac12)D_v(N+pV)^2-\tfrac12D_uD_vZ^2.
\end{split}\tag{CM4}
\]
This is a polynomial identity after clearing pure-source denominators.
It follows by substituting CM3 into all six original event packets and
the complete signed core. No state variable has been set to a contact
value in CM4.

\paragraph{Why this particular normal preserves the actual contact.}
For the actual source contact, $u_c=k=\sqrt x$,
$v_c=\sqrt{L/\sigma}$, $W_c/Z_c=\sqrt{\zeta\sigma}$,
$X_c=-hkZ_c$ and $Y_c=rkW_c$. Thus
$v_cW_c=\zeta kZ_c$ and
\[
 N_c=\theta kZ_c-pR_s\zeta kZ_c=0.\tag{CM5}
\]
Retaining $(m-m_c)N^2$ therefore respects the original kernel. Capping
the uncentered $m$ event itself would not have this property when $M_s>0$.
The contact calculation is a source-field check of the choice of normal;
it is not an identification of independent testing variables with source
parameters in the tensor claim.

\paragraph{The complete first-normal coefficient is bounded.}
For bounded $v$ and the residual infinitesimal $u$, put
$\widehat N=\sqrt{1+\beta}\,N$. Expanding the last three squares
in CM4 shows that its residual $\widehat N^2$ coefficient is
\[
 H_N=\frac{m_c}{1+\beta}
  +\frac{(D+1/2)u^2v^2}{1+\beta}
  +\frac{\beta+1/2}{1+\beta}D_v.
 \tag{CM6}
\]
More precisely,
\[
 H_N-\tfrac12D_v
 =\frac{m_c}{1+\beta}
  +\frac{(D+1/2)u^2v^2}{1+\beta}
  +\frac{\beta D_v}{2(1+\beta)}
\]
is a sum of separated nonnegative products, hence tensor SOS. This
identity states the diagonal reserve without assigning an order to
the mixed tensor ring.
The full first-normal cross term is
\[
 2\widehat N\left[
 \frac{-\mu+(\beta+1/2)D_vp}{\sqrt{1+\beta}}V
 +\frac{(D+1/2)uv}{\sqrt{1+\beta}}J_0\right].\tag{CM7}
\]
All these coefficients are bounded separated expressions on the stated
chart. Indeed ES gives $D,\alpha$ infinitesimal, while
$2\theta P\beta=hB-\sigma H_0$ gives $\beta\theta=O(1)$.
Hence $\beta p=(\beta\theta)/(\zeta R_s)$ and $\beta p^2$
are bounded through $\theta\to0$; $1/\zeta$ and $1/R_s$ are
bounded. CM1 bounds $\mu$ through the independent $M_s$ opening.
The real residue of $H_N$ is at least the residue of $D_v/2$, even
when one of the opening parameters is infinitesimal.

The remaining scalar and $Y$ form is printed explicitly by CM4 with
$N=0$; the original positive $C,A$ rows are retained. A strict normal
pivot does not prove that this remaining form is tensor SOS, and
completing it using a mixed inverse of $H_N$ is not allowed. In particular
CM4 alone does not resolve the matched Bob source/test contact or the
unit-$e$ sector. A valid completion must allocate the full signed soft
form together with CM7, or produce an exact impossible-source proof.
The reciprocal Bob chart is not supplied by a finite-$v$ limit.

The accompanying checker verifies CM3--CM7 against the entire original
four-state polynomial and verifies the contact relation. This reduction
retains every coefficient opening and exposes the remaining soft problem
without a divergent $m$ first-normal coefficient.
