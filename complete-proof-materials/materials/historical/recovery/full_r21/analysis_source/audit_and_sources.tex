\subsection{The mixed denominator in (9.11)}

The printed completion uses
\[
 e(1+u^2)+\beta
\]
and says the denominator lies wholly in one tensor factor.
Under the manuscript's own typing, \(e,\beta\) are source parameters
and \(u\) is a testing parameter. That assertion is false as written.

For its pair \(e(1+u^2)X^2+\beta(X+uB)^2\), a source-only repair is,
with \(c=e+\beta>0\),
\begin{equation}
 c\left(X+\frac{\beta}{c}uB\right)^2
 +\frac{e\beta}{c}u^2B^2+eu^2X^2.
 \label{eq:repair}
\end{equation}
Expansion proves equality. If \(c=0\), nonnegativity gives
\(e=\beta=0\), and the pair is zero. For testing \(X,u,B\),
the roots have ranks at most \(2,1,1\).
The last term must remain: with \(N=X+(\beta/c)uB\), it becomes
\[
 eu^2N^2-2e(\beta/c)u^3BN+e(\beta/c)^2u^4B^2.
\]
A pairwise repair does not by itself repair the subsequent exact
critical center and all degenerate allocations. \src{A0, \S9.1; FP}

\subsection{A reciprocal exact-versus-residue mismatch}

Equations (13.0o)--(13.0q) give
\[
 \frac d\lambda=2\zeta U_d,\qquad
 U_d=\frac{JN_d}{D_0D_1},\qquad \overline{U_d}=1.
\]
After setting \(\lambda=1\), \(z=\varepsilon/c=\zeta\), the later
equation (13.2) calls \(d=2z\) a complete contact equation.
A unit with residue one need not equal one.

Appendix A makes the discrepancy checkable by hand.
Put \(x=r^2\). The constant terms of \(D_0,D_1,N_d\) are \(J\),
so the coefficient of \(x\) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 [x](JN_d-D_0D_1)
 &=J\bigl(N_1-D_{0,1}-D_{1,1}\bigr)\\
 &=J\bigl[J^2(1+z)+Kc^2\bigr]>0
\end{aligned}
\]
on the real strict chamber. Thus \(JN_d=D_0D_1\) is not a
polynomial identity. The exact formula has the nonconstant factor
\(U_d\); \(d=2z\) must be identified as an initial-layer formula or
supplied with an exact reparametrization and restoration argument.

This identifies an inconsistency in the printed identities' scope.
It is not a quantum counterexample and does not establish failure
of the separate October formulas. \src{A0, \S13 and Appendix A;
current August audit}
