1. Why constrained residue nonnegativity follows from physicality
================================================================

The crucial step is A6_INDUCTION lines 38--44. Let S be the current hard matching
and let x_i be its exact event amplitudes. Their state-linear independence gives
a real rational coordinate chart (x,y) with denominators nonzero at the chosen
noncommuting residue. The full parent has the exact form

    F(x,y) = sum_i a_i b_i(y) x_i^2 + sigma G(x,y),

where a_i and sigma are source-field numbers, epsilon_i=sigma/a_i are positive
infinitesimals, the b_i are positive units locally, and G has bounded source
coefficients. Here sigma is the maximum of the signed coefficient and the
remaining event coefficients, so every coefficient of G is bounded after the
declared pure testing coordinate denominators are cleared.

Fix a real y in the coordinate domain. In the coefficient field impose x=0
EXACTLY. The hard packets then vanish identically, regardless of how large
a_i/sigma are. Physicality of the original source gives

    G(0,y) = F(0,y)/sigma >= 0.

The left side is bounded, so taking its real residue gives

    g(y)=residue(G)(0,y) >= 0.

For the homogeneous constrained state form this applies to every real state
in the hard kernel and every noncommuting real angle pair. Consequently its
state matrix is PSD, its determinant is nonnegative, and any interior zero of
the associated Laurent polynomial has zero logarithmic gradient.

This argument uses exact hard-kernel restriction solely to derive information
about the real residue function g. It does not assert that the actual independent
test lies exactly on that kernel. The weighted lemma subsequently certifies
the complete parent at the actual test, retaining all hard normal deviations
and every remaining signed/event term. There is no discarded infinitesimal
term in this passage.

2. The eight-cycle geometry
===========================

2.1 Product residue exit (A6_GEOMETRY lines 4--14)

For a product state the CHSH expression obeys

    S <= |b0-b1|+|b0+b1| = 2 max(|b0|,|b1|) <= 2.

If both Bob moduli are less than one, this is strict. If one has modulus one,
noncommuting Bob reflections prevent the other from also having modulus one.
Both coefficients b0-b1 and b0+b1 are then nonzero. Equality would require both
Alice moduli to be one, impossible for her noncommuting reflections. Therefore
H=1-S/2 has strictly positive real residue. Its actual value in the testing
field is positive; d tensor H has separate field square roots. All event
packets are already tensor squares. This settles the full parent at a product
residue, even if d is a positive infinitesimal.

2.2 Entangled matching (lines 39--61)

Write a real entangled two-qubit state as an invertible two-by-two matrix Psi.
For fixed nonzero local ray a, the vanishing equation a^T Psi b=0 determines
one Bob projective ray, and conversely. Hence vanishing losing events share
no local ray: they form a matching in the eight-cycle.

For at most three events, the product vectors are independent. If three
rank-one matrices in a two-dimensional span were dependent, either the first
two have independent left and right factors, in which case their span contains
only their two rank-one projective points, or they share a left/right factor,
in which case the whole span shares it. Three cycle edges cannot share a
vertex, whose degree is two. At a common nonzero projective kernel state,
these independent linear forms give independent state derivatives. Thus
the local event normal rank really is k for k<=3.

The cycle has exactly two perfect matchings. For {n,m,A,D}, X=Y=0 and the other
two equations give vW=uZ and uW=vZ, whence u^2=v^2. In W=1 coordinates the
two remaining derivatives listed in the text are independent for u!=0. For
{lambda,c,alpha,beta}, W=1 gives X=-1/v, Y=u, Z=uv, followed by u^2v^2=1
with nonzero angular derivative. Each perfect matching has three state
normals and one angular normal. This is a direct geometric classification,
not an appeal to an enumerated machine atlas.

3. The one-hard-event constrained minimum
=========================================

3.1 Exact restriction and state rank (A6_ONE lines 6--10)

The CHSH symmetry group is transitive on the eight events, so the hard event
can be lambda. Its exact kernel is W=-vX. The signed core on Z=0 is positive
definite when d>0 and uv!=0, as displayed in A6_PARENT lines 54--56. On the
three-dimensional constrained state space, the Z=0 intersection has dimension
two. Therefore a PSD constrained matrix at a zero has two positive eigenvalues
and one simple kernel eigenvalue. The kernel has Z!=0, permitting the Z=1
projective chart. This argument is over the real constrained residue field.

3.2 Determinant differentiation (A6_ONE lines 12--29)

The hard lambda packet is a rank-one update. In the determinant dictionary,
differentiate each q_ij with respect to lambda. I checked all eight resulting
outer coefficients against A6_PARENT lines 61--75; they are exactly those
printed in A6_ONE. Each has nonnegative coefficients, and all t_i occurring
in it are strictly positive because the normalized residue d is positive.

The basis with columns e_W, (-v,1,0,0), e_Y, e_Z has determinant one. The
lambda entry is 4 lambda D_u. Thus the coefficient of lambda in det(P) is
4D_u times the determinant of the restricted three-by-three matrix. From
the printed full determinant it follows exactly that

    det(P_constrained) = 4D_u D_v^2 U V L_lambda(U,V).

This is a positive smooth factor times a Laurent polynomial with nonnegative
outer coefficients. Its logarithmic Hessian is the sum of weighted exponent
outer products. The only possible rank-one stationary supports are opposite
pairs on the four lines through the Newton square.

3.3 Exhaustive exclusions (lines 31--49)

The horizontal and vertical cases contradict their off-line zero coefficients;
the slope-minus-one pair also activates an off-line coefficient. The only
remaining rank-one pattern is contained in the perfect matching
{lambda,c,alpha,beta}. On the matching curve all event squares and the signed
core vanish, but the signed core has a nonzero derivative in each of the four
independent event-normal directions. One hard constraint removes only one
of those directions, leaving a nonzero constrained derivative. A globally
nonnegative real function cannot have such a derivative at a zero.

For clarity, on the equivalent matching {n,m,A,D}, set u=t, v=t+delta, W=1,
Z=1+zeta. The four linear event normals are X,Y,R_m,R_D, where

    R_m=delta-X+t^2Y-t zeta,
    R_D=t^2X-Y-delta-t zeta.

The signed-core first differential is

    2t[(1+t^2)dX-(1+t^2)dY-dR_m+dR_D].

All four coefficients are nonzero for t!=0. This can also be checked directly
by differentiating the displayed physical parent at (W,X,Y,Z)=(1,0,0,1).

If no outer exponent is active, the remaining event support is contained in
one of the three nonextendable triples listed in the proof. Their exact kernel
states annihilate all events while the signed core is negative. Thus rank
zero is impossible as well.

The exponent span is therefore two-dimensional at every constrained zero.
Combining its strictly positive angular Hessian with the two positive state
directions gives a strictly positive four-variable constrained Hessian. The
Schur complement used to identify that Hessian is an ordinary real-residue
calculation. No mixed tensor matrix is inverted.

4. The two-hard-event constrained minimum
=========================================

4.1 State rank and pair types (A6_TWO lines 6--12)

The two exact hard event equations leave a two-dimensional state space. Its
intersection with Z=0 has dimension at least one, where the signed core is
positive. Hence at a constrained zero the PSD state matrix has one positive
eigenvalue and one simple kernel with Z!=0. Matching pairs have cycle distances
two, three or four; the three listed representative pairs exhaust them by
dihedral CHSH symmetries. No comparison of the two hard weights is used.

4.2 All 24 activation conditions checked (lines 13--34)

Starting from the eight one-lambda derivative coefficients, differentiate with
respect to m, c or alpha. I checked all 24 positivity conditions in the printed
table by this direct elementary differentiation.

For example, for the {lambda,m} pair the (-1,-1) coefficient is a positive
constant times alpha t8, so it is active exactly when alpha>0. The (-1,0)

follow in the same way; no sign cancellation is hidden in the table.

An adapted state basis makes the coefficient of the product of the two hard
weights a strictly positive angle factor times the constrained determinant.
This is an exact rank-one-update/cofactor identity, not a limit assuming equal
hard-weight scales. Thus the extracted Laurent polynomial has the same zero

4.3 Curvature exclusions (lines 36--53)

For {lambda,m}, each possible opposite-pair line activates an off-line exponent.
With no active exponent, the remaining support is contained in {A} or {c}.
For {lambda,c}, only the slope-plus-one line remains, and its off-line equations
force precisely the perfect-matching support; rank zero leaves only m. For
{lambda,alpha}, the always-active (-1,-1) exponent rules out rank zero and
restricts rank one to the same perfect matching.

The two rank-zero witnesses at u=v=1 are literal:

    (W,X,Y,Z)=(1,-1,0,2) for {lambda,m,A},
    (W,X,Y,Z)=(1,-1,1,3) for {lambda,m,c}.

Their event amplitudes vanish and direct substitution in the signed parent
gives respectively -4 and -8. The rank-one matching pattern is impossible
because two hard equations still leave a tangent direction with nonzero
signed-core derivative while every remaining event square has zero derivative.

Thus the exponent vectors span the plane at every physical constrained zero.
The one positive state pivot and the two positive angular directions give a
strict three-variable constrained Hessian. Again all Schur operations occur
only over the ordinary real residue field.

5. Returning from constrained curvature to the complete tensor parent
====================================================================

A6_TWO line 55 and A6_ONE line 53 use the unequal diagonal hard-scale lemma,
not an assertion that the constrained residue is already the full slack.
For epsilon_i=sigma/a_i, put x_i=epsilon_i s_i in the SOURCE-FIELD stationary
equations. Their residue Jacobian has positive diagonal hard block and the
strict constrained Hessian as tangent block. The semialgebraic implicit-
function argument therefore supplies an exact source-field critical center
with bounded s_ci and x_ci=epsilon_i s_ci.

The Gram roots are

    (x_i(test)-x_ci(source))/sqrt(epsilon_i(source)),
    y_j(test)-y_cj(source).

They are finite sums of elementary tensors. They need not be bounded: the
strict-Gram theorem concerns the bounded coefficient matrix. Exact finite
Taylor allocation leaves a Gram with positive-definite real residue. Hard
normal-tangent entries acquire factors sqrt(epsilon_i), hard tangent entries
acquire epsilon_i, and all soft terms are bounded at the declared scale.
The center value is nonnegative by source physicality and is a pure source
square. All original terms remain in this identity.

The state coordinate changes for one, two or three hard events use only
real testing denominators nonzero at the residue. Clearing their squares
preserves local positivity and the strict constrained Hessian at a zero.
No expression like the mixed denominator in August equation (9.11) appears
in this full-parent construction.

6. Interface with the finite induction
======================================

The finite induction in A6_INDUCTION lines 34--48 uses these propositions in
exactly their proved scope. Starting with sum c_i+d=1 and residue(d)=0, at
least one event coefficient has positive residue. The unresolved entangled
state makes these events a nonempty matching S.

At the next pure source scale sigma, if g(y0)>0 the weighted lemma settles the
test. If g(y0)=0 and residue(d/sigma)>0, the cases |S|=1 and |S|=2 use the
propositions reviewed here; |S|=3 uses the separate Laurent restriction theorem.
If residue(d/sigma)=0, then g is a nonnegative event-square sum. At least one
remaining normalized event weight is positive; zero g forces its residue
amplitude to vanish. Adding all such edges preserves the matching property.
Every still-remaining coefficient is infinitesimal relative to every newly
hard weight, as well as relative to the earlier hard weights.

This addition is a refinement of the normal-scale analysis of the SAME full
parent. It does not set those actual normal deviations to zero in the final
tensor. Each repetition adds at least one edge, and a matching has at most
four. The termination bound of at most three enlargements is therefore valid.
At four edges, the induction explicitly invokes the arbitrary-scale matching
terminal, whose detailed proof lies outside this narrow pass.
