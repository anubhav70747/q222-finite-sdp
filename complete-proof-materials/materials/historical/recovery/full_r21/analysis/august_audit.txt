1. Recovered logical spine
==========================

1.1 Physical finite model (A0 38--62)

Binary contractions have involution dilations. Two projective binary observables
admit Jordan blocks of dimension at most two. Both observables in each two-
dimensional local block can be placed in a real plane. The measured operators
are then real; taking the real part of the state and its real spectral
decomposition preserves all eight expectations. Hence Q222 is the convex hull
of a compact semialgebraic real two-qubit family. Finite Caratheodory gives a
fixed nine-atom formula in R^8, establishing compactness, convexity and
semialgebraicity and making transfer to real closed fields precise.

1.2 Polar reduction (A0 64--130; A1 Repair 1)

The central positivity polytope G and eight CHSH apices give the local polar.
The visible-facet calculation and pairwise-midpoint property reduce the quantum
polar to G and eight Bell-related compact stars. Once one star has a finite
lift of the desired block size, finite convex-hull closure and polarity finish
the upper bound. These closure operations do not need a printed solver-ready
pencil to establish existence.

1.3 Complete support family (A0 134--210)

A common strictly interior point provides a normalization of all physical and
simplex supports. The compact generator family E includes the constant support.
The separation argument proves P_Kstar=cone(E), with at most nine generators.
The extreme-point set is semialgebraic and its convex hull is Kstar. Thus the
primitive target really does suffice for all affine supports and all points.

2. The diagonal mechanism is valid with the correct algebraic hypotheses
=======================================================================

A0 Lemma 3.1 (lines 212--280) follows the idea of S25 Lemma 3.12. A safe formulation
avoids a mixed localization. Let B be a smooth affine real coordinate ring,
A=B tensor_R B, J the diagonal ideal, p a real point, and M the maximal ideal
of (p,p). Suppose tau belongs to J^2 and its image in J^2/MJ^2 is a positive-
definite quadratic form on T_pY. Choose global algebra generators f_i of B and
put v_i=delta(f_i). The v_i generate J. Their differentials span the cotangent
space; therefore the intrinsic positive form extends to a real positive-
definite matrix Q0 on the full generator coordinate space. Consequently

    tau - v^T Q0 v belongs to M J^2,

and the generators v_i v_j give an exact symmetric matrix E with entries in M:

    tau = v^T (Q0+E) v.

Upon independent evaluation at two bounded points with residue p, E has zero
real residue. A finite real square-frame decomposition leaves strictly positive
coefficient reserves. Proposition 3.5 expresses each such coefficient as a
finite sum of nonnegative pure tensors. Every resulting root is a real linear
combination of divided differences multiplied by a pure tensor, and therefore
has tensor rank at most two.

This proves an exact local theorem. It requires the actual tau to belong to
the square of the appropriate ideal and the complete quadratic residue to be
strictly positive. A positive Hessian over a non-Archimedean field can have a
singular real residue and is insufficient. An invertible weighted coordinate
change over that field need not be invertible on the real cotangent fiber.

The phrase in A0 allowing localization at functions of positive residue must
be interpreted with care. A mixed function can have positive residue without
its inverse belonging to the unlocalized tensor product. The global-generator
argument above repairs the general mechanism without that inverse. It does
not, by itself, prove the hypotheses for all exceptional Bell charts.

3. Literal tensor typing in equation (9.11), and its exact local repair
=====================================================================

Locator: A0 Section 9.1, lines 1078--1091. The displayed completion has denominator

    e1(1+u^2)+beta.

With the assignment in Section 4, e1 and beta are Bell point/source coefficients
and u is the independent physical support parameter. Thus this expression is

    e1 tensor (1+u^2) + beta tensor 1,

which is mixed. The sentence saying it lies wholly in one factor is false under
the printed typing. As a one-field identity, the completion is correct; using
its roots directly in the tensor ring needs a different argument.

The newly recovered source-only repair is exact and can be checked by hand.
For e=e1, c=e+beta>0, k=beta/c, h=e beta/c,

    e(1+u^2)X^2 + beta(X+uB)^2
      = c(X+k uB)^2 + h u^2 B^2 + e u^2 X^2.

The three roots are

    sqrt(c) tensor X + beta/sqrt(c) tensor uB,
    sqrt(h) tensor uB,
    sqrt(e) tensor uX.

Their ranks are at most two, one and one. If c=0 the original pair is zero.
The calculation uses ck=beta and ck^2+h=beta. This is a complete correction
to the pair, including its boundary c=0.

It does not automatically repair every subsequent scalar-envelope claim. With
N=X+k uB, the last retained term is

    e u^2 X^2 = e u^2 N^2 - 2 e k u^3 B N + e k^2 u^4 B^2.

Its higher-order contribution and cross term belong to the full parent and
must remain in the exact critical-center analysis. The later R1/C2 work should
be assessed on its own complete identities, not inferred from this repair.

The absolute values in the following matrix decomposition (9.12) also require
either an actual matrix over one ordered factor or a replacement by an exact
bounded tensor Gram with positive real residue. No absolute-value operation on
general mixed tensor coefficients has been specified in the August text.

4. Exact reciprocal identity versus an initial-layer identity
============================================================

This pass found an additional internal inconsistency not resolved by the six
August31 changes.

Locators: A0 lines 1881--1897, equations (13.0o)--(13.0q); lines 1901--1923,
equations (13.1)--(13.2); Appendix A, equations (A.1)--(A.8).

The text first states the exact Cramer relation

    D0 D1 d = 2 J zeta Nd lambda,

hence

    d/lambda = 2 zeta Ud,  Ud=J Nd/(D0 D1),  residue(Ud)=1.

It then normalizes lambda=1, sets z=epsilon/c=zeta, and says the complete
contact equations give d=2z. These two assertions are not the same exact
identity: the printed Appendix A does not have Ud identically one.

Here is a hand derivation using only the first displayed coefficients. Put x=r^2.
Write

    D0 = J + a x + O(x^2),
    D1 = J + b x + O(x^2),
    Nd = J + n x + O(x^2),

where Appendix A gives

    a = J c^2 z + J c^2 - K c^2,
    b = J^2 + J c^2 + J - K c^2 + c^2,
    n = J^2 z + 2 J^2 + J c^2 z + 2 J c^2 + J - K c^2 + c^2.

Therefore

    [x](J Nd - D0 D1)
       = J(n-a-b)
       = J[J^2(1+z)+K c^2].

This is strictly positive for real strict-chamber data J>1, z>0, K>=0. In
particular it is 10 at J=2, z=1/4, c=1, K=0. Thus Ud is a unit of residue one
with a generally nonzero r^2 correction, not the constant one. The formula
d=2z can describe the initial/residue layer, or it could be made exact after
a declared reparametrization, but it cannot simultaneously be the printed
complete formula with the unchanged z=epsilon/c and the preceding Cramer
identity.

This observation does not show that a reciprocal tensor certificate is
impossible. It identifies precisely what a corrected proof must do: retain
the exact Ud correction and all companion coefficients, or define a new
coordinate and rederive their formulas. If only the initial layer is used to
calculate a Hessian, the proof must connect it to the exact centered parent
and bound the correction. An initial Hessian can be sufficient after those
steps; it is not itself the claimed exact identity.

5. Common incidence and descent: exact points versus residue strata
===================================================================

Locators: A0 Section 14.1, lines 2081--2137; Section 14.2, lines 2139--2161;
Section 15, especially lines 2176--2186.

The endpoint paragraph of Lemma 14.1 first establishes coordinate vanishing
at the common residue point, then refers to a proper common coordinate child
and repeats the coefficient filtration inside it. In a real closed extension,
these are different assertions:

    residue(a)=0  does not imply  a=0.

A positive infinitesimal is the elementary example. Thus the child must be
specified either as an exact restriction of the actual source, or as a residue
stratum together with all retained transverse parameters. In the second case
an exact parent-to-chart identity or collar theorem is required. A shared
residue does not itself give independent lifts of the two actual copies into
a proposed smaller exact variety.

The invariant kappa=(N_act,d_stratum) is well founded. It proves termination
provided every recursive arrow actually deletes an original coefficient or
imposes a further exact equation without restoring previous ones. Loss of a
positive real residue alone changes neither entry. Consequently the list of
possible residue nodes and the invariant do not replace the proof that each
actual degeneration is covered with its transverse terms intact.

Section 7 equation (7.5) illustrates the right type of successful bridge:
