2. THE TWO SOURCE EXCLUSIONS HAVE ACTUAL ALGEBRAIC REASONS

HJ4-HJ6 introduce the original contact variables and exact stationary weights.
With z=u_c^2, y=v_c^2, H=v_cY_c, L=v_c(u_cW_c-Y_c), B=H+L,
the original gap j=1+zr-L/y>0 gives L<y(1+zr). Thus L is infinitesimal
and B is bounded. The positive numerator decomposition in HJ5 proves

    alpha weak  <=>  B infinitesimal.

On the stated directed input, therefore, B,z,y are positive infinitesimals.
Set a=z/B, t=y/B, h=H/B, l=L/B=1-h, e=1-r, g=G/B.
Only a,t may be unbounded; h,l,r,e,g,j are positive bounded quantities.

The first important exclusion is HJ13-HJ14:

    n unbounded <=> r infinitesimal;
    m unbounded <=> g infinitesimal;
    A unbounded <=> H/y infinitesimal;
    C unbounded <=> L/(H+y) infinitesimal;
    D unbounded <=> y/B infinitesimal or j infinitesimal.

Consequently all six weights are bounded exactly when
t,h,l,j,r,g are units. This is incompatible with delta_tot infinitesimal.

The equivalence can be checked without a hidden a-scale assumption. HJ11
gives r(2n+1) and AH/y positive units. HJ12 gives m~1/g when g is
infinitesimal and m bounded when g is a unit. For t a unit the remaining
unboundedness tests are exactly h,l,j infinitesimal. If t is infinitesimal,
the j gap forces l infinitesimal and C,D unbounded; if t is unbounded,
C,A are unbounded. This covers the apparently missing a=z/B scale.

The second exclusion is HJ15:

    C weak <=> r,g,H/y,j all infinitesimal.

For the forward implication, the positive terms of M_s/(L delta) force
H/L infinitesimal. Bounded C forces L comparable to H+y, while the j gap
gives L<=y(1+zr), hence L~y and B~y. The other positive terms force
G/delta, zr/delta, zj/delta infinitesimal. If z/delta were infinitesimal,
B/delta and G/delta would instead have residue one. Thus z/delta is a
unit, r,j are infinitesimal, and G>0 forces B/z a unit; g is then
infinitesimal. Conversely the four stated small quantities imply
B/z~1 and B/y~1, making each positive summand small.

This excludes exactly the simultaneous r,j-infinitesimal part of R7 on the
directed strong-C input. It is not legitimate to delete that condition
from R7's positive-pivot argument, and BSR explicitly does not do so.

7. CT REFLECTION AND NATIVE-GAP PRESERVATION

The complete C2 source has actual angles u_c=c, v_c=ac and CT2 gaps.

    (u,v;W,X,Y,Z;n,m,C,A,D,alpha)
    ->(1/v,1/u;Z,X,Y,W;n,D,alpha,A,m,C).

It preserves the parent after multiplying by the positive pure-testing
factor u^2v^2, with literal endpoints treated homogeneously. Packetwise,
for example, uv J'=-M and uv M'=-J, explaining m <-> D.

Let z=c^2. The source map is

    c'=1/(ac), a'=a, b'=a^2/b,
    r'=a^2zr/b, t'=(1-zh)/b.

Direct substitution gives

    h'=a^2zh/b,  g'=a^2j/b,  j'=g/b,
    1-r'=(b-a^2zr)/b,
    1-z'h'=(b-h)/b=a^2t/b,
    b'-a^2z'r'=a^2(1-r)/b.

Every displayed quantity has the required strict positive sign by CT2.
The six coefficients are permuted, so delta_tot is unchanged and n=0 is
preserved. These calculations verify the reflection at the level relevant
to routing. No false rule t -> 1/t is used.

9. SINGLE-ANGLE COVERAGE AND A USEFUL ORIENTATION DETAIL

In the IU chart, n>=0 makes bounded b a positive unit (CT10). For bounded
b there are exactly three cases: g unit, g infinitesimal/r unit, and
g,r both infinitesimal. The actual theorem headers of
c2_openings_and_unbounded_n.tex, c2_last_opening.tex and
joint_centered_packets.tex match those alternatives and allow arbitrary
h and b-h openings. The first derives the formerly assumed lower bounds
on 1-r and b-k^2r from n>=0. Thus importing only the earlier
general_single_angle.tex theorem would be insufficient, but CT imports
the extensions explicitly.

For unbounded b, the positive gaps give B=xb bounded. B unit gives the
strong pitchfork; B infinitesimal gives unbounded_b_small_B.tex. Its
theorem expressly allows arbitrary B/sigma, every n scale, and n=0.
The strong-pitchfork extension explicitly handles unbounded C or alpha,
in addition to the prior bounded-complementary-coefficient theorem.

When the oriented weak UO cell is reflected, it becomes IU with C strong
and alpha weak. This is not a hole: the single-angle bodies do not require
both events strong. In their bounded-b regime C is strong and alpha weak;
the unbounded-b split handles the additional strong-complement regime.
The two column labels in CT6 should therefore not be misread as additional
hypotheses of every leaf named in that row.
