\section{\texorpdfstring{A full-parent terminal for a finite positive-d commuting edge}{A full-parent terminal for a finite positive-d commuting edge}}
\label{component:p4ec1a51262f9}
\hypertarget{a-full-parent-terminal-for-a-finite-positive-d-commuting-edge}{%
\subsection*{A full-parent terminal for a finite positive-d commuting edge}\label{p4ec1a51262f9:a-full-parent-terminal-for-a-finite-positive-d-commuting-edge}}

25 September 2026. New work; the round5 package is unchanged.

This theorem closes the nondegenerate finite-angle part of a distance-three
jet face when one of its two omitted events is allowed to open. It applies
to the C1 and C2 routing wherever the hypotheses below hold. It does not
assert completion of their projective endpoints or flat core boundaries.

\hypertarget{precise-separate-field-statement}{%
\subsubsection*{1. Precise separate-field statement}\label{p4ec1a51262f9:precise-separate-field-statement}}

Let the coefficients belong to the bounded valuation ring of a real closed
extension \(K_c\supset\mathbb R\), and let an independent measurement/state
test belong to the bounded valuation ring of \(K_s\supset\mathbb R\), with
projective charts used as necessary. Assume the coefficient vector is
physical over \(K_c\), is nonnegative, and satisfies \textbf{exactly}

\[
\lambda=0.
\]

Its real residue \(c_0\) is assumed to satisfy

\[
d_0>0,\qquad (e_2)_0=0.
\tag{1}
\]

There are no exact support requirements on \(e_1,e_3,e_4,e_5,\alpha,\beta\).
In particular any of them may open infinitesimally from zero. On the exact
residue face \(\lambda=e_2=0\), write the known determinant decomposition as

\[
Q_{c_0}(U,V)=U E(V)+U^2 B(V),
\quad E(V)=q_{10}+q_{11}V+q_{12}V^2,
\quad B(V)=q_{20}+q_{21}V+q_{22}V^2.
\tag{2}
\]

Fix a real \(v_0\ne0\), and suppose

\[
q_{10}>0,\quad q_{12}>0,\quad E(v_0^2)=0,\quad B(v_0^2)>0.
\tag{3}
\]

Also assume the three-state complement \(M_0(v_0)\) in (5) below is positive
definite. These are entirely real residue conditions. The latter condition
is automatic if \(\alpha_0+(e_5)_0>0\), and is displayed explicitly to avoid
an unproved support-exhaustion claim.

For every independent test with

\[
\bar u=0,\qquad \bar v=v_0,
\qquad [\bar W:\bar X:\bar Y:\bar Z]=[1:0:0:0],
\tag{4}
\]

the original full slack is a finite sum of squares in the original bounded
tensor ring \(B_c\otimes_{\mathbb R}B_s\). All coefficient-opening rates and
all test rates in this chart are allowed. No quotient by an infinitesimal
test quantity or mixed tensor element is used.

The same statement with \(E(v_0^2)>0\) is the immediate positive-definite
four-normal exit described in section 7.

\hypertarget{event-normalization-and-the-complete-polynomial}{%
\subsubsection*{2. Event normalization and the complete polynomial}\label{p4ec1a51262f9:event-normalization-and-the-complete-polynomial}}

Use the full parent in coefficient order

\[
(d,\lambda,e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4,e_5,\alpha,\beta).
\]

The event amplitude \(R_2=vW-X+uvY-uZ\) is a pure tested unit in (4).
Divide the state by \(R_2\). This is a legal pure-support unit operation and
sets

\[
R_2=1,\qquad W=(1+X-uvY+uZ)/v.
\]

The entire opened-event contribution is now the \textbf{constant} \(4e_2\).
Call the resulting full raw slack (F\(X,Y,Z,u,v\)). Its coefficients are
polynomials in the point coefficients and Laurent polynomials in \(v\).
The only denominators are powers of the pure tested unit \(v\).

At \(u=0\), its state Hessian divided by two is

\[
M_0(v)=
\begin{pmatrix}
2(d+2e_1+2\beta)(1+v^2)&0&0\\
0&2d(1+v^2)+4(e_3+e_4)(1+v^2)+4e_5+4\alpha v^2
 &4v(e_5-\alpha)\\
0&4v(e_5-\alpha)&4\alpha+4e_5v^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5}
\]

Its lower block is positive definite if \(d>0\) and

\[
\alpha d+2\alpha e_3+2\alpha e_4+2\alpha e_5(1+v^2)
+e_5v^2(d+2e_3+2e_4)>0.
\]

Introduce \textbf{coefficient-side center variables} \(x,a,b,U\) by the formal
substitution

\[
X=u^2x,\quad Y=ua,\quad Z=ub,\quad U=u^2.
\]

The exact full identity is

\[
F(u^2x,ua,ub,u,v)=4e_2+u^2K(x,a,b,v,U),
\quad U=u^2.
\tag{6}
\]

Here \(K\) is polynomial in \(x,a,b,U\), with Laurent-polynomial \(v\)
coefficients. More strongly,

\[
K_x=U E_x,\qquad
\partial_x E_x\big|_{U=0}=4(d+2e_1+2\beta)(1+v^2)>0,
\tag{7}
\]

and (K\(x,a,b,v,0\)) is independent of \(x\). These statements have been
verified directly on all 105 terms of \(K\), starting from all 106 terms of
the normalized full parent. The formal substitution defines the center;
it is never used to form \(X/u^2,Y/u,Z/u\) in the independent tensor.

\hypertarget{the-residue-center-and-positive-quartic-coefficient}{%
\subsubsection*{3. The residue center and positive quartic coefficient}\label{p4ec1a51262f9:the-residue-center-and-positive-quartic-coefficient}}

At the real residue, minimizing the positive state block in \(a,b\) gives
a scalar jet profile

\[
r(v)=\frac{16(1+v^2)^2}{v^2\det M_0(v)}E(v^2).
\tag{8}
\]

Physicality and (3) imply

\[
E(V)=q_{12}(V-v_0^2)^2.
\]

Consequently \(r(v_0)=r'(v_0)=0\), \(r''(v_0)>0\). The Hessian of

\[
K_0(a,b,v)=K(x,a,b,v,0)
\]

in (\(a,b,v\)) is positive definite at its unique local zero

\[
(a_0,b_0,v_0).
\]

This follows by completing the strictly positive (\(a,b\)) quadratic and
using \(r''(v_0)>0\), so it controls the full Hessian, not just its
determinant. Equation \(E_x=0\) then determines a unique \(x_0\) by (7).

Set \(q=(x,a,b,v)\). The Jacobian of

\[
\mathcal E(q,U)=(E_x,K_a,K_b,K_v)
\tag{9}
\]

in \(q\) at \((c_0,q_0,0)\) is block upper triangular. Its top-left entry is
the positive number in (7), and its lower \(3\times3\) block is the positive
Hessian just proved. Thus it is invertible. The Nash implicit function
theorem gives a coefficient-only local branch \(q_*(c,U)\).

The optimized function

\[
k(c,U)=K(q_*(c,U),U)
\]

satisfies

\[
k(c_0,0)=0,\qquad
\gamma:=k_U(c_0,0)
=\frac{16(1+v_0^2)^2}{v_0^2\det M_0(v_0)}B(v_0^2)>0.
\tag{10}
\]

For clarity, (10) uses the whole parent determinant and the positive state
block. The event-normalized four-by-four matrix has determinant

\[
\det P/v^2.
\]

Its state Schur complement is its determinant divided by the positive
three-state determinant. At \(e_2=0\), (2) therefore gives (8), and at the
zero in (3) its next \(U^2\) coefficient is exactly (10). The equations
for \(a,b\) optimize the order-\(U\) state terms; the divided equation for

\(x\) optimizes the order-\(U^2\) state term. No inverse of this state block
is used in the final tensor identity; this real calculation only proves
strict positivity of the residue coefficient.

\hypertarget{two-coefficient-only-centers}{%
\subsubsection*{4. Two coefficient-only centers}\label{p4ec1a51262f9:two-coefficient-only-centers}}

Put \(\kappa=k(c,0)\). It is a pure coefficient infinitesimal, with either
sign allowed.

If \(\kappa\ge0\), take \(q_c=q_*(c,0)\) and \(U_c=0\). The ordinary
derivatives \(K_a,K_b,K_v\) vanish there, and \(E_x=0\).

If \(\kappa<0\), solve

\[
\mathcal E(q_c,U_c)=0,\qquad
K(q_c,U_c)+U_cK_U(q_c,U_c)=0.
\tag{11}
\]

The full Jacobian at the real base point is block upper triangular with
the invertible block (9) and final diagonal entry \(2\gamma>0\). The
derivatives of the final equation in \(q\) vanish at the base point.
Hence there is a unique nearby coefficient-only solution.

It satisfies \(U_c>0\). Indeed along the normal branch the last equation
is \(g(U)=k(U)+Uk'(U)=0\), with \(g(0)=\kappa<0\) and derivative having
positive residue \(2\gamma\). This argument is local and transfers to
the real closed coefficient field. It uses no comparison with the
independent tested \(u\).

Define

\[
\mu=4e_2+U_cK(q_c,U_c).
\tag{12}
\]

This is nonnegative: realize \(u_c=\sqrt{U_c}\), \(v=v_c\),

\[
(X,Y,Z)=(U_cx_c,u_ca_c,u_cb_c),
\]

and restore \(W\) by the event normalization. This is an actual
coefficient-side state and measurement pair, so full physicality applies.

\hypertarget{exact-full-tail-identity-in-the-original-independent-tensor}{%
\subsubsection*{5. Exact full-tail identity in the original independent tensor}\label{p4ec1a51262f9:exact-full-tail-identity-in-the-original-independent-tensor}}

Keep \(q_c\) fixed and put

\[
N_x=X-u^2x_c,\quad N_y=Y-ua_c,\quad N_z=Z-ub_c,
\quad T=v-v_c,\quad N_v=uT.
\tag{13}
\]

All these belong to the original bounded tensor ring. No small quantity
has been divided out.

The state dependence of the normalized full parent is exactly quadratic.
Consequently its entire expansion is

\[
F=4e_2+U K(q_c\text{ with }v\text{ variable},U)
+U E_xN_x+uK_aN_y+uK_bN_z+N_s^TMN_s,
\quad N_s=(N_x,N_y,N_z),
\tag{14}
\]

where the derivative functions on the right are evaluated at

\((x_c,a_c,b_c,v,U)\). The independent checker verifies this identity,
all three linear coefficients, and the complete quadratic remainder.

At \(v=v_c,U=U_c\) all four center equations vanish. Exact difference
division therefore writes the linear terms as combinations of

\[
N_x U(U-U_c),\quad N_yu(U-U_c),\quad N_zu(U-U_c),
\quad N_xuN_v,\quad N_yN_v,\quad N_zN_v.
\tag{15}
\]

The coefficient functions are bounded. Laurent denominators involve only
the pure units \(v,v_c\); differences of their powers are divided using
finite algebraic identities. Taylor division of the profile in \(v\)
gives an additional term \(N_v^2h\) and a cross term

\[
N_vu(U-U_c)h_1.
\tag{16}
\]

Thus all cross terms with the last scalar generator have an extra \(u\)
or \(U\), and have zero residue at every independent test rate.

For \(\kappa\ge0\), univariate division at \(U=0\) gives the exact form

\[
F=4e_2+\kappa u^2+G^T H G,
\quad G=(N_x,N_y,N_z,N_v,U).
\tag{17}
\]

For \(\kappa<0\), stationarity in (11) and (12) gives a double zero of
the fixed-center univariate profile at \(U_c\). Monic polynomial division
\(its coefficients may contain only the pure coefficient unit (v_c)\)
therefore gives

\[
F=\mu+G^THG,
\quad G=(N_x,N_y,N_z,N_v,U-U_c).
\tag{18}
\]

In both cases the residue Gram is positive definite. Its first block is
the positive state-\(X\) entry together with one half of the positive
Hessian of \(K_0(a,b,v)\); its last entry is \(\gamma>0\). The cross entries
to the last generator vanish by (15)--(16). This statement also accounts
for the possibly nonzero residue cross terms \(N_yN_v,N_zN_v\), which are
part of the positive full jet Hessian.

The bounded positive-residue Gram lemma from round5 now makes

\(G^THG\) an SOS in \(B_c\otimes B_s\). The remaining coefficients
\(e_2,\kappa,\mu\) are nonnegative pure coefficient quantities in their
respective branches and hence squares. Finally multiply the roots by the
original pure tested event amplitude to undo normalization. If normalized
physical slack rather than raw slack is desired, the norm and measurement
denominators are positive pure tested units on this chart and can likewise
be restored. This proves section 1.

\hypertarget{exact-scope-of-the-remaining-cases}{%
\subsubsection*{7. Exact scope of the remaining cases}\label{p4ec1a51262f9:exact-scope-of-the-remaining-cases}}

If \(E(v_0^2)>0\), the original four-normal expansion in

\((X,Y,Z,u)\) after event normalization has a positive residue Gram.
The term \(4e_2\) remains separate, so this strict finite-angle exit needs
no critical center. If the tested state has positive residue loss, the
scalar bounded-positive-residue lemma applies immediately.

The other projective and source cells are proved by the positive-scale union in Section~\ref{component:pc0b2849f8d09}.


\section{\texorpdfstring{A full-parent certificate at the finite-ratio corner}{A full-parent certificate at the finite-ratio corner}}

\subsection*{A positive-d terminal at a finite-ratio distance-four corner}\label{p03b551f2a9a0:a-positive-d-terminal-at-a-finite-ratio-distance-four-corner}}

25 September 2026. This is new round6 work. It uses the same coefficient-only
critical-center argument as Section~\ref{component:p4ec1a51262f9}, with a
separate exact full-tail calculation. It does not cover ratio endpoints or
flat jet boundaries.

\hypertarget{hypotheses-and-conclusion}{%
\subsubsection*{1. Hypotheses and conclusion}\label{p03b551f2a9a0:hypotheses-and-conclusion}}

The physical nonnegative coefficient vector belongs to \(B_c^9\), where
\(B_c\) is the bounded valuation ring of a real closed extension of the
reals. The independent test belongs to the corresponding ring \(B_s\) of
another real closed extension. Suppose

\[
\lambda=0\quad\hbox{exactly},\qquad d_0>0,\quad\alpha_0=0.
\tag{1}
\]

All other coordinates may have arbitrary nonnegative residues or opening
rates. At this distance-four residue face write

\[
Q_{c_0}(U,V)=q_{20}U^2+q_{11}UV+q_{02}V^2
+q_{21}U^2V+q_{12}UV^2+q_{22}U^2V^2.
\]

For a real \(t_0\ne0\), assume

\[
q_{20},q_{02}>0,\quad
q_{20}t_0^4+q_{11}t_0^2+q_{02}=0,
\quad q_{21}t_0^4+q_{12}t_0^2>0.
\tag{2}
\]

Let the exact quadratic jet \(J_4(t_0)\) have kernel

\[
(w_0,x_0,y_0,1),
\]

and suppose its principal block in the variables (\(W,X,Y\)) is positive
definite. Equivalently the normalized kernel is simple and this specified
complement is positive. This real rank/complement condition is stated
explicitly; no unverified support census is used to remove it.

Then the original full slack is SOS in \(B_c\otimes B_s\) for every
independent test with

\[
\bar u=\bar v=0,\qquad
[\bar W:\bar X:\bar Y:\bar Z]=[w_0:0:0:1],
\tag{3}
\]

with the following un-divided normal quantities:

\[
X-vx_0,\quad Y-vy_0,\quad u-vt_0
\]

are the normal quantities handled below. \textbf{No asymptotic comparison or
extra smallness assumption on these three differences is needed.} They
automatically have zero residue in (3), and the positive Gram is proved
uniformly on all their independent rates. In particular this statement
does not assume that \(u/v\) is bounded or that its residue is \(t_0\): the
construction is an identity in the un-divided variables. The selected

\(t_0\) specifies a coefficient-side jet center, not a condition on an
independent mixed or pure-tested ratio.

\hypertarget{full-polynomial-and-residue-hessian}{%
\subsubsection*{2. Full polynomial and residue Hessian}\label{p03b551f2a9a0:full-polynomial-and-residue-hessian}}

The pure-tested amplitude (vY-Z) is a unit in (3). Normalize it to (-1),
so

\[
Z=1+vY.
\]

The whole opened alpha packet becomes \(4\alpha(1+u^2)\). Substituting

\[
X=vx,\quad Y=vy,\quad u=vt,\quad W=w,\quad V=v^2
\]

formally, on the coefficient side only, gives the exact full identity

\[
F=4\alpha+v^2K(x,y,t,w,V).
\tag{4}
\]

The complete \(K\) is polynomial. Its constant \(V\) coefficient is

\[
\begin{aligned}
K_0={}&4e_1x^2+4e_2(w-x-t)^2+4e_3(tw-y)^2+4e_4y^2\\
&+4e_5(tw-y-1)^2+4\beta(x+t)^2\\
&+2d\{(tw-y)^2+(x+t)^2-2tw\}+4\alpha t^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

At the real residue the last term vanishes, and this is precisely
\(J_4(t)[w,x,y,1]\). The stipulated positive three-state complement makes
the \((w,x,y)\) Hessian positive. Its minimized scalar is

\[
\frac{16(q_{20}t^4+q_{11}t^2+q_{02})}{\det M_J(t)},
\tag{6}
\]

where \(M_J\) is that positive principal jet block. Physicality and (2)
make the numerator a positive multiple of \((t^2-t_0^2)^2\). Therefore
the full four-variable Hessian of \(K_0\) at

\[
q_0=(x_0,y_0,t_0,w_0)
\]

is positive definite. Its optimized next coefficient is

\[
\gamma=\partial_V K(q_0,0)
=\frac{16(q_{21}t_0^4+q_{12}t_0^2)}{\det M_J(t_0)}>0.
\tag{7}
\]

The determinant calculation is the full state Schur-complement calculation,
after scaling \(X,Y\) by \(v\). At the zero of (6), derivatives of the
positive state determinant do not affect the next coefficient. This
calculation proves the real sign in (7); no state-block inverse is inserted
into a tensor certificate.

\hypertarget{exact-centers-including-both-signs}{%
\subsubsection*{3. Exact centers, including both signs}\label{p03b551f2a9a0:exact-centers-including-both-signs}}

Solve \(\nabla_qK(q_*(c,V),V)=0\) by the Nash implicit function theorem, justified by the positive
four-Hessian. Let \(k(c,V)=K(q_*(c,V),V)\), and put

\[
\kappa=k(c,0).
\]

If \(\kappa\ge0\), take \(q_c=q_*(c,0),V_c=0\). If \(\kappa<0\), solve

\[
\nabla_qK(q_c,V_c)=0,\qquad
K(q_c,V_c)+V_c K_V(q_c,V_c)=0.
\tag{8}
\]

Its residue Jacobian has the positive four-Hessian and final pivot
\(2\gamma>0\), with zero lower-left block. Hence the nearby solution
exists in the coefficient field and satisfies \(V_c>0\), by the local
monotonicity of \(k(V)+Vk'(V)\) from its negative value at zero.

In the latter branch the pure coefficient scalar

\[
\mu=4\alpha+V_cK(q_c,V_c)
\tag{9}
\]

is nonnegative. It is the original physical slack at the actual
coefficient-side realization \(v=\sqrt{V_c}\),

\[
(u,X,Y,W)=(v t_c,v x_c,v y_c,w_c),\qquad Z=1+vY.
\]

Thus the sign argument is valid for arbitrary physical coefficients, not
only coefficient vectors already at contact.

\hypertarget{verified-full-tail-grouping-without-division-by-v}{%
\subsubsection*{4. Verified full-tail grouping without division by v}\label{p03b551f2a9a0:verified-full-tail-grouping-without-division-by-v}}

Define the un-divided normals

\[
N_1=X-vx_c,\quad N_2=Y-vy_c,\quad N_3=u-vt_c,
\quad N_4=v(W-w_c).
\tag{10}
\]

Write \(z=W-w_c\) while expanding the full polynomial. Its four exact
linear coefficients in \(N_1,N_2,N_3,z\) are

\[
vK_x,\quad vK_y,\quad vK_t,\quad v^2K_w.
\]

Accordingly the whole linear part is \(v\nabla_qK(q_c,V)\cdot N\).
Every monomial of the full remainder belongs to

\[
(N_1,N_2,N_3,vz)^2.
\tag{11}
\]

The direct checker allocates all 55 monomials of that complete remainder
into (11). Terms such as \(N_3^2z^2\) retain \(z^2\) as a bounded
infinitesimal coefficient. Terms with one \(z\) and one other normal have
the required factor \(v\), unless they already have two of the first
three normals. Terms with \(z^2\) and no other normals have \(v^2\).
Consequently no step substitutes \(z=N_4/v\).

The exact resulting normal Gram has residue

\[
\tfrac12\operatorname{Hess}_{(x,y,t,w)}K_0(q_0)\succ0.
\tag{12}
\]

Since \(\nabla K(q_c,V_c)=0\), the linear coefficient polynomial is
divisible by \(V-V_c\). Its contribution couples \(N\) to the scalar
generator \(V-V_c\) with an extra \(v\), hence a zero-residue cross
coefficient. The fixed-center univariate profile gives, by exact monic
division, the two identities

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\kappa\ge0:&F=4\alpha+\kappa v^2+G^THG,
\quad G=(N_1,N_2,N_3,N_4,V),\\[2mm]
\kappa<0:&F=\mu+G^THG,
\quad G=(N_1,N_2,N_3,N_4,V-V_c).
\end{array}
\tag{13}
\]

The final Gram residue is the positive block (12) together with the
positive last entry \(\gamma\). The bounded positive-residue Gram lemma
proves it SOS, and the remaining pure nonnegative point scalars are
squares. Restoring the event normalization multiplies the roots by a
pure tested unit. This proves the claimed original-ring certificate for
every rate in (3).



\section{\texorpdfstring{Positive total scale: the finite-pitchfork case}{Positive total scale: the finite-pitchfork case}}
\label{component:pfb0cf454f653}


The September archive contains an explicit full-parent theorem for a
finite nonzero commuting edge, even though it does not contain the later
A5 terminal files under their recorded names. Its source hypotheses are
automatic on the ordinary and finite-pitchfork branch with
$d,C,A,D,\alpha$ units. This note proves that exact implication and
supplies explicit full-parent projective boundary forms. Thus that
subbundle of A5 is now mathematically audited. Weak-$\alpha$,
projective-pitchfork, and reciprocal terminal branches remain separate
obligations; there is no whole-A5 admission.

\begin{theorem}
Let the original normalized C1 or C2 source be bounded, nonnegative and
physical over a real closed coefficient extension of $\mathbb R$.
Assume $d,C,A,D,\alpha$ are positive bounded units. Retain its exact
canonical zero coefficients, in particular $\lambda=0$, and every
other nonnegative original coefficient $n,m,\beta$. Then its entire
parent is tensor-SOS at every independent projective testing pair and
every real or complex testing state.
\end{theorem}

The proof uses the admitted separated strict-Gram lemma, the freshly
audited noncommuting-residue theorem A6, and the preserved finite-edge
critical-center theorem whose proof is reviewed below. All source and
testing rates remain independent.

\subsection*{Exact source fit of the recovered finite-edge theorem}

The archived notation is
$(d,\lambda,e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4,e_5,\alpha,\beta)
=(d,\lambda,n,m,C,A,D,\alpha,\beta)$.
The theorem requires $\lambda=0$ exactly, $\bar d>0$, $\bar m=0$,
a finite real $v_0\ne0$, and positivity conditions on the residue
determinant at $u=0$. Bars denote real residues in this section.
Its test state is the $W$ ray. It permits arbitrary independent
nonnegative opening rates of all the original coefficients.

At $\lambda=m=0$, the exact determinant polynomial is
\[
 Q(U,V)=U E(V)+U^2 B(V),\quad
 E(V)=q_{10}+q_{11}V+q_{12}V^2,\quad
 B(V)=q_{20}+q_{21}V+q_{22}V^2.
\]
Use the inherited positive quantities
\begin{align*}
 t_1&=d+2(n+D),&t_2&=d+2(\alpha+m+A),\\
 t_3&=d+2(C+D),&t_4&=d+2(\alpha+\beta+m),\\
 t_5&=d+2(\beta+n),&t_6&=d+2(\alpha+C+A),\\
 t_7&=d+2(\beta+n+m),&t_8&=d+2(C+A+D).
\end{align*}
All eight have positive bounded-unit residues. At the residue $m=0$,
the required coefficients are
\[
 q_{10}=4\alpha A t_7t_3>0,\quad
 q_{12}=4CDt_5t_2>0,\quad
 q_{21}=4(\beta n t_3t_2+CA t_1t_4)>0,
\]
and $q_{20},q_{22}\geq0$. Therefore $B(v_0^2)>0$ for every
$v_0\ne0$. Physicality implies $E(V)\geq0$ for all real $V>0$:

\end{aligned}\tag{WA2}

with \(\alpha=D=0\), the other Laurent terms are

prove all the asserted unit bounds without a source-contact assumption.

\end{aligned}\tag{WA4}

 \tag{WA5}

 \tag{WA6}

\section{\texorpdfstring{A source-exact projective pitchfork for C1}{A source-exact projective pitchfork for C1}}

\paragraph{Theorem and precise source domain.}
Take a bounded physical canonical C1 source with $d=1$,
$\lambda=n=0$, $m,C,A,\alpha$ positive bounded units and
$D,\beta\geq0$ infinitesimal. Assume the original actual finite
noncommuting stationary zero contact, and retain every original
source gap and intersection. Then the entire parent has a finite
sum-of-squares certificate in $K_c\otimes_{\mathbb R}K_t$ for
every independent projective test and every real or complex state.

source domain. The two cannot vanish together at an actual contact.

\subsubsection*{Opening-safe source entry}

Write the positive source contact as $(u_c,w_c)=(c,1/v_c)$,
and $V=w_c^2$. The retained positive-$d$ balance proof gives
$c$ a positive bounded unit and $V$ a positive infinitesimal.
Its second stationarity equation, in the notation of that proof, is
\[
 a_3=V^{-2}\{mDa_2/c^2+\beta ma_4+Da_5+\beta a_7c^2\},
 \tag{PP1}
\]
where all $a_i$ are positive bounded units. Therefore
\[
 D=V^2\delta,\quad\beta=V^2\eta,\qquad
 \delta,\eta\geq0\text{ bounded},\qquad
 \delta+\eta\text{ a positive unit}.
 \tag{PP2}
\]
No inverse of either opening is used.

Normalize the source state by $Z_c=1$. Set
\[
 p=W_c/w_c,\qquad y=Y_c/w_c,\qquad x=X_c/V.
 \tag{PP3}
\]
All three are bounded source elements. To prove this without a
shape hypothesis, restrict the reciprocal full parent PP6 to
$(W,Y,X)$ at $w=0$, and then take residues. Its positive hard form is
\[
 H_u(W,Y,X)=m(W+uY)^2+C(uW-Y)^2
       +(A+\alpha+1/2)(1+u^2)Y^2+(1+u^2)X^2/2.
 \tag{PP4}
\]
It is positive definite for every bounded real $u$. Scale the two
$W,Y$ kernel equations at the source by $w_c$, and the $X$
equation by $V$. In the unknowns $(p,y,x)$ the resulting bounded
matrix has a block triangular residue: its $p,y$ block is the
positive $W,Y$ block of PP4, and its $x$ pivot is $(1+c^2)/2$.
The terms $(\beta-D)/V=V(\eta-\delta)$ are bounded. All right
hand sides are bounded, proving PP3 by an ordinary source-field
matrix inverse with unit determinant.

Let $q_{01},q_{10},q_{21},h$ be the original Laurent coefficients.
Their first three residues are positive units because $m,C,A,\alpha$
are units. The actual zero and angular stationarity equations,
using PP2, give
\[
 \bar q_{01}=\bar q_{21}\bar c^{\,4},\qquad
 \bar h=-2\bar q_{21}\bar c^{\,2}.
 \tag{PP5}
\]
Every other nonconstant Laurent contribution apart from $q_{10}w^2$
vanishes in this residue calculation. These are equations wholly in
the coefficient field followed by its real residue map.

\subsubsection*{A complete local normal and divided-difference argument}

It remains locally to treat testing $w$ infinitesimal or zero and
$u$ with the same positive real residue as $c$. The full reciprocal

\begin{split}

 \tag{PP7}

 \tag{PP8}

bounded entries and residue PP4 at $u=\bar c$. Parity and direct
inspection of PP6 give bounded polynomials $a_W,a_Y,a_X$ such that

 \ell_W=w a_W(u,b),\qquad \ell_Y=w a_Y(u,b),\qquad

 \tag{PP9}

Actual state stationarity gives $a_W(c,V)=a_Y(c,V)=0$ and

 \tag{PP10}

       +(u-c)(b-V)J_{11}(b)+(b-V)^2J_{02}(b).

\subsubsection*{The actual source has a bounded shape chart}

The two hard state equations, scaled in the source field by
