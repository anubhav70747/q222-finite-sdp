Q222: VERIFICATION AND PROOF REFINEMENT
22 October 2026

RESULT

I found no unresolved mathematical defect in the retained proof under its
stated hypotheses. The refined document contains the complete qualitative
argument for finite semidefinite representability of the full eight-coordinate
Q222 set, together with the explicit local certificates that the argument
requires. It is a mathematical audit and refinement, not a formal proof-assistant
verification or a claim of external peer review.

The main theorem is the existence of finite integers N,m and real symmetric
matrices A0,A1,...,A8,B1,...,Bm of order N such that

  Q222 = {q in R^8 : there exists y in R^m,
          A0 + sum(q_i A_i) + sum(y_j B_j) is positive semidefinite}.

The proof supplies no numerical bound on N and no verified assertion that all
positive-semidefinite blocks can have order two. In particular, the conclusion
must not be described as a proved second-order-cone representation.

WHAT "COMPLETE" AND "MINIMAL" MEAN IN THIS EDITION

The theorem and its short global argument appear first. The subsequent chapters
prove the physical reduction, tensor-ring lemmas, source/contact reduction,
native signs and parameter entry, common algebra, and exhaustive source cover.
The certificate appendix gives the complete full-slack formulas for the cases
in that cover. Those formulas are part of the proof, rather than optional
computer evidence.

The new PDF has 439 pages, compared with 798 pages in the recovered
complete proof. Some reduction comes from a consolidated layout; the substantive
reductions are identified below. This is a shorter complete version of this
proof route, not a proof that no shorter argument exists. The short opening
alone must not be presented as a self-contained proof of the canonical local
certificate theorem.

The source package compiles the full proof without the recovered archive. Its
proof does not run a computer algebra system. Independent diagnostic programs
and their receipts are supplied separately for reproducibility.

PROOF STRUCTURE

1. Finite quantum model.

   Simultaneous dilation of each party's two binary measurements, the
   two-projection block decomposition, and the real-state reduction give an
   exact two-real-qubit model. Convexification in eight real coordinates needs
   at most nine pure components. Therefore Q222 is compact, convex and
   semialgebraic. Its fixed finite defining formula transfers to arbitrary
   real closed fields. No infinite-dimensional Hilbert-space definition over
   a non-Archimedean field is substituted for that formula.

2. The stronger separated-slack assertion.

   Let Kc and Kt be separately labelled real closed extensions of R. For every
   affine Bell functional f nonnegative on the transferred quantum set over
   Kc, and every transferred testing point q over Kt, the proof establishes

       [f,q] = f0 tensor 1 + sum(f_i tensor q_i)
               in Sigma(Kc tensor_R Kt)^2.

   The tensor ring is not ordered. No embedding in a field and no algebraic
   disjointness of Kc and Kt is assumed. Every inverse and radical used as a
   normalization belongs to a single field factor unless a separate polynomial
   identity explicitly supplies the relevant tensor expression.

3. Strict bounded Gram matrices.

   If Bc and Bt are the rings of bounded elements, their residue maps take
   values in R. An element of Bc tensor_R Bt with strictly positive real
   residue is an exact finite sum of elementary-tensor squares. The proof is
   the finite reserve identity TF4, with positive pure-field factors.

   Consequently, a symmetric matrix over Bc tensor_R Bt with positive-definite
   real residue gives a sum of squares when evaluated on any tensor root
   column. The roots need not be bounded. The finite rank-one decomposition
   TF6 proves this without Cholesky inversion in the mixed tensor ring.

4. Source geometry and entry.

   The nonsignalling vertex argument, event cone, closed-face matrix lifts,
   contact determinant support, strict source-field contact curvature and
   extreme-source argument reduce the remaining proof to two contact families.
   The exact native-entry argument supplies the signs and original stationary
   coefficients used in the local certificates.

   Determinant information is used for geometry and contact curvature. It is
   not treated as an automatic certificate for the entire Bell slack.

5. Complete local certificates and coverage.

   The retained arguments cover positive total source scale; all C1 source
   faces; both-weak and strong C2 cases; the directed weak-C2 R1--R10 cover;
   noncommuting testing residues; projective testing endpoints; and every
   state chart. The local expressions keep the scalar part, both first state
   normals, and the remaining hard state quadratic form. Literal permitted
   zero weights, including n=0 and beta=0 where applicable, are retained.

   Source and testing infinitesimal rates remain independent. Boundedness is
   proved by explicit finite separated coefficient formulas, not by comparing
   an element of Kc with an element of Kt. Finite-cap arguments retain whole
   original event excesses and justify the limiting kernel and remainder.

6. Assembly and external criterion.

   If f = sum_j a_j f_j, q = sum_i w_i q_i, and
   [f_j,q_i] = sum_k h_ijk^2, then exactly

       [f,q] = sum_ijk ((sqrt(a_j) tensor sqrt(w_i)) h_ijk)^2.

   The testing weights sum to one, so the affine constant is preserved.
   Positive pure-source and pure-testing factors can be canceled by their
   separate inverse square roots. Zero weights are never inverted.

   Scheiderer's Corollary 3.19 then gives finite semidefinite representability
   from the tensor-SOS assertion for all real closed extensions. The stronger
   two-field result in the manuscript includes the required case of two
   labelled copies of one arbitrary extension.

  weights, the one- and two-hard support calculations, both three-hard
  Laurent restrictions, the arbitrary-ratio four-matching terminal, and the
  finite increasing-matching induction. A compression pass restored the
  explicit identification q11=h and stated d=0 before using strict t_i>0.

* One source-geometry chapter replaces repeated source, closed-face and
  structural-entry arguments. Its SG17--SG22 prove coercivity, the strict
  five-variable source-field Hessian, contact orientation and forbidden
  event deletion at exactly the strength needed by native entry. Strict
  source-field positivity is not silently strengthened to strict residue.

* A new bounded-a weak-C2 proof replaces the old WC and R9 branches. Its
  standalone version is five pages. It prints the complete four-state
  identity, the hard block and both normals, the physical source inequality
  controlling the delicate Schur complement, and all projective endpoint
  identities. It includes infinitesimal a and literal n=0. This is a proved
  replacement, not a proposed shortcut awaiting a normal or remote-chart
  calculation.

* In C14.2, the CT proof requires only a positive bounded unit for its testing
  ratio c; equality of its residue with the source parameter L is unnecessary.
  The redundant intermediate ratio alternatives can therefore be removed.
  The extreme-ratio CS identities, bounded-state CF, and bounded-H HG/HP
  branches remain.

* In C14.3, the exact DG/LD identities and source deficits, the completed-J
  conductor, the DS3 unbounded-state exit, and the SB remote complement replace
  narrower overlapping admissions. The short JF-to-DG derivation keeps both
  normal terms and every scalar completion explicitly.

* In C14.4, BN already covers every bounded tau, including the former bounded
  n_s SN terminal. SN is retained only for the exact coefficient dictionary
  used by BN and UN. The six source cells and their joins remain explicit.

* Superseded status summaries, unfinished alternative routes, duplicate
  dictionaries and checker-only provenance paragraphs were removed where
  they have no mathematical role. Required definitions and formulas were

MATHEMATICAL REVIEW AND ITS LIMITS

The analytic review followed the operative proof bodies, not only their
theorem statements or old PASS receipts. It examined the source-entry
hypotheses, exact state-normal formulas, coefficient divisibility, strict
residue margins, finite-cap justifications, literal boundary weights,
source/test separation, and the source/testing/state joins.

The four review scopes cover the global physical/tensor/source framework;
shared conductors and critical walls; C1 and the unbounded-b C2 chain;
positive-scale and strong-C2 branches; directed weak-C2; and the newly
compressed core. The separate reports identify their imports and limits.

Not every coefficient in every displayed polynomial has been independently
expanded. Some retained remote determinant formulas were checked structurally
and in their proof context rather than through a separate symbolic expansion.
There is no proof-assistant certificate of the entire theorem. A mathematical
reader should treat this as a carefully audited, explicit written proof,
with additional exact algebra evidence and a clear record of its scope.

A tempting generic shortcut was rejected: a determinant SOS or a cleared
matrix identity does not authorize cancellation of a mixed tensor factor.
The supplementary tensor audit gives a counterexample to an automatic
bounded-conductor inference. This is not a counterexample to Q222. The
refined proof retains the full-slack local certificates that avoid the false
inference.
