Verified by direct derivation, with no PASS receipt used as a premise:
1. Standard-part maps exist for bounded elements of any ordered real
   closed extension of the full real field. Dedekind completeness of R
   is used. This must not be silently generalized to an arbitrary base
   real closed subfield.
2. Bc tensor_R Bt embeds into Kc tensor_R Kt by exactness of tensoring
   vector spaces. The residue kernel is mc tensor Bt + Bc tensor mt.
   Algebraic disjointness of the two fields is unnecessary.
3. A strictly positive real residue has an exact finite positive-pure-
   tensor decomposition. Each summand has an elementary tensor square
   root, so the scalar positive-residue lemma is stronger than ordinary
   finite SOS and does not involve a mixed radical.
4. Real congruence plus the printed diagonal-dominance decomposition
   proves strict bounded Gram. Its roots may be unbounded and dependent.
   The Gram coefficients themselves must belong to Bc tensor Bt and
   its real residue must be positive definite, not merely semidefinite.
5. Strictly positive pure-source/pure-testing factors cancel legally by
   their pure-field inverse square roots. Literal zeros and general
   mixed factors are not covered by this statement.
6. PSD matrix trace pairing and finite conic/convex closure are exact
   tensor identities. Zero convex/conic weights need no inversions.
7. The definition of the valid-affine cone transfers exactly by its
   first-order formula. No non-Archimedean topological compactness is
   invoked by this step.
8. Cor3.19 is correctly applied in arbitrary dimension. The coefficient-
   first convention is carried to the paper's point-first convention by
   the factor flip. The opening explicitly allows two labelled copies
   of one K, which is the case required by the criterion.
9. Unrestricted finite tensor SOS for all K/f/q suffices for qualitative
   existence; no pre-existing common rank or square-count bound is
   required. A bound d via Theorem3.10 would require a common tensor-
   rank bound on each root. Numerical local square counts alone do not
   supply it.

Decisive boundary example (not a Bell-slack counterexample):
K=real closure of R(tau), tau positive infinitesimal.
Delta=tau tensor 1 - 1 tensor tau has residue0 and diagonal value0.
The automorphism tau -> 2tau extends to K, and the tensor homomorphism
x tensor y -> x sigma(y) maps Delta to -tau. Hence Delta is not SOS.
Therefore positive semidefinite residue or nonnegative diagonal testing
cannot replace strict residue or a genuine separated tensor identity.
