\section{Introduction}

The simplest Bell experiment has two measurement choices and two outcomes
at each site. Its quantum correlations depend on eight numbers: four
individual outcome averages and four joint correlators. A finite
semidefinite representation of this set would place every Bell inequality
in the scenario on the same exact optimization domain.

Several descriptions already make this boundary accessible. The
Navascu\'es--Pironio--Ac\'in hierarchy bounds quantum values with
successively larger moment matrices~\cite{NPA}. The reduction to real
two-qubit experiments gives a compact polynomial model~\cite{Masanes},
and the extreme correlations admit an analytical
characterization~\cite{BarizienBancal}. A polynomial model alone leaves
the semidefinite question unresolved: some compact convex semialgebraic
sets have no finite semidefinite lift~\cite{Shadows}.

We prove that the complete quantum set in this experiment has a finite
semidefinite representation. The key structure appears at a supporting
Bell inequality. Its state matrix has three positive transverse
directions, while its determinant reduces to eight angular monomials
with nonnegative coefficients. At contact, those coefficients balance
in the exponent plane. A few elementary identities turn that balance
into exact squares.

The singular measurement limits require a further step. As measurements
become compatible, both the angular terms and the couplings to the state
can vanish. We retain their exact contact relations and combine
complementary positive coefficients before rescaling. This produces
bounded Gram matrices with positive real residue even when the Bell
coefficients and the tested experiment vary independently.
Scheiderer's tensor criterion then gives a finite lift~\cite{Scheiderer}.
The argument below develops these mechanisms; the accompanying
\emph{Complete proofs} establishes their source-dependent hypotheses
and every singular boundary case.

\section{Discussion}

For the two-dimensional tilted-CHSH projection, an exact matrix
representation has a particularly simple form. With $t=a_0$ and
$s=E_{00}+E_{01}+E_{10}-E_{11}$, that projection is
\[
 |t|\le1,\qquad t^2+s^2/4\le2,
\]
represented by
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}\sqrt2+t&s/2\\s/2&\sqrt2-t\end{pmatrix}
 \oplus(1-t)\oplus(1+t)\succeq0.
\]
The matching Bell bound and two-qubit realization establish both
inclusions~\cite{BampsPironio}; they are derived in the accompanying
\emph{Explicit semidefinite representations and local certificates for Q222}.

For all eight coordinates, the existence proof supplies finite
matrices with real algebraic entries. Evaluating a useful such pencil
requires a common finite collection of functions for the local
certificates. The singular identities identify the functions that
must survive this construction. Once extracted, the same feasible
region will optimize every Bell functional in the scenario exactly.
