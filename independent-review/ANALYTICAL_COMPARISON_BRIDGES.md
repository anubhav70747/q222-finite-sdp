# Two analytical consequences of the literature comparison

27 October 2026. These are scoped deductions from identified inputs, not a new global lift theorem. The existing [contact matching](SUN_WANG_YU_CONTACT_MATCH.md) and all original proof packages are retained.

## 1. Eliminate the local behavior by one interval test

Write a normalized nonsignalling vector as P=(A0,A1,B0,B1,E00,E01,E10,E11). The local polytope L is given by the following 24 affine facets:

\[
g_{abxy}(P)=\frac{1+aA_x+bB_y+abE_{xy}}4\ge0
\quad(a,b=\pm1;\ x,y=0,1),
\]

\[
g_\epsilon(P)=2-\sum_{xy}\epsilon_{xy}E_{xy}\ge0,
\qquad\epsilon_{xy}\in\{\pm1\},\quad\prod_{xy}\epsilon_{xy}=-1.
\]

Completeness of the local inequalities uses [Fine's local-model criterion](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.48.291), together with positivity and nonsignalling. The checker below tests facet arithmetic; it does not independently prove this classical characterization.

Fix a candidate real-qubit behavior q. Define, over these 24 facets,

\[
L(P,q)=\max\left(\{0\}\cup\left\{\frac{g_j(P)}{g_j(q)}:g_j(q)<0\right\}\right),
\]

\[
U(P,q)=\min\left(\{1\}\cup\left\{\frac{g_j(P)}{g_j(q)}:g_j(q)>0\right\}\right).
\]

**Proposition.** There exist τ∈[0,1] and l∈L with P=τq+(1−τ)l if and only if

\[
L(P,q)\le U(P,q),\qquad
g_j(q)=0\Longrightarrow g_j(P)\ge0. \tag{1}
\]

**Proof.** For τ<1, put l=(P−τq)/(1−τ). Affinity gives

\[
g_j(P)-\tau g_j(q)=(1-\tau)g_j(l).
\]

Thus the local facets are exactly linear inequalities in τ. Dividing according to the sign of g_j(q) gives (1). At τ=1, the inequalities instead say that P−q belongs to the recession cone of L. Since L is a bounded polytope, that cone is {0}; hence P=q, which is precisely the permitted endpoint. This also proves sufficiency when the feasible interval contains only 1. The endpoint τ=0 is ordinary local membership. ∎

Combine this with [Sun–Wang–Yu, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34364v1): P is quantum exactly when some member q of their five-angle real-qubit family passes (1). Explicitly,

\[
A_x=\cos\theta\cos a_x,\quad B_y=\cos\theta\cos b_y,\quad
E_{xy}=\cos a_x\cos b_y+\sin\theta\sin a_x\sin b_y,
\]

with θ∈[0,π/2] and four measurement angles. The local variables and τ have been eliminated. The remaining dependence on the five angles is nonlinear. This is a useful alternative implementation of the decomposition theorem, not a priority claim or a finite affine SDP construction. The paper already gives a more specialized weight-free complete criterion in Appendix E; its sparse two-local-vertex ansatz is not established as a universal replacement for the local polytope. Using all local facets here avoids that sparse-ansatz restriction.

For affine support functions this does not reduce the global positivity obligation: q already ranges over the real-qubit generators, whose convex hull contains L. Positivity on all q is equivalent to positivity on all their mixtures. Membership search and universal dual certification are different operations.

## 2. A small square count does not give a uniform trigonometric library

[Dritschel, Theorem 4.1, v4](https://arxiv.org/html/1811.06005v4) supplies at most 2d2 analytic matrix squares for a nonnegative matrix-valued trigonometric polynomial Q of degree (d1,d2). Their degrees are bounded by (max(dA,m),2d2−1), with a finite input-dependent m. The statement preceding the theorem explicitly warns that input degree alone gives no uniform degree bound. A separate-field tensor argument is not the reason this fails to be a fixed Gram SDP: a uniform finite real feature basis would suffice directly.

We can sharpen the comparison on this repository's own physical family. Use the already proved bounds and notation in [NATIVE_MULTIPLIER_OBSTRUCTION](NATIVE_MULTIPLIER_OBSTRUCTION.md):

\[
B_T=A_0B_0-A_0B_1+A_1B_0+A_1B_1+(1-T)(A_1+B_0),
\quad\ell_T=4-2T,\quad w_T=\ell_T+4T^3.
\]

For 0<T≤1/100, w_T exceeds the quantum maximum. The 4×4 Bell slack Q_T=w_TI−B_T is therefore positive on the two-torus. Its trigonometric bidegree is (1,1). Dritschel gives a factorization into at most two analytic matrix squares with degrees (N_T,1), for some finite N_T.

**Corollary.** The minimum possible first-coordinate degree in such factorizations tends to infinity as T decreases to zero. More generally, no fixed finite ordinary trigonometric feature list certifies all these slacks in the original testing frame.

**Proof.** Suppose a sequence T→0 admitted a common finite degree bound N. In the SG2 chart put

\[
z_1(u)=\frac{1+iu}{1-iu},\quad z_2(v)=\frac{1+iv}{1-iv},\quad
\Delta=(1+u^2)(1+v^2).
\]

The cleared parent is F_T=Δ ψᵀQ_Tψ. Multiplying the complex matrix-square roots by √Δ and separating real and imaginary parts expresses F_T in a fixed real PSD Gram space generated by

\[
\Re[\sqrt\Delta\,z_1^i z_2^j]\psi_l,
\quad\Im[\sqrt\Delta\,z_1^i z_2^j]\psi_l,
\qquad0\le i\le N,\quad0\le j\le1,\quad1\le l\le4.
\]

Every angular feature is smooth near (0,0). The [existing smooth-feature theorem](SMOOTH_FEATURE_OBSTRUCTION.md) excludes such certificates at w=ℓ_T+κT³ for sufficiently small T and any bounded nonnegative κ. Taking κ=4 contradicts the assumed sequence. The coefficients of the Gram may depend arbitrarily on T; the cited theorem allows that dependence. This proves the corollary. ∎

This uses the preserved smooth-feature theorem and exact doubly tilted upper bound as inputs; it is not a fresh audit of their full proofs. It does not refute pointwise Dritschel factorization: each source can still use a larger finite degree. It also does not refute K364, whose fractional testing functions need not be smooth at the commuting origin, or an arbitrary finite SDP lift without this angular-state factorization.

## What remains useful for the constructive program

The common contact lemma can shorten the finite-contact argument. The interval test simplifies exact membership searches. The degree corollary prevents spending effort on a uniform ordinary trigonometric replacement already excluded by our retained result. A new sufficient fractional Gram construction, global source decomposition into admitted cones, or another adequate fixed finite representation is still needed for H1/H2.

The [exact checker](checks/comparison-bridges/verify_bridges.py) validates the facet arithmetic and endpoint examples, a rational physical test behavior, and the two-torus/Cayley identities. Its [receipt](checks/comparison-bridges/result.json) is a targeted algebra check, not an exhaustive membership computation. One low-cost reader checked the interval signs and endpoints; a second checked the smooth-feature corollary. The lead reconciled both with the primary sources. These bounded reviews did not re-audit the entire external papers or original qualitative theorem.
