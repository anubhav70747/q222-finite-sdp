# A triangle obstruction for C1 polynomial certificates

The C1 parent need not be a sum of ordinary polynomial squares. Three
coefficients of any proposed certificate must form a triangle; the exact
native source below violates this condition. The violation persists on an
open set of the strict C1 family.

Use the complete parent $F_c(u,v,\psi)$ of
[SG2](../latex/chapters/04_geometry.tex), with
$\psi=(W,X,Y,Z)$ and $\lambda=n=0$. At a positive finite native contact,
normalize $Z_c=1$ and put

$$
p=-X_c/u_c>0,\qquad q=Y_c/v_c>0.
$$

**Claim.** If $F_c$ is an ordinary real polynomial sum of squares, then

$$
p\sqrt{4\beta+2d},\qquad q\sqrt{4A},\qquad\sqrt{4\beta}
\tag{1}
$$

are the side lengths of a possibly degenerate triangle.

The parent has degree at most two in each angle and is homogeneous
quadratic in the state. Highest degrees cannot cancel in a sum of real
squares, so every root has the form

$$
R_j=\sum_{\ell\in\{W,X,Y,Z\}}
(a_{j\ell}+b_{j\ell}u+c_{j\ell}v+e_{j\ell}uv)\psi_\ell.
$$

The coefficient of $W^2$ is $4\lambda=0$, forcing $a_{jW}=0$.
Every root also vanishes at the four contacts generated by

$$
(u,Y,Z)\mapsto(-u,-Y,-Z),\qquad
(v,X,Z)\mapsto(-v,-X,-Z).
$$

Averaging those four equations keeps only $W,vX,uY,uvZ$.
The first term is absent, hence

$$
v_cX_c c_{jX}+u_cY_c b_{jY}+u_cv_c e_{jZ}=0.
$$

Collect these coefficients across the roots into vectors
$\mathbf c,\mathbf b,\mathbf e$. Dividing by $u_cv_c$ gives
$p\mathbf c=q\mathbf b+\mathbf e$. SG2 fixes their squared norms:

$$
\|\mathbf c\|^2=4\beta+2d,\qquad
\|\mathbf b\|^2=4A,\qquad
\|\mathbf e\|^2=4\beta.
$$

The triangle inequality proves (1). Equivalently, with
$s_1=p^2(4\beta+2d)$, $s_2=4q^2A$ and $s_3=4\beta$, it requires

$$
2(s_1s_2+s_1s_3+s_2s_3)-s_1^2-s_2^2-s_3^2\geq0.
\tag{2}
$$

This uses the four zeros of each root; no assumption about the root's
individual sign symmetry is needed.

## An exact source that violates the triangle inequality

Choose the native SD1 parameters $k=f=g=1$, $a=7/8$, $t=3/5$ in
[the C1 parametrization](../latex/certificates/c1_short.tex). They give

$$
(d,m,C,A,D,\alpha,\beta)=
\left(\frac{7301}{540},\frac{40}{9},2,\frac{2891}{135},
\frac{4699}{1080},\frac{81}{40},\frac{49}{1080}\right)
$$

and contact

$$
(u_c,v_c;\psi_c)=(1,1;1,-2/5,1/8,1).
$$

Every retained event coefficient is strictly positive. The native gaps
are $M=J=21/40$, $E=3/5$ and $Q=7/8$. The four state-kernel
equations and two angular stationary equations hold exactly, so the
stationary-contact theorem SG7 proves global validity.

Here $p=2/5$, $q=1/8$, and the three squared lengths in (1) are

$$
(s_1,s_2,s_3)=
\left(\frac{196}{45},\frac{2891}{2160},\frac{49}{270}\right).
$$

The first length exceeds $2$, whereas the others are less than $6/5$
and $1/2$. They cannot form a triangle. The left side of (2) is exactly

$$
-\frac{10994179}{1555200}<0.
$$

Thus the full parent is not an ordinary polynomial sum of squares at
any degree. The native formulas are continuous here, and all gaps and
the violation are strict, so the same conclusion holds on a nonempty
open set of strict C1 sources.

For C2, the second omitted event removes the third vector and forces
the [rigid coefficient ratio](C2_POLYNOMIAL_OBSTRUCTION.md). In C1 it
leaves a triangle condition. This argument establishes neither exclusion
on the entire C1 family nor sufficiency when the triangle condition holds.
Fractional-power certificates and multiplied parents remain separate
possibilities.

The [exact checker](checks/c1_polynomial_obstruction.py) reconstructs this
source from SD1 and verifies all six stationary equations, four signed
contacts, coefficient norms and the negative value in (2).

## A local square completion must preserve the other zeros

The same source rules out a natural attempt to turn a contact completion
into a global certificate. Shift the state by its contact value,
$\psi=(\xi+\xi_c Z,Z)$, and write
$F=\xi^TH\xi+2Z\xi^T\zeta+Z^2\gamma$. Put
$s=u-1$, $t=v-1$. In the scalar contact Gram, let $K\succ0$ be the
two-by-two block for $(s,t)$.

One completion extracts
$(hZ+K^{-1}B^T\xi)^TK(hZ+K^{-1}B^T\xi)$, choosing its second
coupling column as $B_2=J_t+b t$, where $J_t$ and $b$ are the
coefficients of $t$ and $t^2$ in $\zeta$. For the $W$ component,
direct differentiation of SG2 at the displayed contact gives

$$
(J_t)_W=-\frac{170}{9},\qquad b_W=27,
\qquad (B_2)_W(0,0)=-\frac{413}{9}.
$$

But $F(0,0;e_W)=H_{WW}(0,0)=0$. At this test $Z=0$ and
$\xi=e_W$, so the extracted term equals
$(B^Te_W)^TK^{-1}(B^Te_W)>0$. Its matrix remainder therefore has
a negative $WW$ entry. Continuity preserves the failure at nearby
positive angles.

This excludes this coupling allocation, not the fractional feature
space. A globally positive decomposition must make every extracted
square vanish at every zero of the parent. Positive definiteness of
the remainder near one contact does not establish that condition.
The [exact reconstruction](checks/c1_local_completion_zero.py) checks
the full three-channel identity and its negative remainder at this
deterministic zero.
